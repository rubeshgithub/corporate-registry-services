Alberta corporations incorporated under the *Business Corporations Act (Alberta)* (ABCA) must file an annual return every year with the Alberta Corporate Registry to maintain their good standing. Alberta has one of the shortest filing windows in Canada — annual returns are due within just one month of the corporation's anniversary date. This guide covers the rules, deadlines, fees, forms, and late-filing consequences — with a jump-off point to file when you're ready.

## What Is an Alberta Annual Return?

An Alberta annual return is a statutory filing that confirms your corporation's continued existence, updates director and address information on the registry, and keeps your corporation in active good standing under Alberta law.

## When Is the Alberta Annual Return Due?

Alberta annual returns are due **within 1 month of your corporation's anniversary date**. The anniversary date is the date the corporation was originally incorporated. This is a strict deadline — Alberta's 1-month window is the shortest of any Canadian jurisdiction.

For example: if your corporation was incorporated on July 22, your annual return is due by August 22 every year.

## Who Must File?

All corporations incorporated under the *Business Corporations Act (Alberta)* must file, including:
- Active operating corporations
- Holding companies
- Non-operating or dormant corporations
- Corporations with no employees and no revenue

## What Information Is Required?

An Alberta annual return requires:

- Full legal name of the corporation
- Alberta Corporate Access Number (CAN)
- Registered office and records address (must be in Alberta)
- Names and addresses of all current directors and officers
- Designated officer designation (if applicable)

## How to file an Alberta annual return through CORES

Alberta annual returns are filed through the **Alberta Corporate Registry** using the **CORES** portal (Corporate Registry Online filing), accessible through [alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/annual-return-information.aspx). Alberta does not offer direct public filing outside of CORES — you either use CORES yourself or file through an authorized service provider like CRS.

**Steps to file your Alberta annual return:**
1. Access CORES Online (Corporate Registry Online filing)
2. Search for your corporation using your Corporate Access Number
3. Select "File Annual Return" from your corporation's profile
4. Confirm or update director and address information
5. Pay the government filing fee
6. Receive confirmation of filing

## Annual returns for Alberta societies and non-profits

Alberta societies (registered under the *Societies Act*) and non-profit corporations must also file annual returns — but under different statutes and with slightly different information requirements. Alberta societies file annually with the Alberta Corporate Registry, disclosing directors, registered office, and (for non-profits) a summary of activities. Societies file their annual return **directly with Corporate Registry** on form REG3185 rather than through a registry agent, and on a different deadline — the last day of the month following the society's anniversary month. If you need a society or non-profit return filed, contact us and say so: it is a different process from a corporate annual return, and we will confirm the current fee and what has to accompany the filing before you pay anything.

## What happens if my Alberta annual return is late?

Alberta is strict about annual return compliance. Missing the 1-month deadline results in:

1. The corporation entering **non-compliance** status on the registry
2. A notice from the registry warning of potential dissolution
3. Administrative dissolution if the filing remains outstanding
4. A revival application required to restore the corporation (additional fees and processing time apply)

Note: Alberta corporate annual returns are separate from **GST returns** (filed with CRA) and **T2 corporate income tax returns** (also with CRA). Late CORES filing does not directly trigger CRA penalties — but a dissolved corporation cannot legally operate, which cascades into GST/T2 compliance problems very quickly. If you have late CORES *and* late GST filings, address the CORES filing first — you cannot file GST on a corporation the registry has dissolved.
