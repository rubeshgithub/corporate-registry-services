## What is an Alberta annual return — and what does it actually update?

The annual return is a statutory filing that confirms your corporation still exists and reports who owns it. Corporate Registry's own form describes the purpose plainly: the information "is required to confirm the corporation's ongoing existence and ownership." That ownership piece is the heart of it — the return lists the corporation's largest shareholders by percentage of issued voting shares, and the registry uses it to keep the public record current.

What the annual return does **not** do is change your directors, registered office or agent for service. Those are separate filings in Alberta (more on that [below](#what-the-annual-return-does-not-update)), and a common mistake is to assume that filing the return "updates everything." It doesn't — a corporation can be fully current on its annual returns and still have a director who resigned two years ago sitting on the registry.

The annual return is also not a tax filing. It goes to the Alberta Corporate Registry, not the CRA, and it has nothing to do with your [T2 corporate tax return](/articles/annual-return-vs-corporate-tax-return). You file both, every year, with two different offices.

## When is the Alberta annual return due?

Alberta works on your **anniversary month** — the month your corporation was incorporated (or amalgamated). Three dates matter:

1. **One month before the anniversary**, Corporate Registry sends a reminder notice to your corporation's registered office (and, where the registry has one, to the corporation's email address).
2. **The last day of your anniversary month** is your annual-return year-end. The government's own form uses this example: a corporation incorporated on March 20 has an annual-return year ending March 31 every following year. The shareholder information you report must be current as of that date.
3. **Within one month of that year-end** the return must be filed. Leave it longer and the corporation drops out of good standing and the dissolution clock starts.

So for a corporation incorporated on **July 22**: the reminder arrives in **June**, the year-end is **July 31**, and the return should be filed by the end of **August** at the very latest. Our advice is simpler — file in your anniversary month, the moment the reminder arrives, and the deadline never comes into play.

| Incorporated on | Reminder sent | Annual-return year-end | File no later than |
|---|---|---|---|
| January 9 | December | January 31 | End of February |
| April 30 | March | April 30 | End of May |
| July 22 | June | July 31 | End of August |
| November 3 | October | November 30 | End of December |

Two things catch people out. First, the reminder goes to the **registered office address and email on the registry**, so if you moved or changed email and never filed a change of address, the notice goes to the old address and you never see it. Second, the return is due even in years when **nothing has changed** — the form says so explicitly: "A corporation must file an annual return every year, even if there have not been any shareholder changes."

## Who has to file?

Every corporation incorporated under the *Business Corporations Act (Alberta)* files, regardless of size or activity:

- Active operating corporations
- Holding companies
- Professional corporations (doctors, dentists, lawyers, accountants — see [annual returns for a medical professional corporation](/articles/how-to-file-annual-returns-medical-professional-corporation))
- Dormant or non-operating corporations with no revenue and no employees
- Corporations registered in Alberta from other provinces or countries (with three exceptions — see [extra-provincial corporations](#do-extra-provincial-corporations-file-an-alberta-annual-return))

Alberta places **no residency requirement on directors** — none of them has to live in Canada — so the filing obligation is the same whether your directors are in Calgary or overseas. ## What do you need before you file? The five-minute checklist

Gather these before you start and the filing itself takes minutes:

1. **Legal name and Corporate Access Number (CAN).** The CAN is the registry's ID for your corporation. It's printed on your certificate of incorporation, on every registry notice, and on your reminder letter — or search your corporation above and we'll show it.
2. **A current email address for the corporation.** The form marks this field mandatory. It's where next year's reminder goes.
3. **Your largest shareholders.** Names, addresses, and each one's percentage of issued voting shares, current as of the last day of your anniversary month. Alberta.ca asks for your top five. If a shareholder is itself a corporation, have its Corporate Access Number handy.
4. **Confirmation that your registered office and records address are still right.** Both must be Alberta street addresses (a legal land description is acceptable; a PO box on its own is not). If either has changed, that's a separate notice — see the next section.
5. **Your agent for service, if you have one.** Confirm the name is still correct.
6. **Who is filing.** The return is submitted by an authorized representative of the corporation — a director, officer, or a person the corporation has authorized (your accountant or bookkeeper counts). They give their name and their relationship to the corporation.

That's the whole list. You don't need financial statements, minutes, or a tax return — those belong in your [corporate minute book](/guides/what-is-a-corporate-minute-book-canada), not in the annual return.

## What is the REG3062 annual return form?

**REG3062** — officially the *Annual Return for Alberta and Extra-provincial Corporation* — is the Government of Alberta's annual return form. If you file at a walk-in registry office, this is the paper you fill in. If you file online with CRS, we complete it from your registry record and your answers, so you never touch the form — but it helps to know what's on it:

- **Section 1–5:** name of corporation, Corporate Access Number, Business Number (optional), date of incorporation, and the annual return year.
- **Section 6:** a box to tick if this return *amends* one already filed for an earlier year.
- **Section 7:** the corporation's current address (or the agent for service's address for an out-of-province corporation), plus the mandatory email address.
- **Sections 8–9:** current agent for service and directors — with a checkbox for each confirming whether a notice of change is attached.
- **Section 10:** the shareholder section. Tick boxes for "no shareholder changes since last reported," "a shareholder's name has changed," "a shareholder's address has changed," or "there has been a change of one or more shareholders," followed by the table of current shareholders: name, address, Corporate Access Number if the shareholder is a corporation, any new name, and **percentage of issued voting shares**.
- **Section 11:** the authorized representative — name, relationship to the corporation, email, and signature with the date of submission.

The form carries a notice that it is public once completed — shareholder names and percentages on an Alberta annual return are part of the public registry record. The official PDF is at [cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG3062](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG3062), and the form itself tells you where it goes: "The completed form must be submitted to an authorized Corporate Registry Service Provider."

## What the annual return does *not* update

This is the part the paper form hints at with its "notice attached" checkboxes. In Alberta, director and address changes are **separate filings with their own deadline** — [alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/corporations-cooperatives-organizations-change-notices) says Alberta corporations "need to provide an authorized Corporate Registry service provider with address or director updates within 15 days of a change." The relevant notices are:

| Change | Form | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Registered office / records address | Notice of Change of Address (REG3016) | Within 15 days |
| Director appointed or resigned | Notice of Change of Directors (REG3017) | Within 15 days |
| A director's address | Notice of Change of Director Address (REG3220) | Within 15 days |
| Agent for service | Notice of Change of Agent for Service | As soon as it occurs |

If any of these happened during the year and was never reported, file the notice **with** the annual return — the registry will otherwise still show the old director or the old address, and your next reminder goes to the wrong place. CRS can file them alongside your return: [change of directors](/order/change-directors) and [change of registered address](/order/change-address). When you order the annual return online, we ask whether anything changed and quote the notice at the same time.

## Do extra-provincial corporations file an Alberta annual return?

It depends on where the corporation is from. Alberta.ca draws the line clearly:

- **Home province is British Columbia, Manitoba or Saskatchewan:** no Alberta annual return. Under the New West Partnership, these corporations are exempt. (They still file in their home province — see [BC](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-british-columbia) and [Saskatchewan](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-saskatchewan).)
- **Any other province, territory or country:** yes. An Ontario, federal, Québec or US corporation registered extra-provincially in Alberta files an Alberta annual return every year, on the same REG3062 form and the same anniversary schedule — the anniversary being the date of its *Alberta registration*, not its original incorporation. Its address section reports the Alberta agent for service, and address or director changes must be reported within 30 days (not 15).

## What happens if my Alberta annual return is late?

Alberta.ca states the consequence in one sentence: "If you do not file the annual return, your corporation may be dissolved." In practice it unfolds in stages:

1. **The corporation drops out of good standing.** Anyone who searches the registry — a bank, a lender, a buyer, a landlord — sees that the annual return is outstanding. That alone can stall a financing or a sale.
2. **Corporate Registry issues a dissolution warning.** The notice goes to the registered office; if yours is out of date, you won't see it. If you've received one, read [what to do about an intent to dissolve notice](/articles/intent-to-dissolve-notice-alberta) — filing the overdue return stops it.
3. **The corporation is dissolved.** Alberta doesn't publish a fixed number of missed returns before it strikes a corporation, and registry agents and law firms report it happening anywhere from about fifteen months to a couple of years after a missed filing. Don't test it. Once dissolved, the corporation no longer legally exists: it cannot sign contracts, its bank may freeze the account, and anyone signing for it is signing for an entity that no longer exists.
4. **Revival.** A dissolved Alberta corporation can be revived — the window runs for years, not months — but it means a revival application, the government's revival fee, every outstanding annual return, and the time the corporation spends unable to operate. [Corporate revival](/order/revival) is the route; [how to dissolve a corporation](/articles/how-to-dissolve-a-corporation-in-canada) explains what *voluntary* dissolution looks like if closing down is actually the plan.

**Missed more than one year?** Each outstanding year is filed as its own return, in order. When you order online we show every year the registry has outstanding and file them together.

## Is the annual return the same as my corporate tax return?

No, and confusing the two is the single most common reason Alberta corporations get struck. The **annual return** goes to the Alberta Corporate Registry, confirms your existence and ownership, and is due in your anniversary month. The **T2 corporate income tax return** goes to the CRA, reports your income, and is due six months after your fiscal year-end. Your accountant files the T2; the annual return is usually nobody's job unless someone makes it theirs. If both are late, file the annual return first — a dissolved corporation can't fix its tax filings. Full comparison: [annual return vs. corporate tax return](/articles/annual-return-vs-corporate-tax-return).

## What about Alberta societies and non-profits?

Different statute, different form, different route. Societies registered under the *Societies Act* file their annual return on form **REG3185 directly with Corporate Registry** — not through a registry agent — by the last day of the month following the society's anniversary month, with their financial statements attached. Non-profit companies under the *Companies Act* have their own form too. Neither uses REG3062 and neither can be filed through the corporate annual return process on this page; if you run a society, see our guide to the [Alberta society annual return](/articles/alberta-society-annual-return).
