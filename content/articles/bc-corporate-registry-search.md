British Columbia corporate searches run through **BC Registries and Online Services**, the provincial registry for every B.C. company, cooperative, society and registered firm. The government's own search tool — **Business Search**, inside the BC Registries application at bcregistry.gov.bc.ca — is the official route, but it comes with a catch most people only discover after clicking through: you need to create a BC Registry account and log in before you can run even a basic search. This guide covers where to search, the free no-login alternatives, what a B.C. record actually shows, and what the registry's certificates cost.

## Where Do You Search the B.C. Corporate Registry?

The official database is [Business Search](https://www.bcregistry.gov.bc.ca/), part of BC Registries and Online Services. Once you're logged in, you can look up a business by name, by incorporation or registration number, or by CRA business number, and narrow results by business type or status. The registry's own guidance recommends an account mainly for people who search regularly — which tells you something about how much setup is involved for a one-off lookup.

Two things to know before you start:

- **A login is required, even to search.** Business Search sits behind a BC Registry account, and signing in means a BC Services Card app, a Business BCeID or a similar verified credential. If you only need to check one company once, that is a lot of identity verification for a name lookup — our guide to [Business BCeID and BC Registry accounts](/articles/business-bceid-bc-registry-account) walks through exactly what the setup involves and when it's worth it.
- **Searching shows the basics; documents cost extra.** The result list gives you the business's status — **Active** or **Historical** — plus identifiers like the CRA business number where available. Official outputs such as a business summary, a Certificate of Good Standing or a Letter Under Seal are purchased per document, with each fee shown at checkout, and stay downloadable in your account for 14 days.

The legacy **BC OnLine** system still operates alongside Business Search for corporation, limited partnership and society records, but it's built for volume users: account setup alone is $100, so it only makes sense for law firms and search houses running constant queries.

## Can You Search a B.C. Company Without Creating an Account?

Yes — three routes get you a free British Columbia company search with no registry login:

1. **[OrgBook BC](https://www.orgbook.gov.bc.ca/)** — the province's public directory of registered organizations. The government's own records page points here to check whether a corporation is legally registered to do business in B.C. or to look up "doing business as" names. No account, no fee.
2. **Canada's Business Registries search** — the federal aggregator includes B.C. alongside the other participating registries, so a name typed there returns B.C. corporations with their status and jurisdiction.
3. **[CRS's Canada corporations search](/canada-corporations-search)** — searches B.C. and the other Canadian registries from one box, no login, and shows status and registry details on the spot. If the free result isn't enough, you can order the official record from the same page.

## What Does a B.C. Registry Search Show You?

A British Columbia corporate record can include the company's status (active or not), its recognition or incorporation date, the date of its last annual report, previous names, the registered and records office addresses, the names of directors and officers, and copies of filed annual reports.

Three details worth reading closely:

- **Status: Active vs Historical.** "Historical" means the entity is no longer active on the register — typically dissolved, struck or amalgamated. In B.C., companies that stop filing their annual reports can end up dissolved by the registrar, so a Historical status on a company you're about to pay is a genuine red flag.
- **Date of last annual report.** A company that's active but years behind on annual reports is drifting toward dissolution. (If that company is yours, [filing the overdue B.C. annual report](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-british-columbia) is the fix — the government fee is $43.39 and it's due within two months of your anniversary each year.)
- **What's *not* in the public record.** Shareholder, member and share certificate details aren't available through the registry search — B.C. keeps those with the company's own records office, so you have to contact the company directly for ownership information.

## Certificate of Status, Good Standing or Letter Under Seal — Which One Do You Need?

B.C. is unusual in offering three different proof documents, and banks and counterparties regularly ask for the wrong one by name:

| Document | What it proves | Who can get it |
|---|---|---|
| **Certificate of Status** | The entity is active in B.C. | Only companies, societies or co-ops formed in B.C. |
| **Certificate of Good Standing** | Annual reports and required filings are up to date | Incorporated B.C. entities (order form REG760) |
| **Letter Under Seal** | Registration type and current status, whatever the status is | Any registered entity — including extraprovincial companies, partnerships and sole proprietorships |

The practical rule: extraprovincial companies registered in B.C. can't get a B.C. Certificate of Status — they request a Letter Under Seal instead, and companies registered in B.C. under the New West Partnership from Alberta, Saskatchewan or Manitoba get their good-standing proof from their home registry. If you're not sure which document your bank or lawyer actually needs, our guide to the [certificate of good standing](/guides/what-is-a-certificate-of-good-standing-canada) explains what each party is usually looking for.

## How Do You Verify a B.C. Company Before You Do Business With It?

Run the name through a free search first — OrgBook BC or [CRS's registry search](/canada-corporations-search) — and check three things: the status says Active, the legal name matches the name on your contract or invoice exactly, and the company's incorporation date is consistent with how long it claims to have operated. A name that returns nothing, or returns only a Historical record, means the entity either isn't registered in B.C. or no longer exists — stop and ask questions before money moves.

For anything with real stakes — a supplier contract, a lease, litigation, buying the company — get the official record rather than relying on a free summary. A corporate profile report shows the directors and registered offices on file, and a certificate of good standing proves the company is current on its filings. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is searching the BC corporate registry free?

Searching is free, but B.C.'s own Business Search requires a BC Registry account login before you can run a lookup. For a no-login search, use OrgBook BC, Canada's Business Registries, or CRS's Canada corporations search — all free. Official documents cost extra, per the registry's legislated fee schedule.

### What is BC Registries and Online Services?

It's British Columbia's corporate registry, under the Ministry of Citizens' Services. It incorporates B.C. companies, registers firms, cooperatives and societies, maintains the public record of each entity's status, directors and offices, and issues official proof documents like Certificates of Good Standing and Letters Under Seal.

### How do I find out if a BC company is still active?

Search the name in OrgBook BC, Business Search, or CRS's Canada corporations search and read the status: Active means the company is on the register, Historical means it has been dissolved, struck or amalgamated. ### Can I see who owns a BC company?
