A Canadian business number (BN) is the 9-digit identifier the Canada Revenue Agency assigns to a business, and you can look one up free: enter the BN — or the company's name — in the search above, and the corporate registry record that carries it comes back in seconds. That works in both directions, which matters, because the CRA's own public tool (the GST/HST Registry) only works one way: it *confirms* a BN you already have, and cannot find a BN from a name. This guide covers what the number actually is, how to find a company's BN, how to run a reverse lookup on a number someone gave you, and why some business numbers legitimately can't be found in any public registry.

## What is a business number in Canada?

The business number is a 9-digit identifier issued by the CRA when a business first registers for any federal program — it's the root of every tax account the business holds. The [CRA's program account system](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/need-program-accounts.html) extends that root with a 2-letter program code and a 4-digit reference number:

- **RT** — GST/HST (so a "GST number" like 123456789RT0001 is just the BN plus its GST account)
- **RP** — payroll deductions
- **RC** — corporate income tax
- **RM** — import/export
- **RR** — registered charity
- **RZ** — information returns

One business, one BN — however many program accounts hang off it. If someone hands you "123456789RT0001", the business number is the first 9 digits; the suffix only tells you which tax account you're looking at.

A BN is **not** the same as a corporation number. A federal corporation has a 7-digit Corporations Canada number; an Alberta corporation has a 10-digit Corporate Access Number; B.C. uses numbers like BC1234567. Those identify the legal entity in its *corporate registry*; the BN identifies it to the *CRA*. The registry record is the public bridge between the two — which is exactly why a registry search is how you look a BN up.

## How do you find a company's business number?

Search the company by name in a corporate registry — the registry record lists its business number. The free [Canada-wide corporation search](/canada-corporations-search) on this site queries the national registry index (Canada's Business Registries, run by the federal government) plus provincial sources, and the BN appears right on the result when the registry shares it. Three ways in:

1. **By company name.** Type the legal name (or the distinctive part of it) and pick the right company from the results. The record shows the business number along with status, jurisdiction and registry ID.
2. **By corporation number.** If you have the registry number from articles or a certificate, that finds the exact company — no name ambiguity.
3. **From the company's own paperwork.** Before searching at all: the BN is printed on GST invoices (it must appear on any invoice charging GST/HST), T4 slips, CRA correspondence, and import documents.

## Can you do a reverse business number lookup?

Yes — paste the 9 digits into the search at the top of this page and it returns the corporation registered under that BN. Include or drop the program suffix; "123456789", "123456789RT0001" and "123 456 789" all resolve the same way.

This is the lookup the government doesn't really offer. The CRA's [GST/HST Registry](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/confirming-a-gst-hst-account-number.html) requires you to enter the BN *and* the business name *and* a transaction date — it confirms a match, it doesn't find anything. It exists to verify that a supplier charging you GST is actually registered, not to tell you who a number belongs to. The national registry index, by contrast, does index business numbers against corporate records — so a registry search answers "whose number is this?" directly.

Two honest caveats on reverse lookups: the registries index the 9-digit root, not program suffixes, so search the plain 9 digits; and a BN that belongs to a sole proprietorship or partnership won't resolve, for the reason below.

## Why can't you find a business number?

Four common reasons, none of which mean the number is fake:

- **It belongs to a sole proprietorship or partnership.** The CRA issues BNs to unincorporated businesses too, and those never appear in a *corporate* registry — there's no corporation to register. The GST/HST Registry confirm-tool is the only public check for those.
- **The company is in a registry that doesn't share BNs.** PEI, Newfoundland and Labrador, New Brunswick and the territories don't feed searchable records into the national index — a company from those registries needs a manual registry pull, which is exactly the kind of lookup [we do by hand](/contact).
- **You searched the full program account.** "123456789RT0001" with the suffix attached can miss where the bare 9 digits hit. Strip it down to 9.
- **It's a charity's registration number.** A registered charity's number has an RR suffix; the 9-digit root still searches like any BN, but the charity's own details live in the CRA's charities listing rather than a corporate registry.

If a number comes back empty everywhere and the "company" quoting it is asking for money, treat that as a due-diligence red flag and verify through the [company-verification steps](/guides/how-to-verify-a-canadian-company) before paying.

## Business number vs. GST number vs. corporation number

Three numbers, constantly confused, quick reference:

| Number | Looks like | Who issues it | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business number (BN) | 123456789 | CRA | The business's root tax identity |
| GST/HST number | 123456789RT0001 | CRA | The BN's GST program account — what must appear on invoices |
| Corporation number | 1234567 (federal), 2012345678 (Alberta), BC1234567 (B.C.) | The corporate registry | The legal entity's identity in its home registry |

Practical rules: an invoice needs the RT number; a bank or lawyer usually wants the corporation number *and* a current registry record; the CRA wants the BN root on almost everything. ### Can I find out who a business number belongs to?

Usually, yes — if it belongs to a corporation. Enter the 9 digits in the search above and the registry record carrying that BN comes back with the company's name, status and jurisdiction. A BN belonging to a sole proprietorship or partnership won't appear in corporate registries; for those, the CRA's GST/HST Registry can only confirm a name-and-number pair you already have.

### Is a GST number the same as a business number?

The GST number is the business number plus its GST/HST program account suffix: BN 123456789 becomes GST number 123456789RT0001. Same 9-digit root, so a GST number minus its last six characters is the BN.

### Is a business number the same as a corporation number?

No. The BN is issued by the CRA for tax accounts; the corporation number is issued by the corporate registry where the company was incorporated (Corporations Canada, Alberta Corporate Registry, and so on). A corporation has both, and its registry record is the public document that links them.
