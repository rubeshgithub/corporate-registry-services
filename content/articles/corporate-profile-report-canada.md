## What is a corporate profile report?

A corporate profile report (Ontario's registry calls it a "Profile Report"; other provinces use names like "company summary" or "corporation search") is a point-in-time snapshot of everything on a corporation's public record. It's produced by the corporate registry itself — not compiled from third-party databases — which is why banks, courts, and government bodies accept it as verification.

Every incorporated company in Canada has one, whether it's registered federally under the CBCA or provincially in Alberta, BC, Ontario, or elsewhere. The report reflects the record as of the date you pull it, so most institutions want one issued within the last 30–90 days.

## What does a corporate profile report show?

The exact layout varies by registry, but a profile report typically includes:

- Current legal name and any former names
- Corporation number (and Alberta's Corporate Access Number, where applicable)
- Status — active, dissolved, struck, or in default
- Date and jurisdiction of incorporation, amalgamation, or continuance
- Registered office and mailing address
- Names and addresses of current directors (and officers, in some jurisdictions)
- Filing history, including the most recent annual return on record
- Extra-provincial registrations, where applicable

What it does *not* show: shareholders, share ledgers, or financial statements. Ownership lives in the corporation's [minute book](/guides/what-is-a-corporate-minute-book-canada), not on the public registry — a common surprise for lenders and buyers doing due diligence.

## When do you need one?

The report comes up whenever someone needs verified facts about a corporation:

- **Banking and KYC** — opening a business account or securing financing; the bank verifies directors and status before signing anything.
- **Due diligence** — buying a business, extending trade credit, or [verifying a company you're about to deal with](/guides/how-to-verify-a-canadian-company).
- **Accounting setup** — QuickBooks Online and similar platforms ask for registry confirmation of the legal name and incorporation details.
- **Legal matters** — litigation, commercial leases, and contracts where counsel needs the registered office and director list on record.
- **Your own compliance check** — confirming your annual returns are on file and your corporation hasn't slipped into "not in good standing." If the report shows missed filings, [file your annual return](/order/annual-return) before the registry moves toward dissolution.

## How much does a corporate profile report cost in Canada?

Government fees are modest — the cost is mostly in figuring out which registry to use and navigating its process. Current fees at the source:

| Jurisdiction | Where it comes from | Government fee |
|---|---|---|
| Ontario | Ontario Business Registry (form 5310E by mail/email, or through an authorized service provider) | $8 per Profile Report |
| Federal (CBCA) | Corporations Canada online database | Free to search; uncertified document copies free online |
| British Columbia | BC Registries — Service BC, mail, or a BC OnLine account ($100 account setup) | Legislated fees vary by product |
| Alberta | Authorized registry agents only — there is no direct-to-government search portal | Varies by agent |

Ontario's $8 fee is set out in the province's official search-products form; the federal figures are published by Corporations Canada. Alberta works differently: all corporate registry searches flow through authorized service providers, so the price you pay depends on the agent you use — typically in the $25–$75 range including service fees.

## Corporate profile report vs. certificate of good standing — which one do you need?

A profile report gives *information* — the full public record, usually about another company. A certificate of good standing gives *proof* — a one-page certification that a corporation is active and compliant, usually requested about your own company for a bank, a grant, or an extra-provincial registration. They're often confused because both come from the registry.

If a bank or lawyer asked you for "a corporate search," they almost always mean the profile report. If they asked you to "prove your company is in good standing," you need the [certificate of good standing](/good-standing) instead. The full breakdown is in our [profile report vs. certificate of good standing guide](/guides/corporate-profile-report-vs-certificate-of-good-standing).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a corporate profile report the same as a corporate search?

In everyday use, yes — "corporate search," "corporation search," and "profile report" usually refer to the same product: the registry's report on a corporation's public record. Ontario's registry formally calls it a Profile Report; BC calls its equivalent a company summary. If someone asks you for a corporate search on a specific company, a profile report is almost always what they want.

### Can I get a corporate profile report for a company in any province?

Yes. Every provincial and territorial registry, plus Corporations Canada federally, produces a profile report or equivalent for its corporations. CRS retrieves reports from any Canadian jurisdiction for $49 all-in, so you don't need to learn each registry's process — useful when you're checking companies in more than one province.

### How current is the information in a profile report?

The report is a snapshot of the registry record on the date it's generated — director changes or filings made after that date won't appear. That's why banks and lawyers typically ask for a report dated within the last 30–90 days, and why it's worth pulling a fresh one rather than reusing last year's.

### Does a corporate profile report show who owns the corporation?

No. Shareholders and share transactions are recorded in the corporation's minute book and share ledger, not on the public registry, so no profile report in Canada lists ownership. The report shows directors, which is often the closest publicly available signal — for true ownership you need the corporation's own records.

### How fast can I get a corporate profile report?
