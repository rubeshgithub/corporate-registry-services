QuickBooks Online asks Canadian corporations using Payments or Payroll direct deposit for a current government document showing the business name, address and directors — usually called a corporate profile report. Every registry calls it something different. This page lists the document Intuit names for each province and federally, and CRS can get any of them for you for {{price:profile-report}} + GST.

## What QuickBooks Online asks for

Because QuickBooks Payments and Payroll direct deposit fall under FINTRAC, Canada's anti-money-laundering regulator, Intuit asks a private corporation for:

- the name and address of **every director**,
- the name and address of **everyone owning 25% or more**, and
- **one current government-issued document** showing (a) the business name and address, (b) the business name and directors, or (c) all three. A company/corporate profile report, articles or certificate of incorporation, or an unexpired business licence all qualify.

New Payments or Payroll customers have 30 days from sign-up to provide it; Intuit says reviews for new customers typically take 2–3 business days.

## Which registry: where you're incorporated, not where you operate

Use the report from the jurisdiction the corporation was **incorporated** in. A federal corporation that operates in Alberta needs Corporations Canada's Corporate Profile, not an Alberta search. A company incorporated in Ontario but working in Manitoba needs the Ontario Profile Report.

## Province by province

### Federal

Corporations Canada's Corporate Profile shows the corporation number, name, status, registered office and directors. Intuit notes that uncertified copies are acceptable for QuickBooks.

### Manitoba

The Manitoba Companies Office report shows the corporation's registration details, registered office and directors.

### Saskatchewan

The Saskatchewan Corporate Registry Profile Report shows the corporation's status, registered office and directors.

### Québec

The Registraire des entreprises record ("état de renseignements") shows the enterprise's name, addresses, and its directors and principal shareholders as declared to the registry.

### Nova Scotia

The Registry of Joint Stock Companies Profile Report shows the company's status, registered office and directors. See our guide to the [Registry of Joint Stock Companies](/articles/registry-of-joint-stock-companies-nova-scotia).

### New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador

Each registry produces its own report from its corporate database (CARD in New Brunswick, the PEI Corporate Registry, and Companies and Deeds Online in Newfoundland and Labrador). Some of these registries can't be searched instantly online — CRS retrieves the record and emails it to you.

### Yukon, Northwest Territories and Nunavut

The territorial registries produce an entity profile showing the corporation's status, registered office and directors. CRS retrieves it and emails you the PDF.

## Before you upload, check the report matches QuickBooks

Whatever the province, uploads usually fail because the report and the QuickBooks form disagree:

1. **Legal name** — use it exactly as the registry shows it.
2. **Directors** — if the board changed and the registry wasn't told, file the change first, then order a fresh report.
3. **Address** — an unreported move means the report shows the old address.
4. **Status** — a corporation that's behind on annual returns can be struck or dissolved. Fix that first.

## Uploading it in QuickBooks Online

Use the link in Intuit's email, the banner inside QuickBooks Online or the notice on the **Payroll Overview** page. Upload the PDF and enter the director and owner details. Intuit says the reminder can keep appearing for 2–3 business days after a successful upload; if it asks for more, its follow-up email links to the resolution centre with the details.
