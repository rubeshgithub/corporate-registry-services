## What is a director resolution?

A director resolution is a formal decision of the board of directors of a corporation — the mechanism by which the board authorizes the corporation to take some action or approves some fact. Under the *Canada Business Corporations Act* (s. 117) and every provincial equivalent — Ontario's *OBCA*, Alberta's *ABCA*, BC's *Business Corporations Act*, Quebec's *QBCA* — the board of directors is the corporation's governing body, and its decisions are recorded through resolutions.

Common director resolutions include:

- Approving the issuance or transfer of shares
- Appointing or removing officers (President, Treasurer, Secretary)
- Approving the annual financial statements
- Declaring dividends
- Authorizing bank accounts and signing officers
- Approving major contracts, loans, or leases
- Approving changes to the registered office address
- Approving the corporation's by-laws (and any amendments)

Every director resolution goes in the **resolutions section** of the minute book, in chronological order, signed and dated. If the resolution changes directors or officers, the register of directors or register of officers must also be updated at the same time.

## Types of director resolutions

Canadian corporate practice recognizes three broad categories:

**1. Organizational resolutions** — the first resolutions passed after incorporation. These are typically bundled into a single "organizational package" covering:
- Adopting the corporate seal (where used)
- Adopting the by-laws
- Appointing the first officers
- Authorizing the corporate bank account and signing officers
- Approving the issuance of the first shares
- Fixing the fiscal year

**2. Annual resolutions** — the yearly package (see below) every corporation must pass.

**3. Ad-hoc resolutions** — everything else. A resolution to declare a mid-year dividend, approve a specific contract, appoint a new officer, or authorize a specific transaction is an ad-hoc resolution passed as the need arises.

## Resolutions in writing vs. minutes of a meeting

There are two ways to record a director decision under Canadian corporate law:

**Resolution in writing (in lieu of meeting).** The decision is drafted, circulated, and signed by every director entitled to vote. No meeting is held. This is by far the most common method for small Canadian corporations — it is faster, cheaper, and just as legally valid as a meeting. The catch: the resolution must be signed by *every* director. One missing signature invalidates the resolution.

**Minutes of a meeting.** The board holds an actual meeting — in person, by teleconference, or by video call — and decisions are recorded in minutes signed by the meeting chair and secretary. The resolution passes when the majority of directors present vote in favour (or whatever threshold the by-laws specify).

For a typical small Canadian corporation with two or three directors who are also the shareholders, resolutions in writing are the standard method. They avoid the friction of scheduling and recording a formal meeting for what is often a routine decision.

## Who signs a director resolution?

For a **resolution in writing**, every director entitled to vote must sign. A director who is absent, unavailable, or disagrees with the resolution cannot sign — and their non-signature means the resolution has not been passed. In practice this means that a resolution in writing is used only when the board is genuinely unanimous.

For a resolution passed at a **meeting**, only the meeting chair and secretary sign the minutes documenting the resolution. The individual directors do not sign — their vote at the meeting is what counts.

## Consent to act as director

Every director appointed to a Canadian corporation must sign a **Consent to Act as Director** — a written acknowledgement that they have agreed to serve as a director. This document is required alongside the resolution appointing them and is stored in the minute book together with the resolution and the register of directors update.

Skipping the consent is a common gap in DIY minute books. It typically surfaces during due diligence when the buyer's lawyer asks for the consent form and finds nothing.

## Annual director resolutions: the yearly package

Every Canadian corporation — even a one-person corporation where the sole director is also the sole shareholder — must pass **annual director resolutions** each year. The typical package covers:

- Approving the annual financial statements
- Appointing (or reappointing) officers for the coming year
- Confirming banking and signing officer arrangements
- Approving any dividends declared during the year

**Important:** annual director resolutions are not the same as your annual return. The annual return is a government filing that keeps your corporation active on the public registry (we file those — see our [annual return service](/annual-return)).
