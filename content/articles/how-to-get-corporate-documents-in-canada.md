The short answer, before we get into the specifics: **every corporate document ever filed with the registry is still there. The registry never deletes anything.** You (or a retrieval service acting on your behalf) can order a fresh certified copy of the articles of incorporation, the certificate of incorporation, and every historical filing at any time — usually within 24 hours for digital-era records.

## The three documents people usually want

There are really only three things people mean when they say "I need my corporate documents":

**1. Articles of Incorporation** — the substantive founding document. Share classes, restrictions on share transfers, number of directors, name, first registered office. Signed on the day the corporation was created and never re-issued unless the articles were formally amended. This is what a bank, lender, or lawyer means when they say "send us your articles."

**2. Certificate of Incorporation** — the one-page government-stamped document confirming the corporation legally exists as of a specific date. Think of it as the corporation's birth certificate. Federal corporations get one from Corporations Canada; provincial corporations get an equivalent from their provincial registry.

**3. Historical corporate filings** — every other document filed with the registry over the life of the corporation: annual returns, notices of directors, changes to registered office, amendments, restated articles, revivals, notices of dissolution. Anyone doing due diligence on the corporation — a buyer, a lender, an estate lawyer, a new CFO — will ask for this complete set.

There is also a **fourth category** that trips up most searchers, and it's important to be clear about it up front.

## What is *not* on file with the registry

Directors' resolutions, shareholders' resolutions, share certificates, share subscription forms, meeting minutes, by-laws, and the shareholder and securities registers are **never filed** with any corporate registry in Canada. They live in the corporation's internal **minute book**, kept at the registered office. If the minute book was lost or never assembled, no registry can send them to you — they have to be reconstructed by counsel or a compliance service.

## How to order corporate documents in Canada — three paths

You have three practical paths to get a copy of your articles of incorporation and full corporate file.

### Path 1 — Direct from the government registry

Every Canadian jurisdiction has a registry portal that will sell you a certified copy of the articles and, separately, a certified copy of the certificate. Government fees run roughly $20-$40 per document, per jurisdiction.

Federal corporations: [Corporations Canada](https://www.ic.gc.ca/eic/site/cd-dgc.nsf/eng/home) — you'll need to know the corporation number and pay per-document fees.

Provincial:
- Alberta: through a registry agent (Alberta doesn't operate a direct-to-public portal for corporate documents).
- British Columbia: BC Registry Services, direct.
- Ontario: Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery via ONBIS / Ontario Business Registry.
- Saskatchewan, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland, PEI, Nunavut, Yukon, NWT — each operates its own portal or delegates to an authorized agent.
- Quebec: REQ (Registraire des entreprises du Québec).

**When this is the right path:** you know the exact document you need, you're comfortable navigating multiple portals if the corporation has been extra-provincially registered elsewhere, and you can wait through any queue.

### Path 2 — Through a registry agent

Alberta, Manitoba, and a few other provinces use authorized private agents (e.g., Alberta's registry agent network) as the front counter to their registry. If you walk into an agent's office with the corporation name and number, they can produce most documents on the spot for a service fee on top of the government charge.

**When this is the right path:** you happen to be near one, you like paper, or you need one specific document urgently and the corporation is Alberta-based.

## Jurisdiction snapshot

Every jurisdiction offers substantially the same underlying documents, but the labels and delivery mechanics differ:

- **Federal (Corporations Canada)** — articles + certificate + all historical filings. Digital delivery for anything filed after ~2001.
- **Alberta** — articles + certificate + full history via the CORES registry system through an authorized agent.
- **British Columbia** — certificate + charter documents + full filing history via BC Registry Services (Corporate Online).
- **Ontario** — articles + certificate + all filings via the Ontario Business Registry (formerly ONPRO).
- **Saskatchewan, Manitoba** — direct-to-registry or through an agent depending on document type.
- **Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, PEI** — smaller registries; digital retrieval for recent filings, paper for older records.
- **Quebec (REQ)** — état de renseignements and constating documents; some records only in French.
- **Northwest Territories, Yukon, Nunavut** — smaller volumes; typically retrieved through the territorial registrar's office directly.

If your corporation is registered extra-provincially — for example, a BC corporation registered to do business in Alberta — you may need documents from both registries. A retrieval service handles that in one order.
