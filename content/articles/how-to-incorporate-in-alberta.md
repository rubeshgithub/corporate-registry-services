## Can you incorporate directly with the Alberta government?

**No — and this is the part that surprises people who have incorporated in other provinces.** Alberta.ca is explicit: incorporation applications go to a registry agent or authorized service provider. There is no public portal where you log in and submit Articles yourself, the way BC Registry Services or the Ontario Business Registry work. Alberta's Corporate Registry is accessed through CORES, the province's electronic filing system, which only authorized agents can use — [here's how the CORES system works](/articles/what-is-cores-alberta) if you want the background.

Two practical consequences. First, comparing providers is genuinely worth ten minutes, because the service fee is the only variable — the $291.75 government fee is identical everywhere. Second, there is no per-corporation login or company key to lose: your registry agent files for you, so Alberta never blocks you the way a lost company key blocks Ontario filings.

## How do you incorporate in Alberta step by step?

Alberta.ca's own checklist runs seven steps; here is the practical version:

1. **Choose your name — or take a number.** An Alberta corporate name needs three elements: a distinctive part ("Bowline"), a descriptive part ("Consulting"), and a legal element (Ltd., Inc., Corp., Limited, Incorporated, or ULC for an unlimited liability corporation). If branding isn't settled, incorporate numbered and add a name later by Articles of Amendment.
2. **Get an Alberta NUANS report (named corporations only).** The [NUANS name search](/guides/nuans-name-search-canada-guide) compares your proposed name against existing corporate names and trademarks and **reserves it for 90 days**. The report must be less than 91 days old when you file.
3. **Prepare your Articles of Incorporation (form REG3047).** Share classes and the rights attached to them, the number (or range) of directors, and any restrictions on the business. This is the document worth getting right the first time — amending Articles later costs another government fee and a shareholder resolution.
4. **Record your registered office (Notice of Address, REG3016).** A physical street address in Alberta where documents can be served — not a P.O. box.
5. **Elect your directors (Notice of Directors, REG3017).** At least one director, 18 or older. There is no Canadian-residency requirement (more below).
6. **Appoint an agent for service (Notice of Agent for Service, REG3037).** An individual located in Alberta who can receive legal documents on the corporation's behalf, in person or by mail.
7. **Submit the package to a registry agent and pay.** The agent files through CORES; Service Alberta issues the Certificate of Incorporation, typically same-day or next-day once the filing is clean.

End to end, Alberta is one of the fastest jurisdictions in Canada. CRS-filed incorporations run the name search on day 1, file the Articles on day 1–2, and deliver the certificate plus complete minute book in **2–4 business days**.

## Do directors need to live in Canada?

**No.** Alberta removed its 25% Canadian-resident-director requirement effective March 29, 2021, when amendments to the *Business Corporations Act* (ABCA) came into force. Every director must be at least 18, but they can live anywhere in the world — which is why Alberta, alongside BC, is a go-to jurisdiction for international founders. Federal (CBCA) incorporation still requires 25% of directors to be resident Canadians; if you're weighing that trade-off, our [federal vs. provincial incorporation guide](/articles/federal-vs-provincial-incorporation-canada) covers when the CBCA's Canada-wide name protection justifies its residency rule.

The counterweight: while your directors can be anywhere, your corporation's footprint cannot. You need a **registered office at a physical Alberta address** and an **agent for service who is an individual located in Alberta**. Non-resident founders usually satisfy both through a service provider — mention it when you order and CRS will walk you through the options.

## Do you have to visit a registry office in Calgary, Edmonton or your local town?

**No.** Any authorized registry agent can file an Alberta incorporation, wherever you — or they — happen to be. The Alberta Corporate Registry is a single provincial system: a filing submitted through an online registry agent lands in exactly the same CORES queue as one walked into a counter in Calgary, Edmonton, Red Deer or Strathmore. Founders outside Alberta's cities (or outside Alberta entirely) don't need to find a local office, book an appointment, or mail anything: CRS handles the entire filing online, and the Certificate of Incorporation arrives as a PDF the moment Service Alberta issues it.

## What happens after you incorporate?

The certificate is the start, not the finish. To be organized properly, a new Alberta corporation should have:

- **Organizational resolutions** — the first directors' and shareholders' resolutions appointing officers, issuing shares, and adopting by-laws. Our guide to [director resolutions](/articles/director-resolutions-in-canada) explains the format.
- **Issued share certificates** — founders are shareholders only once shares are actually issued and certificated.
- **A minute book** — the corporation's legal record book. Banks, buyers, and the CRA all eventually ask for it; a [digital minute book](/minute-books/digital-minute-book-canada) keeps it complete and signable from anywhere.
- **A Business Number** from the CRA, plus GST registration once revenue crosses the threshold.

And one recurring obligation with an unusually tight window: an Alberta corporation must file an annual return **within one month of its incorporation anniversary**, every year, through a registry agent. Miss it two years running and the corporation can be struck from the registry — [here's how to file the Alberta annual return](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-alberta), and what to do if you've already received a [notice of intent to dissolve](/articles/intent-to-dissolve-notice-alberta).
