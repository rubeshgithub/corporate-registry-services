Registering a business in Alberta means one of two quite different things, and picking the wrong one costs money to undo. You are either **registering a trade name** — a sole proprietorship or partnership operating under a business name — or you are **incorporating**, creating a separate legal entity that owns the business.

There is also a fact about Alberta that catches almost everyone out, and it explains why every quote you find online is a different number.

## Sole proprietorship or corporation — which do you actually need?

The honest answer is that most people know already; they just want permission.

**A trade name (sole proprietorship)** registers a name you trade under. It does not create a separate legal entity. You and the business are the same person in law: you keep the profits, you report the income on your personal return, and you carry the liability personally. It is fast and it is cheap.

**Incorporation** creates a corporation — a legal person separate from you. It can own property, sign contracts and be sued in its own name. Your liability is generally limited to what you put in. It can access the small business deduction, retain earnings at corporate rates, and continue to exist when you sell or die.

Choose the trade name when you are testing something, the risk is low and the income is modest. Choose incorporation when you have real liability exposure, meaningful income you don't need to draw personally, employees, investors, or clients who require it. Contractors and consultants often find the client decides for them.

You can start as a sole proprietor and incorporate later — plenty do. It is not free, and you will move assets across, but it is not a trap.

## Why can't you register directly with the Alberta government?

This is the Alberta-specific fact, and it surprises people who have incorporated elsewhere.

**You cannot file corporate registrations with Service Alberta yourself.** Alberta's own guidance is explicit: *"You need to take your forms to a registry agent or authorized Alberta service provider."* There is no consumer portal where you log in and submit articles of incorporation, the way BC and Ontario provide.

Every Alberta business registration — trade names and incorporations alike — goes through an **authorised registry agent** operating on the CORES system. That is not a middleman you can route around; it is how the province designed the service.

Two consequences follow, and both matter:

**Quotes vary wildly, and legitimately.** Alberta.ca states that *"the service provider that processes your application will charge a government fee and a service fee."* Registry agent service fees are **not regulated** — each agent sets its own. That is why one website quotes $250 and another $900 for the same filing. They are not describing different work; they are describing different margins.

**There is no password to lose.** Because filings go through an agent rather than a customer login, Alberta has no per-corporation access code of the kind that blocks people in Ontario, BC, Saskatchewan and Manitoba. If you have ever been stuck unable to file because you lost a company key, Alberta simply doesn't work that way.

## How much does it cost to register a business in Alberta?

Two components, always: the **government fee**, which is fixed, and the **registry agent's service fee**, which is not.

**Trade name (sole proprietorship).** The government fee is **$10**. The agent's fee sits on top, and published examples bring the total to roughly $60–$90. Registration must be renewed every 5 years.

**Incorporation.** Alberta does not publish a consumer-facing incorporation fee on alberta.ca — it points you to the registry agent product catalogue, because the agent's fee is part of the total. A **NUANS report** is also required for a named corporation, though not for a numbered one.

## How do you register a trade name in Alberta?

1. **Choose the name.** No NUANS required, but it must not be misleading and cannot imply you are a corporation — no "Ltd.", "Inc." or "Corp."
2. **Complete a Declaration of Trade Name.**
3. **Take it to a registry agent**, who files it and returns proof of registration.
4. **Diarise the renewal** — trade names lapse after 5 years.

A trade name registration is not a business licence. Municipal licensing is separate, and so is GST registration once you pass the CRA's small-supplier threshold.

## How do you incorporate in Alberta?

Alberta's process runs to seven steps:

1. **Choose a name** — or take a numbered company and skip the next step
2. **Get an Alberta NUANS report** and check nothing identical or confusingly similar exists
3. **Complete the forms** — Articles of Incorporation and the accompanying notices
4. **Record the registered office address** in Alberta
5. **Elect at least one director** — Alberta has *no* Canadian residency requirement, unlike the federal CBCA, which requires 25%
6. **Appoint an agent for service** in Alberta
7. **Submit through a registry agent or authorised service provider**

The residency point at step 5 is why Alberta is a common choice for founders who are not Canadian residents, and it is worth knowing before you default to federal.

## What happens after you register?

The part nobody mentions at the point of sale: an Alberta corporation owes an **annual return every year**, due **within one month of the incorporation anniversary** — one of the tightest windows in Canada. The government fee is $47.

Miss it repeatedly and the registrar strikes the corporation from the register. It ceases to exist, bank accounts become a problem, and getting it back means a revival plus every missed return.

There is no account for the registry to email, because Alberta filings run through agents. That is precisely why it gets forgotten. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I register a business in Alberta myself, online?

Not with the government directly. Alberta requires corporate registrations to be filed through an authorised registry agent or service provider — its own guidance says to take your forms to one. There is no consumer self-serve portal for incorporation the way BC and Ontario have. Any online Alberta registration you complete is being submitted by an agent on your behalf.

### How much does it cost to register a business in Alberta?

A trade name carries a $10 government fee plus the registry agent's service fee, commonly bringing it to roughly $60–$90. Incorporation costs the government fee plus the agent's fee, plus a NUANS report for a named company. Because agent service fees are unregulated, published totals vary widely between providers. CRS charges {{price:incorporation-numbered}} all-in + GST for a numbered Alberta corporation and {{price:incorporation-named}} for a named one.

### Do I need a NUANS report to incorporate in Alberta?

Yes for a named corporation — Alberta requires an Alberta-biased NUANS report so the registrar can confirm no identical or confusingly similar name exists. You do not need one for a numbered company, because Corporate Registry assigns the number itself.

### Sole proprietorship or incorporation — which is cheaper in Alberta?

A trade name is cheaper to register and cheaper to maintain, with no annual return. Incorporation costs more up front and carries a yearly filing, but limits your personal liability and opens access to the small business deduction. The right comparison is not the registration fee but the liability you are carrying and the tax position you are in.

### Do Alberta corporations need Canadian resident directors?

No. Alberta has no Canadian residency requirement for directors, which distinguishes it from federal (CBCA) incorporation, where 25% of directors must be Canadian residents. That makes Alberta a practical choice for founders and investors who are not resident in Canada.

### What do I have to do after registering a business in Alberta?

A corporation must file an annual return within one month of its incorporation anniversary, every year, with a $47 government fee. A trade name must be renewed every five years. Neither is a tax filing, and neither replaces municipal business licensing or GST registration where those apply.
