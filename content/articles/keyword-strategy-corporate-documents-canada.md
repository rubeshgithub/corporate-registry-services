# SEO Keyword Strategy — Cluster: Corporate Documents Retrieval (Canada)

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Site:** corporateregistryservices.ca · **Geography:** Canada (EN) · **Goal:** own the "how do I get a copy of my corporation documents" long tail → convert to `/order/corporate-documents`

## The search intent

Canadians who search this cluster are almost always in one of five situations:
1. **Bank/loan officer asked for articles of incorporation** — they need a specific PDF now.
2. **Lawyer/M&A due diligence** — they need every historical filing (annual returns, changes, etc.).
3. **Lost the original** — company was set up years ago, articles are missing.
4. **New CFO/accountant** — building out the compliance file for the first time.
5. **Estate/succession lawyer** — reconstructing the corporate record after the founder is gone.

All five convert well because they arrive with an external forcing function (someone else is waiting on the document). They are NOT casual research queries.

## The vocabulary problem

Canadians conflate multiple products under the same search:

| What they type | What they usually want | What we call it |
|---|---|---|
| "articles of incorporation" | Original founding document (Form 1 / articles) | Articles of Incorporation |
| "certificate of incorporation" | Government-issued birth certificate of the corp | Certificate of Incorporation |
| "incorporation papers" | Both of the above bundled | The incorporation package |
| "corporate documents" | Anything on file at the registry | Full corporate file / historical filings |
| "corporate records" | Same as above + internal minute-book records | Registry documents + minute book (two different things — this is a common trap) |
| "download corporation documents" | A DIY portal to grab PDFs (usually doesn't exist for older filings) | Retrieval service |

Ranking pages must use ALL of these terms while quietly correcting the difference between registry-held documents (retrievable) and internal minute-book documents (not retrievable — they were never filed).

## Competitive landscape

**Top-ranking competitors (SERP inspection, 2026-08-01):**
1. Government pages (Corporations Canada, provincial registries) — thin, product-focused, no explanatory content. Beatable.
2. Ontario Business Central, LawDepot — decent but generic, don't explain jurisdiction differences.
3. Legal blogs (Osler, Torys) — high authority but rarely target this specific query.

**Content gap to exploit:** No competitor page cleanly explains the difference between (a) documents held by the registry (retrievable) and (b) internal minute-book documents (never filed, must be reconstructed). This confusion is the #1 driver of frustrated searches. Owning this explanation = trust + rankings.
