## Where Do You Search the New Brunswick Corporate Registry?

Everything starts at Service New Brunswick's [Corporate Registry search page](https://www2.snb.ca/content/snb/en/sites/corporate-registry/registry.html), which links into the Corporate Affairs Registry Database — the searchable index maintained by the Corporate Registry branch. It covers New Brunswick-incorporated business corporations and non-profit companies, extra-provincial corporations registered to operate in the province, and partnership and business-name registrations.

The database offers three ways in:

- **Keyword search** — single or combined keywords against registered names. This is the search most people want: type part of the company name, get a list of matches.
- **Reference number search** — the file number the Corporate Registry branch assigned when the entity was incorporated or registered.
- **Business number search** — the 15-digit account number assigned to the business by the Province of New Brunswick in conjunction with the Canada Revenue Agency (e.g. `123456789NP0001`).

One thing worth knowing before you rely on a printout: SNB is explicit that the database **is not the official legal record**. The official record is the set of documents on file at the Registry itself — which is why certified copies exist (more on those below).

## What Do the Status Codes in Search Results Mean?

A New Brunswick search result leads with a one-letter status code, defined in the registry's [official glossary](https://www2.snb.ca/content/snb/en/sites/corporate-registry/glossary.html). For business corporations, the ones you'll actually see:

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| **A** | The corporation legally exists and is in compliance with its annual return obligations — what a bank or counterparty wants to see |
| **B** | The corporation legally exists but is **not current** on its annual returns |
| **F** | The corporation no longer exists — dissolved, or its charter forfeited |
| **I** | Dissolution proceedings have been started by the Director, or an intent to dissolve has been filed |
| **J** | The corporation amalgamated into another entity |
| **K** | The corporation continued out of New Brunswick into another jurisdiction |

Status B is the one that trips companies up. It usually just means a missed annual return — New Brunswick corporations must file within the 30 days following their incorporation anniversary, at $60 e-filed — but two years of unfiled returns puts a corporation on the road to involuntary dissolution. If that's your own corporation showing B, our guide to [filing your annual return in New Brunswick](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-new-brunswick) walks through the fix, or CRS can [file it for you](/order/annual-return?src=article-new-brunswick-corporate-registry-search) — $99 all-in + tax, filed within 1 business day.

## Can You Search New Brunswick Companies Through the Federal Registry?

Mostly no — and this catches a lot of people. [Canada's Business Registries](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cbr-rec/en/search), the federal multi-jurisdiction search, aggregates only some provincial registries, and New Brunswick's records live in SNB's own database rather than the federal aggregator. Searching a NB company name on the federal site and finding nothing does **not** mean the company doesn't exist — it usually just means you're searching the wrong registry.

The reverse trap exists too: a federally incorporated (CBCA) company operating in New Brunswick appears in Corporations Canada's database *and* as an extra-provincial registrant in the NB registry. No — that's Nova Scotia.
