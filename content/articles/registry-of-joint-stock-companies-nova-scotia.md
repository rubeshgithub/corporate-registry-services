The Registry of Joint Stock Companies — the RJSC, or just "Joint Stocks" to most Nova Scotians — is Nova Scotia's corporate registry: the provincial office where every company, business name, partnership, society and co-operative in the province is registered, renewed and searched. If you're looking for the registry itself, the free public search now lives in the province's **RJSC Connect** portal; if you need an official document from it, or your annual renewal filed, that's what CRS does. This guide covers what the RJSC is, how to search it for free, what it charges, and the filings it expects from you every year.

## What Is the Registry of Joint Stock Companies?

The RJSC is the Government of Nova Scotia's registry of businesses and non-profits, operated under Service Nova Scotia. Despite the old-fashioned name — "joint stock company" is simply the historical term for a share-issuing company — it covers far more than corporations. The registry holds the public record for:

- **Limited companies** incorporated under Nova Scotia's *Companies Act*
- **Business names** (sole proprietorships and operating names)
- **Partnerships** registered under the *Partnerships and Business Names Registration Act*
- **Societies and non-profits**
- **Co-operatives**
- **Extra-provincial registrations** — federal and out-of-province corporations carrying on business in Nova Scotia, which must register with the RJSC too

Every one of these entities has a public record at the RJSC showing its name, registration number, status, registered office, and (for companies) its directors and recognized agent. When a bank, lawyer or supplier wants to confirm a Nova Scotia business is real and in good standing, the RJSC record is what they check.

## What Is RJSC Connect?

RJSC Connect is the registry's online portal — it's where businesses and non-profits [register, renew and manage their filings](https://novascotia.ca/registry-joint-stock-companies-changes/) with the RJSC. The basic search is open to everyone; to file anything (a renewal, an address change, a new registration) you sign in or create an account. If you can't file online, the RJSC still accepts forms and payment by mail or in person at Access Nova Scotia, with roughly 1–2 weeks to process and update the record. Payment is by credit card, debit, cheque, money order or cash.

In practice, the account requirement is where many owners hand the job off: if you only touch the registry once a year, CRS files your renewals and changes without you ever setting up or remembering an RJSC Connect login.

## When Is Your RJSC Renewal Due?

Your renewal is due in the **anniversary month** of your original registration or incorporation — one of the tighter windows in Canada — and the RJSC sends a notice about a month ahead. Companies must also keep a recognized agent in the province and keep their registered office, directors and officers current on the registry; letting the renewal lapse starts the road to being struck off the registry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is searching the Registry of Joint Stock Companies free?

Yes. The RJSC's public search in RJSC Connect is free and doesn't require an account for a basic lookup — you'll see the entity's name, status, addresses and registration dates. Official documents like profile reports and certificates of status carry fees.

### Why is it called the Registry of Joint Stock Companies?

"Joint stock company" is the traditional legal term for a company whose capital is divided into shares. Nova Scotia kept the historical name for its registry, so locals say "Joint Stocks" — but it registers every business structure in the province, not just share companies.

### What happens if I don't renew my RJSC registration?

Your business falls out of good standing, and a registration left unrenewed can eventually be struck from the registry — which ends the company's legal capacity and puts its name back up for grabs. Renewal is due in your anniversary month each year; the RJSC mails a notice about a month before.

### Do federal corporations have to register with the RJSC?

Yes. A federal (CBCA) or out-of-province corporation carrying on business in Nova Scotia must register extra-provincially with the RJSC and keep that registration renewed, on top of its home-jurisdiction filings.

### How long does the RJSC take to process filings?

Filings made through RJSC Connect are the fastest route; paper forms submitted by mail or at Access Nova Scotia typically take 1 to 2 weeks to be processed and reflected on the registry record.
