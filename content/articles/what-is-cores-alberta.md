CORES is Alberta's **Corporate Registry System** — the government platform where every Alberta incorporation, annual return, amendment, and corporate search is actually processed. Here's the part that surprises most business owners: **you can't log into CORES yourself.** Access is restricted to accredited registry agents, law firms, and high-volume subscribers, which is why Alberta corporations file through an authorized CORES agent instead.

This guide covers what CORES does, who's allowed to use it, what the accreditation levels mean, and how to get your filings done without an account.

## What does CORES actually do?

Every transaction on the Alberta Corporate Registry flows through CORES, including:

- Incorporating a new Alberta corporation
- Filing the mandatory [annual return](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-alberta)
- Changing directors, registered office addresses, or corporate names
- Corporate searches and profile reports
- Dissolutions, revivals, and amalgamations
- Registering out-of-province (extra-provincial) corporations

If your corporation exists in Alberta, its official record lives in CORES — and keeping that record current isn't optional. Corporations that stop filing are flagged as non-compliant and eventually dissolved.

## Who can access CORES?

Access is limited to accredited users, not the general public. According to Service Alberta, direct access is available to:

- **Authorized registry agents** — private service providers accredited by the province. This is how most Albertans interact with the registry, whether they realize it or not.
- **Law firms in public practice in Alberta**, via the Registries Online subscriber program
- **High-volume subscribers** such as financial institutions — the program requires a minimum of 50 transactions per month and at least one accredited person on staff

Everyone else — including the owner of the corporation itself — files through one of these intermediaries.

## What are CORES Levels 1, 2, and 3?

CORES users are accredited at three levels, each requiring a separate Service Alberta exam. Higher levels authorize progressively more complex registry work — Level 1 covers foundational transactions, while Level 3 accreditation covers the most complex corporate filings. Training programs run 10–18 hours per level, and the exams are administered by Service Alberta itself, not the training providers.

What this means for you as a business owner: when a filing service says it's a **certified CORES registry agent**, someone on that team has passed the province's accreditation exams for the transactions they file on your behalf. It's worth asking — not every "filing service" online holds Alberta accreditation.

## Can I file through CORES myself?

No — and this catches a lot of first-time Alberta incorporators off guard, especially those coming from provinces where owners file directly through a government portal (Ontario's Business Registry, [BC Registry Services](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-british-columbia), or [Corporations Canada](/articles/How-to-File-Your-Annual-Return-Federal-Canada) federally). Alberta deliberately delivers registry services through accredited private agents rather than a public-facing website.

Practically, you have two routes:

1. **Walk into a physical registry office** — the same offices that handle licence plates. Done in person, during business hours, at fees each agent sets independently.
2. **Use an online CORES-certified agent** — the same legal filing, handled remotely, usually faster.
