The credit officer looks at your file, everything is signed, the funds are queued to release Friday — and then the email arrives: *"We need a current Certificate of Good Standing before we can advance."* Suddenly a Thursday afternoon is a scramble to figure out what document they actually want, where it comes from, and whether it'll arrive in time.

This guide covers what your bank is really asking for, how each of the big-5 handles it, and how to keep a stale certificate from delaying your funding.

## What a Certificate of Good Standing actually is

A Certificate of Good Standing (sometimes called a *Certificate of Status* in Ontario and Alberta, or a *Certificate of Compliance* / *Certificate of Existence* federally) is a one-page official document issued by the corporate registry where your company is incorporated. It confirms two things and only two things:

1. Your corporation legally exists as of the certificate's issue date.
2. Your corporation has filed everything it's required to file — annual returns, address updates, director changes — and is not in default.

That's it. It doesn't show directors or shareholders — that's a [Corporate Profile Report](/profile-reports). It doesn't show historical filings — that's a [Minute Book](/minute-books). It's a compact, government-sealed *yes/no* on whether the corporation is in good standing right now.

## Why banks ask for it before a business loan

Corporations Canada [publishes explicitly](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/business-corporations) that Certificates of Compliance are "often needed to support a loan request." Banks won't advance funds to a corporation that has been struck from the registry — the entity technically doesn't exist and can't sign a valid promissory note. So credit adjudicators require independent proof.

The Certificate of Good Standing is that proof. It's what the credit file needs to close, and it's what regulators expect to see in the file if the loan is later audited or securitised.

## What each big-5 bank typically asks for

Requirements vary by bank, product, and adjudicator — always confirm with your relationship manager — but the working defaults:

| Bank | Document usually asked for | Freshness window |
|---|---|---|
| **RBC** | Certificate of Good Standing / Certificate of Status | 30 days |
| **TD** | Certificate of Status (Ontario), Certificate of Good Standing (elsewhere) | 30 days |
| **Scotiabank** | Certificate of Good Standing | 30 days |
| **BMO** | Certificate of Good Standing plus a Corporate Profile Report for larger commercial files | 30 days (14 days on syndicated deals) |
| **CIBC** | Certificate of Status (Ontario) or Good Standing (other provinces) | 30 days |

For commercial loans above $500,000, expect the bank to also ask for a **Corporate Profile Report** showing current directors and registered office — used to verify signing authority. If your file involves multiple related corporations (holding company + operating company), each entity typically needs its own certificate.

## Ordering it: fast, all-in, no back-and-forth

If your bank asked for it today, you're looking at three options:

1. **Direct from the provincial or federal registry** — cheapest at $30–$60, but you'll need an account on the government portal (ONE-key for Ontario, CORES for Alberta, etc.), you're on their business hours, and the certificate arrives without any of the formatting most credit teams expect.
2. **Order through CRS** — $79 all-in + GST, PDF delivered by email within one business hour for most jurisdictions. Government fee included. No portal signup, no waiting on registry business hours.
3. **Through your lawyer or accountant** — reliable but adds a professional fee on top and rarely faster than option 2.

## How to avoid the stale-certificate delay

The single biggest reason a bank loan is held up at the finish line: the Certificate of Good Standing was pulled two weeks ago and by the time funding is queued it's outside the 30-day freshness window. The credit adjudicator sends it back, you re-order, and the whole file waits another 24–48 hours.

Two rules avoid this:

- **Don't pull the certificate until the loan is at "clear to close."** Once the file is 48–72 hours from funding, then order.
- **Confirm the freshness window with your banker in writing.** Some products (bridge loans, construction draws, term-loan renewals) use tighter windows than the bank's default. Written confirmation lets you time the pull.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Certificate of Good Standing the same as a Certificate of Status?

Yes — the two terms describe the same document. Ontario, Alberta, and Corporations Canada officially call it a *Certificate of Status* or *Certificate of Compliance* / *Certificate of Existence*; other provinces call it a *Certificate of Good Standing*. Every Canadian bank accepts whichever term your jurisdiction uses.

### Does the certificate confirm my corporation's directors or address?

No — that's a [Corporate Profile Report](/profile-reports). A Certificate of Good Standing only confirms that the corporation exists and is compliant with its filings. If your bank needs to verify signing authority or registered office, order both.

### What if my corporation isn't in good standing?

The registry won't issue a Certificate of Good Standing if you have outstanding annual returns or an active dissolution notice. File the outstanding [annual return](/annual-return) first — CRS files them within 24 hours — then order the certificate.

### Can I get the certificate on the same day?

For most Canadian jurisdictions, yes — through CRS, the certificate is delivered within one business hour of order. Some jurisdictions (Nunavut, Northwest Territories, and occasional slow days at REQ Quebec) run to one business day.
