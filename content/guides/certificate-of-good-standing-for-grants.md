Your grant application is 90% complete. Then you hit the compliance checklist and see the line: *"Attach current Certificate of Good Standing (dated within 30 days)."* The deadline is 10 days out. This guide explains exactly what funders want, why they want it, and how not to lose your intake window over paperwork.

## Why grant funders demand a Certificate of Good Standing

Federal and provincial grant programs — and virtually every not-for-profit funder — need to know that public money is going to a legally existing, compliant organisation. A Certificate of Good Standing is the single document that proves both. The Ontario Trillium Foundation, Ontario Gaming Grants, IRAP, Canada Cultural Investment Fund, provincial arts councils, and the Community Services Recovery Fund all require it. So do most private foundations that use OTF/Trillium-style application templates.

The certificate proves:

1. Your corporation legally exists on the certificate's issue date.
2. All required corporate registry filings — annual returns, address changes, director updates — are current.

If either fails, the funder rejects the application. In competitive intakes with more applicants than money, an incomplete compliance section is the fastest way to get filtered out before your project is even read.

## What "in good standing" means to a funder

The compliance officer reviewing your application is looking for two things:

- **Certificate of Good Standing** (also called *Certificate of Status* or *Certificate of Compliance*) — from your corporate registry
- **Sometimes also**: a Corporate Profile Report or Directors Register to verify signing authority and eligible-to-apply status

Some funders (OTF, larger federal programs) audit these against the registry themselves. If your certificate is genuine but you're in default on your annual return, the funder sees it and disqualifies the application before reading the ask.

## Timeline: how to time the certificate pull

Here's the pattern that wins:

1. **60+ days out from deadline** — Confirm your annual return is filed. If overdue, [file it now through CRS](/annual-return) (filed within 24 hours).
2. **30 days out** — Check the program's specific freshness window. Ontario Gaming requires the certificate be current at intake; OTF requires it dated within 30 days of application.
3. **14 days out** — Order the Certificate of Good Standing through CRS. Delivered by email within one business hour for most jurisdictions.
4. **Application day** — Attach the PDF to your application and submit.

Ordering the certificate at day 45 is a mistake — by the time you submit at day 5, it's a 40-day-old document and outside the freshness window. Time it late in the cycle, not early.

## What blocks issuance (fix before you order)

The registry will refuse to issue a Certificate of Good Standing if any of these are true:

- Annual return is overdue (most common — [file it now](/annual-return))
- Registered office address is out of date and the registry has issued a notice
- Director changes haven't been filed
- A dissolution notice is active

Fix these before ordering the certificate — the certificate itself is the last step, not the first. If you're not sure what your status is, order a [Corporate Profile Report](/profile-reports) first ($49) to see exactly what the registry has on file.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Certificate of Good Standing the same document across all Canadian jurisdictions?

Different name, same substance. Ontario calls it a *Certificate of Status*. Corporations Canada calls it a *Certificate of Compliance* or *Certificate of Existence*. Alberta calls it a *Certificate of Status*. Most other provinces call it a *Certificate of Good Standing*. Every major Canadian funder accepts whichever term is used in your jurisdiction.

### What if my organisation is a not-for-profit corporation?

The same certificate applies. Not-for-profit corporations incorporated federally (CNCA) or provincially issue Certificates of Good Standing / Status through the same registry. For federal not-for-profits, it's the *Certificate of Compliance*. See our [Not-for-Profit Incorporation guides](/not-for-profit) for the incorporation side.

### Do I need this even for small community grants?

Increasingly, yes. Any funder that has adopted OTF-style application templates requires the certificate — even for grants under $5,000. Check the program's application checklist before assuming.

### Will the funder accept an older certificate?

Almost never. The freshness window (usually 30 days, sometimes 14) is a hard cut-off. An expired certificate is one of the top three reasons applications are returned incomplete.
