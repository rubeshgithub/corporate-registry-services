A supplier gives you an invoice. A landlord signs a lease. A contractor bids on your project. A customer offers 60-day terms. Before you say yes, one question needs an answer: *is this actually a real, registered Canadian company, and can these people bind it?*

This guide walks through the exact process — free tools first, paid documents where the free tools stop — for verifying any Canadian corporation in under 5 minutes.

## The three-level verification framework

Not every situation needs a full corporate diligence pack. Match the tool to the risk:

| Level | Situation | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sanity check** | Vendor invoice, low-stakes contact | Free registry name search | Free |
| **Standard verification** | Contract signing, extended credit, customer onboarding | [Corporate Profile Report](/profile-reports) | $49 all-in |
| **High-stakes verification** | Purchase, financing, litigation, deep KYC | Profile Report + Certificate of Good Standing + Minute Book review | $49 + $79 + $299+ |

Do the free search first. If any red flag surfaces (name doesn't match, no results, status looks wrong), don't proceed on the free data — that's the moment to order the certified document.

## Level 1: The free sanity check (2 minutes)

Every Canadian corporate registry has a free name search. The fastest path when you don't know where a corporation is incorporated:

1. Go to the [Canada-wide corporations search](/canada-corporations-search) — it queries every provincial and federal registry at once.
2. Enter the company name, business number, or corporate access number.
3. Read the results carefully. Look for:
   - **Exact name match** — does the name on your invoice or contract exactly match a registered entity?
   - **Current status** — is it *Active* or something else?
   - **Jurisdiction** — is the company where they said they were?

If everything matches and the transaction is low-value, you may not need to go further. But if the amount is more than a few thousand dollars, or the counterparty is unfamiliar, always order the certified Profile Report.

## Level 2: The Corporate Profile Report (60 seconds to order)

A [Corporate Profile Report](/profile-reports) is the government-issued PDF that answers, definitively:

- Does this corporation exist?
- Is it currently in good standing?
- Who are the sitting directors?
- Where is its registered office?
- What is its official legal name (and any operating names)?
- When was it last active with the registry?

$49 all-in through CRS, PDF delivered by email within one business hour for most jurisdictions. This is the document your bank, lawyer, insurer, and any FINTRAC-regulated entity actually accepts as verification.

If the person handing you the contract isn't on the directors list, don't sign until you have a director's resolution authorising them.

## Level 3: When you need the full history

The Profile Report is a *current snapshot*. For due diligence involving prior directors, share transfers, past addresses, historical amalgamations, or old resolutions, you need the corporation's [Minute Book](/minute-books). This is the corporation's complete filed history — private, held by the corporation itself. If you're doing serious diligence (buying the business, extending large secured credit, litigating), Minute Book review is table stakes.

## Business Number vs. Corporation Number — don't confuse them

Two identifiers, two different purposes:

- **Business Number (BN)** — 9 digits, issued by CRA, used for tax filings, GST/HST, payroll. Doesn't identify the corporation on the corporate registry.
- **Corporation Number / Corporate Access Number** — issued by the incorporating registry (Corporations Canada for federal, provincial registries otherwise). This is what you search on the corporate registry.

If you only have the BN, most searches will still find the corporation because both numbers are cross-referenced in most registries. But the *primary* identifier for a corporation's registry record is its Corporation Number.

## Verifying operating names and trade names

Many Canadian businesses trade under a name different from their legal corporate name. Examples: *ABC Consulting* might legally be *1234567 Ontario Inc. o/a ABC Consulting*.

The Profile Report shows any operating names / trade names on file. If your contract is with the operating name only, the actual legal entity signing is the underlying corporation — verify both to make sure you know who's contracting with you.

## Red flags to catch on every verification

Order a Profile Report and specifically watch for:

- **Status not "Active"** — struck, dissolved, in default, or amalgamated. Do not sign.
- **Registered office is a mail-drop or unfamiliar address** — cross-check with the address on the invoice or contract.
- **Directors don't include the person signing** — get a directors' resolution before proceeding.
- **Last document filed is 2+ years ago** — the corporation may be on the path to administrative dissolution.
- **Recent amalgamation** — historical liabilities may or may not have carried over. Ask counsel.
- **Different jurisdiction than expected** — a company claiming Ontario head office but incorporated in the BVI is a different diligence conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I verify a Canadian company entirely for free?

You can do a basic sanity check for free through provincial and federal registries. But if you need the government-sealed PDF that banks, lawyers, insurers, and FINTRAC-regulated entities accept as evidence, that's the paid Corporate Profile Report ($49). Free lookups are for judgement; paid documents are for records.

### How do I search by Business Number?

Most registries let you search by either the Business Number (9-digit CRA number) or the Corporation Number. If you only have one, try it — most will cross-reference. Our [Canada-wide search](/canada-corporations-search) accepts either.

### What if the counterparty won't give me their registration details?

That itself is a red flag. Every legitimate Canadian corporation has a Business Number and a public registry record. If the counterparty won't share their legal name and registration number, either they're not incorporated or something is off.

### Are shareholders on public record in Canada?

Not usually. In most provinces, only directors are on the public corporate registry — shareholders are recorded in the corporation's private Minute Book, which is not disclosed to the public. British Columbia does record some shareholder information; federally, the *individuals with significant control* register (ISC) captures 25%+ beneficial owners but is only available to law enforcement and tax authorities, not the public.
