## Why incorporate in British Columbia?

**No Canadian resident director requirement.** BC has never imposed a residency rule on directors. Every director must be 18+, but no residency requirement applies — making BC one of the four Canadian jurisdictions (with Alberta, Quebec, and — as of 2023 — Ontario and Nova Scotia) open to fully non-resident founding teams. Federal (CBCA) still requires 25% Canadian residents.

**Modern BCA statute with clean drafting.** The BC Business Corporations Act was substantially rewritten in 2003, making it one of the more contemporary Canadian corporate statutes. The Articles of Incorporation defaults, share class provisions, and shareholder protections are well-understood by Canadian and US cross-border deal counsel.

**Deep Vancouver tech and biotech ecosystems.** Vancouver hosts one of Canada's largest tech clusters (Hootsuite, Slack's early roots, Traction on Demand, Bench, Later, and hundreds of Series A/B startups) and a growing biotech sector. Provincial incorporation aligns with local investor and legal ecosystems.

**Popular for cross-border and holding structures.** BC is a common choice for structures with US, UK, or Asia-Pacific founders, and for personal holding companies. The BCA's flexibility with share classes, restrictions on transfer, and unanimous shareholder agreements supports diverse ownership arrangements.

**Fast and reliable filing.** BC Registry Services processes online filings quickly — often same-day for named incorporations once the name is cleared. Turnaround is competitive with Ontario and Alberta.

## British Columbia incorporation requirements

Under the BC *Business Corporations Act*:

- **Incorporation Application** — filed with BC Registry Services, includes the Notice of Articles.
- **Articles** — the internal governance rules (share classes, transfer restrictions, meetings, director powers).
- **Notice of Articles** — the public-facing summary of the Articles that gets recorded with the registry.
- **Central Securities Register (CSR)** — mandatory record of every share issuance, transfer, and cancellation, plus the current shareholder list. Must be maintained at the records office in BC.
- **Records office in BC** — physical BC address where the corporate records (including the CSR) can be inspected. Can be the same as the registered office.
- **At least one director** — must be 18+. No Canadian residency requirement.
- **BC name reservation** or NUANS report — provincial name reservation is sufficient for BC-only operations; NUANS provides broader national protection.

## What happens after you incorporate

Every BC corporation has ongoing statutory obligations. In your first year:

- **BC Annual Report** — due within 2 months of your anniversary date, filed with BC Registry Services. (BC calls it an Annual Report, not an annual return, but it serves the same statutory purpose.) $43 government fee, filed at $99 all-in through CRS.
