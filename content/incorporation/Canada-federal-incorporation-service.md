Federal incorporation under the *Canada Business Corporations Act* (CBCA) creates a corporation with authority to operate across all of Canada, cross-Canada name protection, and the statute of choice for most institutional investors and cross-border transactions. The trade-off: federal corporations must have at least 25% Canadian resident directors and must file extra-provincial registration in each province where they carry on physical business.

## Why incorporate federally?

**Cross-Canada name protection.** A federal NUANS search reserves your corporate name across all 13 provinces and territories. A provincial name search only protects the name within that one province — meaning a competitor in another province could later register an identical or confusingly similar name and force you into an expensive rebrand or trademark fight. For any business with national ambitions, federal name protection is the biggest single reason to pick CBCA.

**Preferred by institutional investors and cross-border deals.** Canadian and US-based VCs, private equity firms, and corporate lenders often prefer CBCA portfolio companies because the statute is well-understood by cross-border deal counsel, the Articles of Incorporation follow standard patterns across firms, and closing paperwork tends to be smoother. If you're raising national or US-connected capital, federal is a safe default.

**Portable across all Canadian jurisdictions.** A federal corporation can register as extra-provincial in any province without re-incorporating. This is especially valuable for businesses that start regional but plan to expand, or that operate in industries with unpredictable geography (SaaS, e-commerce, professional services).

**Access to federal programs and grants.** SR&ED tax credits, federal innovation programs, government procurement contracts, and various sector-specific grants often have federal-corporation preferences or eligibility criteria.

**Prestige and credibility.** For B2B enterprise sales, government contracts, or international partnerships, a federal corporation registered with Corporations Canada carries more implicit weight than a small provincial corporation. This is soft but real.

## Federal incorporation requirements

Under the *Canada Business Corporations Act*:

- **Articles of Incorporation** (CBCA Form 1) — the founding document.
- **Notice of Directors** (Form 6) — names, addresses, and Canadian residency status of each initial director.
- **Notice of Registered Office** (Form 3) — physical address in Canada (not a PO box).
- **NUANS report** — mandatory for named corporations; reserves the name nationally for 90 days.
- **At least one director** — must be 18+. **At least 25% must be Canadian residents.** For a 1–3 director board, at least one director must be a Canadian resident.
- **Registered office in Canada** — can be in any province, doesn't need to match where you operate.

## What happens after you incorporate

Every CBCA corporation has ongoing statutory obligations. In your first year:

- **Federal annual return** — due within 60 days of your anniversary month, filed with Corporations Canada. $12 government fee, filed at $99 all-in through CRS.
