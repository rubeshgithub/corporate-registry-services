Nova Scotia is one of the more distinctive Canadian jurisdictions to incorporate in. Unlike Ontario, Alberta, or BC — all of which use modern *Business Corporations Acts* — Nova Scotia companies are formed under the older *Companies Act*, which uses a Memorandum + Articles of Association structure inherited from English company law. That creates some unique advantages, a few unique compliance requirements, and one specialised vehicle (the NSULC) that no other Canadian jurisdiction offers.

## Why incorporate in Nova Scotia?

**No Canadian resident director requirement.** Nova Scotia does not require any of your directors to be Canadian residents — just 18 or older. Alongside BC, Alberta, and Quebec, this makes NS one of the four Canadian provinces genuinely open to international founders. Federal (CBCA) requires 25% Canadian residents; a US-founded startup incorporating federally would have to appoint at least one Canadian director to the board.

**The NSULC option for cross-border structures.** Nova Scotia is unique in Canada for offering the Unlimited Liability Company (NSULC), a vehicle US tax planners use for "check-the-box" pass-through treatment under US law. If your business involves US shareholders or a US parent, an NSULC can materially improve after-tax outcomes. This is a specialist decision — book the consultation if you're not sure whether it applies to you.

**Straightforward annual compliance.** Nova Scotia's annual return government fee is $25 — one of the cheapest in Canada. Deadline is within 30 days of your anniversary date each year (one of the tightest windows, but CRS monitors it for you).

**Atlantic Canada operations stay simple.** If you're operating primarily in Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI, or Newfoundland, provincial NS incorporation avoids the extra-provincial registration you'd need on top of a federal corporation.

## Nova Scotia incorporation requirements

Because Nova Scotia uses the *Companies Act* rather than a modern BCA, the founding documents and required officers look a little different from other provinces:

- **Memorandum of Association** — the founding document specifying the corporation's name, share structure, and objects. Signed by the incorporator(s). Unique to NS's Companies Act framework.
- **Articles of Association** — the internal governance rules (share transfers, meetings, director powers, officer roles). Serves the role that by-laws serve in a BCA jurisdiction.
- **At least one director** — must be 18+. No Canadian residency requirement.
- **Corporate secretary appointment** — mandatory under the Companies Act. The secretary can be a director or a separate person, but the role must be filled.
- **Registered office in Nova Scotia** — a physical address (not a PO box) where records can be inspected.
- **Name reservation** — a name search through the Registry of Joint Stock Companies plus a NUANS report if the name has federal reach or cross-provincial similarity risk.
