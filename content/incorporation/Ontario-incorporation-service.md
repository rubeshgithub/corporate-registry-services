Ontario is Canada's largest business jurisdiction by GDP and the default incorporation choice for most small and mid-sized businesses operating in the province. The regulatory environment has modernised significantly in the past few years — the Ontario Business Registry replaced the old ministry filing system in 2021, and the 25% Canadian resident director requirement was removed in 2023 — making OBCA incorporation both faster and more accessible to international founders than it used to be.

## Why incorporate in Ontario?

**No Canadian resident director requirement.** As of July 2023, Ontario no longer requires any of your directors to be Canadian residents. This aligns Ontario with BC, Alberta, Quebec, and Nova Scotia — meaning international founders can now incorporate in Canada's largest market without appointing a Canadian director purely for compliance. Federal (CBCA) still requires 25% Canadian residents, so an Ontario corporation with 100% US or European founders is now genuinely feasible.

**Access to Canada's largest business market.** Ontario represents roughly 38% of Canadian GDP, the largest talent pool, and the deepest capital markets outside the US. If your customers, employees, or investors are primarily in the GTA, provincial Ontario incorporation avoids the extra-provincial registration burden a federal corporation would carry.

**Preferred structure for many VC-backed startups.** While federal (CBCA) is the default for some institutional investors, a large share of Canadian VCs and angel networks are comfortable with — and often prefer — OBCA for portfolio companies operating within Ontario. Term sheets and closing documents are well-established under OBCA and rarely encounter friction.

**Modern, fast filing infrastructure.** Since the OBR launched in 2021, Ontario incorporations that once took 5–10 business days now typically complete in 24–72 hours at the registry level. The paperwork is standardised, and status is trackable online.

## Ontario incorporation requirements

Under the Ontario Business Corporations Act:

- **Articles of Incorporation** — the founding document specifying name, share structure, restrictions on transfer, and any other special provisions.
- **Notice of Directors (Form 5)** — names and addresses of the initial board.
- **Notice of Registered Office (Form 3)** — physical address in Ontario (not a PO box) where records can be inspected.
- **Consent to Act as Director** — each initial director signs a consent (mandatory in Ontario and often forgotten in DIY minute books; buyer's lawyers flag missing consents in due diligence).
- **At least one director** — must be 18+. No Canadian residency requirement.
- **NUANS name search** for named corporations, or skip it for a numbered corporation.
- **Corporate name** — Ontario names are protected within Ontario only. If you want cross-Canada name protection, incorporate federally instead.

- **Annual return** — due within 6 months of your fiscal year-end, filed with the OBR (moved from the CRA in 2021). $22 government fee, filed at $99 all-in through CRS. See our [Ontario annual return guide](/articles/how-to-file-your-annual-return-in-ontario).
