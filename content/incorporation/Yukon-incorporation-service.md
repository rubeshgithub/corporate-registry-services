## Why incorporate in Yukon?

**No Canadian resident director requirement.** Yukon's Business Corporations Act has never imposed a residency rule on directors. Every director just needs to be 18+, making Yukon one of the Canadian jurisdictions (with BC, Alberta, Quebec, Ontario, and Nova Scotia) open to fully non-resident founding teams. Federal (CBCA) requires 25% Canadian residents.

**Alignment with mining and mineral exploration.** Yukon's economy is anchored in mining (gold, silver, zinc, copper, tungsten) and mineral exploration. Provincial incorporation aligns with the territorial regulatory environment and Yukon's licensing regimes for exploration and extraction.

**Northern tourism and outdoor operations.** Yukon has a strong tourism and outdoor-operator sector. Provincial incorporation aligns with the licensing regimes for outfitters, guides, and hospitality operators in the territory.

**Straightforward corporations statute.** Yukon's Business Corporations Act is close in structure to the federal CBCA — Canadian deal counsel and Canadian VC investors are comfortable with Yukon corporations. Standard Articles of Incorporation defaults are conventional and well-understood.

## Yukon incorporation requirements

Under the Business Corporations Act (Yukon):

- **Articles of Incorporation** — the founding document specifying name, share structure, and any special provisions.
- **Notice of Directors** — names and addresses of the initial board.
- **Notice of Registered Office** — physical Yukon address (not a PO box).
- **NUANS report or Yukon name search** — for named corporations. Numbered corporations skip this.
- **At least one director** — must be 18+. No Canadian residency requirement.
- **Filing through Corporate Affairs Yukon** — CRS handles this on your behalf; no territorial portal account required on your end.
