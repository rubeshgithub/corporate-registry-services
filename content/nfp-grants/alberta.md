# Grants for Nonprofits in Alberta (2026 Funding Guide)

Alberta funds its community sector through two flagship provincial programs — the Community Facility Enhancement Program (CFEP) for buildings and spaces, and the Community Initiatives Program (CIP) for projects and events — backed by the Alberta Foundation for the Arts (AFA) for arts organizations. If you are comparing grants for nonprofits in Alberta, these three names will come up in almost every conversation, and together they cover everything from a hall renovation to a new youth program to a season of theatre.

The province runs on predictable intake deadlines (many programs use January 15, May 15 and September 15), which rewards organizations that plan ahead. Federal programs, municipal grants and community foundations round out the picture. This guide sets out the verified amounts, matching rules and 2026 deadlines for the major programs, plus recent funding news, so you can build a realistic funding calendar for your organization.

Two cautions on program status. First, the CIP Operating Grant stream is closed and no longer accepting applications, so organizations seeking operating support should look to the project stream, the AFA (for arts mandates) or federal and municipal sources. Second, the Civil Society Fund — a pandemic-era grant for non-profits addressing social issues — has not run an open intake recently; its most recent published guidelines date from the 2022 round ([Civil Society Fund guidelines, open.alberta.ca](https://open.alberta.ca/publications/guidelines-civil-society-fund)). Treat it as inactive unless the province announces a new round, and check alberta.ca's [funding for non-profits page](https://www.alberta.ca/funding-for-non-profits) for what is currently open.

Both CFEP streams and the CIP project grant are matching programs: your organization must bring cash, eligible in-kind contributions or other grants equal to what you request. Build that into your fundraising plan before the deadline, not after.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Alberta's provincial grant programs are explicit: applicants must be Alberta-based non-profit organizations registered under legislation such as the Societies Act or equivalent provincial or federal statutes (registered charities also qualify). CFEP Large goes further, requiring at least two years of registration before you apply, and the CIP project grant requires at least one year of operations. Municipalities, for-profit companies and organizations with overdue grant reports are screened out.

That means an unincorporated community group is, for practical purposes, invisible to CFEP, CIP and the AFA. Incorporation creates the legal entity that holds the grant agreement, the board that signs it, and the paper trail — bylaws, minutes, financial statements, annual returns — that reviewers verify. Because Alberta's clock starts at registration (one year for CIP, two for CFEP Large), incorporating early is a strategic move even if your first application is a year away.

## How to strengthen your grant application

Start with the match. Alberta's flagship programs fund at most half of your project, so a credible application shows the other half — confirmed donations, reserves, other grants or documented in-kind contributions — before submission. Reviewers can tell the difference between "we will fundraise the balance" and a board-approved budget with sources named.

Next, get your registry file in order. Alberta cross-checks applicants against corporate registry records and prior grant reporting; an overdue annual return or missing final report on a past grant can sink an otherwise strong application. Third, be specific about community benefit: who uses the facility or program, how many people, and what changes as a result. For CFEP, letters from user groups and municipal support carry real weight. Finally, respect the calendar — decisions take roughly six months, so a January 15 application funds a project starting in the second half of the year. Plan cash flow accordingly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in Alberta?

The two flagship provincial programs are the Community Facility Enhancement Program (CFEP Small up to $125,000; CFEP Large up to $1 million) for facilities, and the Community Initiatives Program project grant (up to $75,000) for programs and events. Arts organizations can access Alberta Foundation for the Arts operating and project funding, and federal programs add further options.

### What are the CFEP deadlines in 2026?

CFEP Small accepts applications three times a year — January 15, May 15 and September 15 — with decisions roughly six months later. CFEP Large runs one annual cycle: an expression of interest due May 15, a call for applications June 15, and a final application deadline of July 30, with notification in December.

### Does Alberta require matching funds for community grants?

Yes, for its major programs. CFEP Small, CFEP Large and the CIP project grant are all approved on a matching basis: your organization must contribute an amount equal to or greater than the grant, and Government of Alberta funding cannot exceed half of total project costs. Confirmed matching sources strengthen your application significantly.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in Alberta?

No. Provincial programs require incorporated non-profit status — for example, registration under Alberta's Societies Act — rather than CRA charitable registration. Charitable status mainly matters for issuing tax receipts and accessing private foundations that only fund qualified donees. Many Alberta societies receive CFEP and CIP funding without being charities.

### How long does my organization need to exist before applying?

It depends on the program. The CIP project grant requires at least one year of operations, while CFEP Large requires registration for a minimum of two years. CFEP Small has no comparable published waiting period, but all programs require good standing — current filings and no overdue grant reports — so incorporate and stay compliant early.
