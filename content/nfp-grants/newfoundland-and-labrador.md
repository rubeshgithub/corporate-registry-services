# Grants for Not-for-Profits in Newfoundland and Labrador (2026 Funding Guide)

Community organizations carry a lot of weight in Newfoundland and Labrador — running festivals, maintaining trails, staffing museums and keeping rural halls open — and the provincial government funds them accordingly. The most significant grants for nonprofits in Newfoundland and Labrador come from two departments: Municipal and Community Affairs, which runs the Community Enhancement Employment Program for rural job-creating projects, and Tourism, Culture, Arts and Recreation (TCAR), which funds everything from community festivals to arts facilities. ArtsNL, the province's arm's-length arts council, adds jury-assessed grants for artistic work.

Federal programs, the Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency and the Community Foundation of Newfoundland and Labrador complete the funding landscape. Nearly all of it comes with the same threshold requirement: an incorporated non-profit organization in good standing. This guide covers the provincial programs we verified in July 2026, with amounts, intake windows and recent announcements.

The **Community Enhancement Employment Program** is unusual among community grants: it funds wages. Municipalities, Inuit Community Governments, local service districts, community and economic development organizations and other non-profit organizations apply for projects — trail work, facility renovations, tourism improvements — that employ local residents for up to 470 hours, helping workers in rural areas while getting real community work done. The 2026–27 call opened May 22, 2026 with a June 26 deadline ([NL news release, May 22, 2026](https://www.gov.nl.ca/releases/2026/mca/0522n03/)), and the program runs annually, so a missed spring deadline means preparing for next year's call.

If you have older program lists mentioning the **Community Healthy Living Fund**, be aware that it no longer appears among TCAR's current funding programs and its former program page has been removed; the department's active recreation and healthy-living funding now runs through the **Active NL Fund** ([TCAR funding programs](https://www.gov.nl.ca/tcar/funding-programs/)). TCAR's list also includes the Sport Championship Hosting Program, KidSport and the Indigenous Cultural Heritage Program for organizations with those mandates.

On the arts side, **ArtsNL** runs jury-assessed programs including the Professional Project Grants Program and a Creation Grant Program supporting Indigenous-led projects; eligibility criteria and deadlines change between intakes, so verify against the current guidelines at [artsnl.ca](https://artsnl.ca/) before preparing an application. TCAR's own Arts Infrastructure and Programming Fund, by contrast, is squarely aimed at incorporated not-for-profit organizations that operate arts facilities or deliver arts programming.

## Federal grants Newfoundland and Labrador not-for-profits can also access

Federal programs are a natural complement, especially for organizations whose provincial funding covers projects but not people. [Canada Summer Jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/canada-summer-jobs.html) subsidizes summer youth wages and is heavily used by museums, heritage sites and recreation groups across the province. The [New Horizons for Seniors Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/new-horizons-seniors-community-based.html) funds community-based seniors projects — its June 2026 call accepted proposals of up to $50,000 until July 14, 2026, and calls recur annually. The [Enabling Accessibility Fund](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/enabling-accessibility-fund-small-projects.html) helps pay for accessibility upgrades to community buildings. See our [federal grants guide](/nfp-grants/federal) for the full list and current intakes.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

The eligibility lists above are consistent. CEEP names "other non-profit organizations" alongside municipalities and development organizations. The Cultural Events Fund funds not-for-profit community groups — not individuals or for-profit companies. The Arts Infrastructure and Programming Fund goes further and spells it out: applicants must be *incorporated and in good standing in Newfoundland and Labrador*.

That phrase — in good standing — matters. Incorporation gets you into the applicant pool, but staying registered, filing annual returns and keeping your directors' information current is what keeps you there. A lapsed corporate registration can sink an otherwise strong application at the eligibility screen. If your group is not yet incorporated, or your registration has drifted, our guide to [incorporating a not-for-profit in Newfoundland and Labrador](/not-for-profit/newfoundland-and-labrador) covers the process, and keeping the annual filings current afterward is exactly the kind of housekeeping funders quietly check.

## How to strengthen your grant application

Timing is strategy in this province. The Cultural Events Fund and the Arts Infrastructure and Programming Fund both operate first-come until their budgets are spent — TCAR explicitly encourages applying early in the year, well before your event. CEEP runs on a firm annual deadline, so build your project scope and cost estimates (the province publishes a project cost calculator with the application guide) in the winter, ready for the spring call.

Beyond timing, the fundamentals: align your project with the program's stated purpose and say so plainly; present a budget that respects the cost-share rules (the arts fund covers 90% only on basic projects — larger asks require you to bring 10–20% or more); and document your governance with a board list, financial statements and your certificate of incorporation. Describe outcomes in countable terms — jobs created and hours worked for CEEP, attendance and participation for events funding. Include letters of support from your town council, local service district or partner organizations; for rural employment and infrastructure projects, municipal endorsement is persuasive. Finally, budget time for reporting — TCAR requires final reports (within 30 days of completion for the arts fund), and clean reporting on a small grant is the strongest argument for a bigger one next year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in Newfoundland and Labrador?

Key provincial programs include the Community Enhancement Employment Program (rural employment projects — over $4.7 million awarded in the first 2025–26 round), the Cultural Events Fund ($1,000–$2,000 for festivals), the Arts Infrastructure and Programming Fund (up to $40,000), the Active NL Fund for recreation, and ArtsNL's jury-assessed arts grants.

### What happened to the Community Healthy Living Fund?

The Community Healthy Living Fund no longer appears among the current funding programs listed by the Department of Tourism, Culture, Arts and Recreation, and its former program page has been removed. Organizations seeking healthy-living and recreation funding should look to the department's Active NL Fund, which supports equipment, trails, facilities and active-living programming.

### Who can apply for the Community Enhancement Employment Program?

Municipalities, Inuit Community Governments, local service districts, community and economic development organizations, and other non-profit organizations. Projects must support tourism or economic development, community or municipal infrastructure, or community services in rural areas, and provide short-term employment — up to 470 hours per individual in 2026–27. Applications run through an annual spring call.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in Newfoundland and Labrador?

No. Provincial programs like CEEP, the Cultural Events Fund and the Arts Infrastructure and Programming Fund require incorporated non-profit status rather than CRA charitable registration. Charitable status becomes relevant when you want to issue donation tax receipts or approach private foundations, but it is not required for these government programs.

### When should a nonprofit apply for the Cultural Events Fund?

As early as possible. The fund assesses applications first-come, first-served for events taking place between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027, and TCAR encourages organizations to apply early in the year, well before the event date. The minimum grant for 2026–27 is $1,000, with multi-day festivals eligible for more.
