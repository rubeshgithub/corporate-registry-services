# Grants for Not-for-Profits in Ontario (2026 Funding Guide)

Grants for nonprofits in Ontario come from three main directions: the Ontario Trillium Foundation (OTF), which runs the province's flagship community granting program on a predictable annual calendar; Ontario government ministries, which fund seniors' programming, sport and recreation infrastructure and workforce training; and the federal government, whose programs — from Canada Summer Jobs to New Horizons for Seniors — are open to Ontario organizations on top of everything provincial. Municipalities and community foundations add a further local layer in most cities.

For a new or growing organization, the good news is that Ontario's biggest funder publishes its deadlines a year in advance. The less good news is that nearly every program on this page requires you to be an incorporated not-for-profit with a board, a track record and financial statements before you can apply. This guide covers the major programs, the 2026 intake windows, and what to have in place before the next deadline.

The Ontario Trillium Foundation deserves special attention because its three Community Investments streams are designed as a ladder. A Seed grant funds the unglamorous work of getting organized — strategy, technology, staff training, pilots — for organizations that have operated for at least 12 months in one of OTF's four sectors (sports and recreation, arts and culture, environment, and human and social services). A Grow grant then scales what works, with two- to three-year funding, overhead allowed up to 15 per cent and even a capital component up to 20 per cent. The Capital grant handles bricks, mortar and equipment on a 12-month cycle. Eligibility across all three extends to incorporated non-profits, First Nations, small municipalities and collaboratives serving one of OTF's 16 catchment areas, and decisions arrive roughly four to five months after each deadline.

On the sport and recreation side, provincial investment currently flows mainly through the Community Sport and Recreation Infrastructure Fund, first announced in April 2024 and topped up with an additional $300 million in the 2026 Ontario Budget; eligibility details and intake dates for the expanded fund are expected later in 2026. The longer-running Ontario Sport and Recreation Communities Fund is listed on the Grants Ontario portal, but no current intake could be confirmed at the time of writing — check the [Grants Ontario funding directory](https://www.ontario.ca/page/available-funding-opportunities-ontario-government) for its status alongside dozens of other ministry programs. That directory is the best single place to scan everything the province currently has open, from arts and culture supports to the Skills Development Fund, whose Training Stream names non-profit organizations (including Indigenous Band offices) among its eligible applicants.

Many Ontario municipalities also run their own community grant streams for local arts, recreation and social-service groups; amounts and deadlines vary widely, so check your city or county website once your provincial applications are mapped out.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Look at any OTF eligibility page and the first listed applicant type is "non-profit organizations" — meaning incorporated ones. OTF also expects at least 12 months of operations, demonstrated financial and organizational capacity, and programming in one of its four funding sectors. Provincial ministry programs are similar: the Seniors Community Grant Program funds "local not-for-profit community groups and organizations," and the Skills Development Fund screens applicants for legal status before anything else.

In practice, that means an unincorporated committee — however worthy its project — usually cannot sign the funding agreement, open the dedicated bank account or provide the financial statements reviewers ask for. If your group intends to apply to the OTF Seed intake closing August 19, 2026, or the Grow intake closing November 4, 2026, incorporation should happen first, since eligibility also depends on months of operating history. Ontario groups can incorporate provincially under the Not-for-Profit Corporations Act (ONCA) or federally; our [Ontario incorporation guide](/not-for-profit/ontario) compares the two routes.

## How to strengthen your grant application

OTF and ministry reviewers read hundreds of applications per intake, and the strongest ones share a few habits. First, match the stream to your actual stage: pitching a brand-new idea to Grow (which funds proven programs) or an expansion to Seed (which funds capacity) is the most common self-inflicted rejection. Second, ground the need in local evidence — OTF assesses projects within specific catchment areas, so data and testimonials from your own community carry more weight than provincial statistics.

Third, budget honestly against the eligible-cost rules: Grow allows overhead to 15 per cent and capital to 20 per cent, and reviewers notice budgets that ignore those ceilings. Fourth, show governance health — an engaged board, current bylaws, clean financial statements and up-to-date corporate filings signal that your organization can steward public money. Finally, collect letters of support from partner organizations and, where relevant, your municipality; they corroborate both the need and your capacity to deliver.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in Ontario?

The largest recurring source is the Ontario Trillium Foundation, with Seed grants ($10,000–$100,000), Grow grants (up to $600,000 over three years) and Capital grants ($10,000–$200,000). Provincial ministries add the Seniors Community Grant Program, sport and recreation infrastructure funding and the Skills Development Fund, while federal programs like Canada Summer Jobs stack on top.

### When are the Ontario Trillium Foundation deadlines for 2026?

The Capital grant window ran February 4 to March 4, 2026. The Seed grant application period runs July 22 to August 19, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. ET, and the Grow grant period runs October 7 to November 4, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. ET. OTF publishes each year's calendar in advance at otf.ca.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in Ontario?

No. OTF and most provincial programs fund incorporated not-for-profit organizations regardless of charitable registration. Charity status with the CRA matters mainly for issuing tax receipts and for some private foundations. What funders do require is incorporation, an active board, financial statements and — for OTF — at least a year of operations.

### How much can you get from an OTF Seed grant?

Seed grants range from $10,000 to $100,000 for projects lasting 6 or 12 months. They fund capacity-building: creating or adapting strategy, developing digital technology for service delivery, training staff and volunteers, or piloting an innovative program. The 2026 application window is July 22 to August 19, 2026.

### Can a new organization apply for Ontario Trillium Foundation funding?

Not immediately. OTF expects applicants to have operated for at least 12 months and to demonstrate financial and organizational capacity, and grants go to incorporated non-profits, First Nations, eligible municipalities and collaboratives. Incorporating now starts that clock — a new organization can use its first year to build governance, keep records and prepare a competitive Seed application.
