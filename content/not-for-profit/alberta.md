# How to Incorporate a Society in Alberta (Societies Act Guide)

In Alberta, the most common way to create a not-for-profit organization is to incorporate a **society** under the *Societies Act*, R.S.A. 2000, c. S-14 — the province itself describes it as [the simplest and least costly way to incorporate a non-profit](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-a-non-profit). If you're wondering how to incorporate a society in Alberta, the process runs through the Alberta Corporate Registry, with the NUANS name-search system and Alberta's network of registry agents playing supporting roles.

This guide is for founders doing it for the first time: community league volunteers, sports club organizers, cultural and religious groups, and associations that may one day seek grants or charitable status. It covers the five-member minimum, the forms in the [official application package](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-a-society), the $51.50 fee, the annual return — and when the Part 9 non-profit company is the better fit.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Alberta

A society is a membership organization incorporated for purposes such as recreation, culture, community benefit or charity — its "objects" are stated in the application and define what the organization exists to do. The trade-off for the simple, low-cost structure is a real limitation: an Alberta society cannot carry on a significant commercial business, cannot issue shares, and cannot declare dividends or otherwise distribute gains to its members. Revenue is allowed; distributing it as profit is not.

Organizations expecting permanent, revenue-generating operations — Alberta's guidance gives examples like second-hand stores and golf courses — should instead look at the **non-profit company** under Part 9 of the *Companies Act*, covered below. Faith-based congregations have a third option, the religious society under the *Religious Societies Land Act*.

Neither structure automatically makes you a charity: registered charity status — and the ability to issue donation receipts — comes separately from the [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/charities.html) after incorporation. And unlike a business corporation, a society exists for its objects, not its owners — members with votes, no shareholders with equity.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

An unincorporated association has no legal existence apart from its members, so contracts, leases and liabilities land on individual volunteers. Incorporation under the *Societies Act* creates a separate legal person: the society can hold property, open a bank account, sign agreements and sue or be sued in its own name, and it carries on regardless of turnover on the board — perpetual existence that outlasts any founder.

Incorporation also generally limits members' personal liability, and it is the credential funders expect: most Alberta grant programs require applicants to be incorporated non-profits in good standing, often with financial statements and a functioning board. If charitable registration with the CRA is part of your long-term plan, an incorporated body with clear objects and governance documents is the practical starting point — and banks routinely ask for the certificate of incorporation before opening an account.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate a society in Alberta

1. **Gather your founding members.** Five or more people are required to form an Alberta society. Agree on the name, the objects (purposes) and who will serve as the first directors.
2. **Get an Alberta-biased NUANS report.** Before filing, obtain a NUANS name search report to confirm no identical or confusingly similar name exists. You can order one through a [NUANS service provider](https://www.nuans.com/app/scr/corp/nuans/public/resources/search.html?lang=eng) — many Alberta registry agents provide this service. The report reserves your proposed name for 90 days and must be less than 91 days old when you submit your application.
3. **Complete the Application to Form a Society (REG3088).** This [official form](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG3088) sets out the society's name, its objects and the applicants' signatures. Forms must be signed in duplicate.
4. **Prepare your bylaws.** The bylaws govern membership, meetings, voting, and the election and duties of directors and officers. Alberta publishes [sample society bylaws](https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/sa-society-bylaws-form.pdf) you can adapt; the full list of documents is on the [incorporation forms for non-profits](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporation-forms-non-profits) page.
5. **Complete the notices.** File the [Notice of Address for a Society (REG0022)](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG0022) giving the society's registered address in Alberta, and the [Notice of Directors for a Society (REG12362)](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG12362) listing your first board, together with a request-for-service form.
6. **Mail the package and pay the fee.** Send the signed duplicate forms, the NUANS report and the $51.50 fee (cheque or money order payable to the Government of Alberta — credit cards are not accepted by mail) to Alberta Corporate Registry, Box 1007 Station Main, Edmonton, Alberta T5J 4W6.
7. **Receive your certificate.** Once approved, Corporate Registry mails your certificate of incorporation, and the CRA issues the society's federal business number by email. Your society now legally exists and can open accounts, sign contracts and apply for funding.

## The alternative: an Alberta non-profit company under Part 9 of the Companies Act

If your organization will run a permanent, revenue-generating operation, Part 9 of the *Companies Act* offers the [non-profit company](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-non-profit-company). It is formed with a Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association rather than an application and bylaws, needs at least 2 people for a private company (maximum 50 members, no public share sales) or 3 for a public one, and costs $77.25 in government fees. A NUANS report is required for named companies; Alberta residency requirements for directors were eliminated on March 29, 2021; and non-profit companies file their own annual return, [form REG11322](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG11322). Alberta recommends legal advice on which structure fits before you file.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

An Alberta society keeps its status by filing an **annual return** — the [Annual Return for a Society, form REG3185](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG3185) — with Corporate Registry every year. As the province's [annual returns guidance](https://www.alberta.ca/corporations-cooperatives-organizations-annual-returns) puts it, filing the annual return is how you keep your non-profit organization active and in good standing. A society that stops filing risks eventually being struck from the register and dissolved, ending its legal existence and grant eligibility; confirm current deadlines with Corporate Registry.

Between annual returns, keep the registry record current: file a [Change of Address (REG0022)](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG0022) when the registered office moves and a [Change of Directors (REG12362)](https://cfr.forms.gov.ab.ca/Form/REG12362) when the board changes — the full list is on the [registration forms for non-profits](https://www.alberta.ca/registration-forms-non-profits) page. Internally, follow your own bylaws: hold the meetings they require, keep minutes, membership records and financial statements, and file amendments to objects or bylaws with Corporate Registry. Note that [Corporate Registry does not supervise the conduct of non-profits](https://www.alberta.ca/responsibilities-corporations-cooperatives-organizations) — disputes among members are yours to resolve under your bylaws.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need to start a society in Alberta?

Five or more people are required to form a society under Alberta's *Societies Act*. They sign the Application to Form a Society (form REG3088), which states the society's name and objects. By contrast, a Part 9 non-profit company can be formed by as few as 2 people (private) or 3 (public).

### How much does it cost to register a society in Alberta?

The government fee to incorporate an Alberta society is $51.50, payable to the Government of Alberta by cheque or money order — credit cards are not accepted for mailed applications. On top of that, budget for the NUANS name search report, which is purchased separately from a NUANS service provider or registry agent, and any professional help with bylaws.

### How many board members does an Alberta society need?

The *Societies Act* does not fix a minimum board size the way some provinces do; your bylaws determine how directors are elected, removed and how many there are. You must, however, file a Notice of Directors (form REG12362) listing your board at incorporation and keep it updated. Grant funders often expect a functioning multi-person board in practice.

### Do Alberta societies have to file an annual return?

Yes. Every Alberta society files an annual return with Corporate Registry using form REG3185 to remain active and in good standing. A society that stops filing risks being struck from the register and dissolved, losing its legal existence and grant eligibility. Confirm your society's current filing deadline directly with Alberta Corporate Registry.

### Can an Alberta society run a business or make a profit?

A society can earn revenue from memberships, events or fundraising, but it cannot distribute gains to members — no shares, no dividends — and it cannot carry on a significant commercial business. Organizations planning permanent business operations, such as a store or facility, should consider incorporating as a non-profit company under Part 9 of Alberta's *Companies Act* instead.

### Is an Alberta society the same as a registered charity?

No. Incorporating a society is a provincial step that creates a legal entity under the *Societies Act*. Charitable registration is a separate federal application to the Canada Revenue Agency, and only registered charities can issue official donation receipts. Most Alberta charities incorporate first — as a society or non-profit company — and then apply to the CRA.
