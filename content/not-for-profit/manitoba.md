# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit in Manitoba (Corporations Act Guide)

Understanding how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Manitoba starts with one piece of legislation: [The Corporations Act](https://web2.gov.mb.ca/laws/statutes/ccsm/c225.php) (C.C.S.M. c. C225). Unlike provinces with a standalone societies or non-profit statute, Manitoba incorporates its non-profits as **corporations without share capital** under Part XXII of the same Act that governs business corporations. A non-share corporation has members instead of shareholders and pursues purposes — charitable, religious, educational, sporting, cultural and more — rather than profit.

Incorporation is administered by the [Manitoba Companies Office](https://companiesoffice.gov.mb.ca/non_profit_corporations.html), part of Entrepreneurship Manitoba, which accepts filings online and on paper. This guide is written for first-time incorporators — community groups, sports clubs, cultural associations, faith organizations and would-be charities — who want the whole path laid out before they file.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Manitoba

Manitoba law does not use the label "not-for-profit corporation" as a separate entity type; the legal vehicle is a **corporation without share capital** under Part XXII of The Corporations Act. Because there are no shares, there are no shareholders — the corporation instead has members, whose rights are set out in the articles and by-laws, and its purposes must fall within categories the Companies Office accepts: patriotic, religious, charitable, educational, agricultural, scientific, literary, artistic, social, professional, fraternal, sporting, athletic or similar objects. Your articles must describe your purpose in enough detail to show it fits.

The defining feature is the non-distribution constraint: income and property are applied to advancing the corporation's purposes, not paid out to members. That is also what separates a non-share corporation from a for-profit business corporation, where shareholders are entitled to dividends and capital gains.

It is equally important to distinguish provincial incorporation from **registered charity** status. Incorporating in Manitoba creates the legal entity; becoming a registered charity is a separate application to the Canada Revenue Agency, and only registered charities can issue official donation tax receipts. Many Manitoba organizations incorporate first with charitable purposes, then pursue CRA registration once they are operating.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

An unincorporated association is legally just its members — which means volunteers can be personally exposed on leases, loans and contracts. Incorporation creates a separate legal person that carries those obligations itself, giving directors and members meaningful liability protection when the organization is properly run. It also gives your group perpetual existence: boards turn over, founders retire, but the corporation continues, keeping its property, agreements and name intact.

Credibility is the other practical driver. Granting bodies, sponsors and government programs almost universally require applicants to be incorporated, and banks will want a certificate of incorporation and corporate documents before opening an organizational account. Incorporation also lets your organization hold real property in its own name, and it lays the groundwork for a future charitable registration with the CRA — much easier when your articles were drafted with charitable purposes in mind from day one.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Manitoba

1. **Choose and reserve your name.** File a Request for Name Reservation with the Companies Office ($45) — online filing is available and fastest. The office checks the proposed name against existing registrations; once reserved, your incorporation must be filed before the reservation expires.
2. **Assemble your board of at least three directors.** Every Manitoba non-share corporation, including one with charitable purposes, needs a minimum of three directors. Each must be an individual (not a corporation), at least 18 years old and not bankrupt, and at least 25% of the board must be resident in Canada.
3. **Prepare your Articles of Incorporation (without share capital).** Use the official form or its online equivalent. The articles set out your exact reserved name, a full Manitoba civic address (with postal code) for the registered office, your purposes within the accepted categories, membership classes, and provisions for the distribution of property on dissolution. All incorporators — each 18 or older and not bankrupt — must sign.
4. **Collect director consents.** Any first director who is not also an incorporator must sign a Consent to Act as First Director, filed with the articles.
5. **File and pay the fee.** Submit the articles online through the [Companies Office Online system](https://companiesonline.gov.mb.ca/Account/Login) or on paper, with the $120 fee. Online filing offers the fastest processing.
6. **Receive your Certificate of Incorporation.** Once issued, your corporation legally exists. Hold an organizational meeting to pass by-laws, appoint officers, admit members and set the financial year — and diarize your annual return date immediately.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

Every Manitoba non-share corporation must file an **annual return** with the Companies Office, with a $40 fee, due by the end of the month following the month of your incorporation anniversary. The return keeps the public record current — directors, officers and registered office — and filing it online through Companies Office Online is the quickest route. The consequence of neglect is stark: a corporation that fails to file its annual return for two consecutive years is subject to automatic dissolution, which strips away limited liability, freezes the corporate name and complicates any property the organization holds. Revival is possible but costs time and money you would rather spend on your mission.

Between annual returns, keep your registrations accurate: changes to directors or officers and changes of registered office address each have their own notice filings, and amendments to your articles (such as a name or purpose change) require Articles of Amendment with the applicable fee. Good corporate housekeeping also means holding an annual general meeting, maintaining registers of members and directors, keeping minutes, and preserving signed by-laws — the very records grant funders and the CRA ask to see.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many directors do I need for a non-profit in Manitoba?

A minimum of three. The Companies Office requires every corporation without share capital — including one incorporated for charitable purposes — to have at least three directors. Directors must be individuals (not corporations), at least 18 years old and not bankrupt, and at least 25% of the board must be residents of Canada.

### How much does it cost to register a non-profit in Manitoba?

Plan for $165 in government fees: $45 to reserve your corporate name and $120 to file Articles of Incorporation without share capital. After that, the recurring cost is a $40 annual return each year. Professional help with drafting purposes, by-laws and consents is extra but often saves costly amendments later.

### What law governs non-profit corporations in Manitoba?

The Corporations Act, C.C.S.M. c. C225 — the same statute that governs Manitoba business corporations. Non-profits are created under Part XXII as corporations without share capital, meaning they have members rather than shareholders. Manitoba has not adopted a standalone non-profit corporations act, so Part XXII plus the general provisions of the Act apply.

### Do Manitoba non-profit directors have to live in Canada?

Not all of them. The requirement is that at least 25% of the directors be residents of Canada. On a minimum three-person board, that means at least one director must be a Canadian resident. All directors, wherever they live, must be individuals aged 18 or older who are not bankrupt.

### What happens if a Manitoba non-profit doesn't file its annual return?

Miss one year and you are offside; miss two consecutive years and the Companies Office can dissolve the corporation automatically. Dissolution ends the corporation's legal existence — contracts, property and the protection of limited liability are all thrown into doubt. The annual return costs $40 and is due by the end of the month after your anniversary month.

### Can a Manitoba non-profit become a registered charity?

Yes. Incorporation under Part XXII is often the first step: you incorporate provincially with exclusively charitable purposes, then apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for registered charity status. CRA registration is a separate federal process, and only registered charities may issue official donation receipts, so plan your articles with that application in mind.
