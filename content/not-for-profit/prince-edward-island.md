# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit in Prince Edward Island (Companies Act Part II Guide)

If you are researching how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Prince Edward Island, the first thing to know is which law applies. Despite PEI's recent corporate modernization — the Business Corporations Act overhauled the rules for business companies — Island non-profits still incorporate under Part II of the venerable Companies Act. As of July 2026, no standalone modernized not-for-profit corporations statute has come into force in PEI, so community organizations continue to be created by letters patent on a petition to the province.

Incorporation is handled by PEI's Corporate Registry, administered by the Consumer, Corporate and Financial Services Division of the Department of Justice and Public Safety, with filings submitted online through the provincial registry portal. This guide walks first-time founders — sports clubs, cultural associations, religious groups, service organizations and future charities — through the requirements, fees and annual obligations in plain English.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Prince Edward Island

Part II of the Companies Act allows a minimum of three people to petition for incorporation of a corporation without share capital, formed for purposes of a patriotic, religious, philanthropic, charitable, scientific, artistic, social, professional or sporting character, or the like. Instead of shareholders there are members, and instead of articles there are letters patent — a charter granted by the province that sets out the corporation's name, objects and first directors.

The non-distribution constraint is the legal heart of the form: a PEI non-profit corporation exists to advance its stated purposes, not to enrich its members. It can charge membership dues, run fundraising events and hold surpluses, but profits must be applied to the organization's objects rather than paid out to members.

Keep two distinctions straight. A non-profit corporation is not the same as a registered charity — charitable status comes separately from the Canada Revenue Agency and is what permits official donation receipts. And it is not a business corporation: if your venture's real purpose is commercial, PEI's Business Corporations Act is the correct vehicle, not Companies Act Part II.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

An unincorporated committee can run a bake sale; it struggles to sign a five-year lease. Incorporation creates a legal person separate from its members — one that can hold property, open bank accounts, hire staff, enter contracts and sue or be sued in its own name. Members gain limited liability, and the organization gains perpetual existence: founders retire, boards rotate, and the corporation continues.

For Island organizations, the most immediate benefit is often eligibility. Provincial programs, federal funders and community foundations almost always require applicants to be incorporated, and many want to see an active board, financial statements and a corporate registry listing in good standing before they release a cent. Incorporation is also the foundation for later CRA charitable registration, where your letters patent and by-laws must align with recognized charitable purposes. Getting the governing documents right at the start saves an expensive supplementary letters patent application later.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Prince Edward Island

1. **Line up your people.** You need a minimum of three applicants to petition for incorporation and a minimum of three directors. Most founding groups name the same people to both roles at the start.
2. **Choose a name and order the name search.** Review PEI's business name rules before settling on a name. A $40 name search is part of the standard filing package and confirms your proposed name is available.
3. **Prepare the Petition for Incorporation.** The petition states the proposed corporate name, the purpose of the corporation, the head office location in PEI, the applicants' names, addresses and occupations, and the provisional officers and directors. It must be sworn by affidavit before a Commissioner of Oaths or Notary Public. The province recommends this document be prepared by a legal professional.
4. **Prepare the Memorandum of Agreement.** This companion document functions as the by-laws, covering membership, operations, how directors and officers are chosen and removed, financial management and custody of the corporate seal. Again, PEI's guidance is to have it professionally prepared.
5. **File online and pay $180.** Submit the petition, memorandum and supporting material through the provincial corporate registry portal and pay the combined fee: $125 registration, $40 name search and $15 for publication.
6. **Receive your letters patent.** A completed application takes roughly 10 business days to process; registry staff typically respond to questions within 5 business days. Once granted, the letters patent bring your corporation into existence.
7. **Hold your organizational meeting.** Confirm directors and officers, adopt any additional rules, appoint an auditor (you will report the auditor's name on your annual renewal), open the bank account and start your minute book.

A note on modernization: PEI replaced the business-company side of the old Companies Act with the modern Business Corporations Act, but the non-profit provisions of Part II remain in force and unreformed — a gap Island non-profit advocates have noted compared with federal law and other provinces. Until a new statute is proclaimed, the petition-and-letters-patent process described here is the law. Check the registry's pages for updates before you file.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

PEI keeps annual compliance simple but mandatory. Every non-profit corporation must renew its registration annually through the province's online renewal service, paying a $20 fee. The renewal confirms the corporation's identity and contact information, its CRA Business Number or PEI registration number, the name of the auditor appointed at the annual meeting, and the current directors and officers, noting any changes from what is on the register.

That filing presumes the governance behind it: hold your annual meeting, elect or confirm directors, appoint the auditor and approve the financial picture before you renew. Keep minutes of members' and directors' meetings, maintain your member register, and store the letters patent and Memorandum of Agreement in a proper minute book — funders and banks ask for them regularly.

Fundamental changes — a new name, amended objects or other alterations to the letters patent — require an application to the registry rather than a simple board vote, so build lead time into any restructuring. And do not let renewals lapse: a corporation that fails to renew risks losing its good standing and, ultimately, its corporate status, which can freeze bank accounts, void grant eligibility and expose members to the very liability incorporation was meant to prevent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does PEI have a new Not-for-Profit Corporations Act?

Not yet in force. As of July 2026, PEI non-profits still incorporate under Part II of the Companies Act by petition and letters patent. The province modernized its business-company law with the Business Corporations Act, but non-profit incorporation remains under the older statute. Check the PEI Corporate Registry for any future proclamation of new legislation.

### How many directors do you need for a non-profit in PEI?

A minimum of three. PEI's official registration guidance requires at least three applicants to petition for incorporation and at least three directors for the corporation. The petition names the provisional officers and directors, and each year's renewal confirms the current directors and officers with the Corporate Registry.

### How much does it cost to register a non-profit in PEI?

The total government fee is $180, made up of $125 for registration, $40 for the name search and $15 for publication. The annual renewal costs $20 thereafter. Legal fees are extra: the province recommends having the Petition for Incorporation and Memorandum of Agreement prepared by a legal professional.

### How long does non-profit incorporation take in PEI?

Plan on roughly 10 business days for the registry to process a completed application, with initial responses to questions typically within 5 business days. Incomplete petitions, missing affidavits or name conflicts add time, so have your name search, sworn petition and Memorandum of Agreement in order before filing online.

### Do PEI non-profit corporations file an annual return?

Yes. Every non-profit corporation registered with the province must renew annually through the online renewal service. The $20 filing confirms contact details, the CRA Business Number or PEI registration number, the auditor appointed at the annual meeting, and current directors and officers. Letting renewals lapse jeopardizes the corporation's good standing.

### Can a PEI non-profit make a profit?

It can earn more than it spends in a given year, but it cannot distribute profits to members. Surpluses must be applied to the purposes in its letters patent — charitable, religious, artistic, sporting, social and similar community objects. To issue donation tax receipts, the corporation must separately register as a charity with the CRA.
