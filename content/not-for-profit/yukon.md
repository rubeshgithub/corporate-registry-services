# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit in Yukon (Societies Act Guide)

Yukon Societies Act incorporation is the standard route for turning a community group into a legal not-for-profit in the territory. Yukon's modernized *Societies Act* came into force on **April 1, 2021**, replacing the old legislation with a plain-language statute, simplified constitutions, a new member-funded society category and an online registry. Societies are administered by **Professional and Corporate Affairs** (Corporate Affairs Yukon), part of the Government of Yukon, and most filings now happen through the **Yukon Corporate Online Registry (YCOR)**.

This guide is for the people who start societies — organizers of sports clubs, arts groups, First Nations community organizations, church congregations, service clubs and would-be charities. It covers the director and incorporator minimums, the constitution and bylaws, the exact government fees, and the annual reporting that keeps a society in good standing.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Yukon

In Yukon, a not-for-profit is a **society**: a member-based organization incorporated under the *Societies Act* to pursue lawful purposes that benefit the community — sport, culture, recreation, religion, education, social services and more. A society has members rather than shareholders, and its resources must go toward its stated purposes. That non-distribution principle is what separates a society from a business corporation, which exists to generate returns for its owners.

The 2021 Act also created a second class, the **[member-funded society](https://yukon.ca/en/doing-business/businesses-societies-and-securities/find-out-about-new-member-funded-society-category)** — organizations funded primarily by their own members (think of a private club) that get lighter obligations, including a one-director minimum and no mandatory public-style financial reporting. Note that incorporation under either class is distinct from **registered charity** status, which only the Canada Revenue Agency can grant under the federal *Income Tax Act*. A Yukon society that wants to issue donation tax receipts must apply separately to the CRA after incorporation.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

Incorporation turns your group into a legal person separate from the individuals who run it. The society can sign contracts, hold a bank account, lease space and own property in its own name, and it continues to exist as volunteers come and go — no re-papering every time the board turns over. Members and directors gain the protection of **limited liability**, so a claim against the organization is not automatically a claim against the people around the table.

There are practical payoffs, too. Yukon funding bodies, national foundations and federal grant programs almost always require applicants to be incorporated, and banks will ask for your certificate of incorporation and current director list before opening an account. Incorporation is also the natural first step if you eventually want CRA charitable registration, since the CRA expects a properly constituted corporate body with clear purposes and governance.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate

1. **Create a YCOR account and reserve your name.** Yukon does not use NUANS for societies; names are reserved directly through [YCOR](https://yukon.ca/en/doing-business/businesses-societies-and-securities/how-use-yukon-corporate-online-registry) for **$40 online** (or $60 by paper form). Processing takes up to 5 business days, and the reservation holds your name for **90 days**. A society's name must include a descriptive element such as "Society," "Association," "Club," "Foundation," "Centre," "Church," "Council," "Network" or a similar term from the registry's approved list.
2. **Draft your constitution.** Under the modernized Act the constitution is deliberately short: it states the society's **name and purposes** — lawful activities that benefit the community. A member-funded society must also include the prescribed member-funded statement.
3. **Adopt bylaws.** You can adopt the government's **sample bylaws** (best-practices, basic, or member-funded versions) or draft custom bylaws that comply with section 12 of the *Societies Act*, covering membership, meetings, directors and internal governance.
4. **Line up your incorporators and first board.** You need at least **3 incorporators**. A standard society must have at least **3 directors, including at least one Yukon resident**; a member-funded society needs only one director with no residency requirement. People who are underage, bankrupt or under guardianship cannot serve as directors.
5. **Set your registered office.** Every society must maintain a physical registered office location in Yukon.
6. **File the Application for Incorporation (Form 1) and pay the fee.** File online through YCOR for **$50** — processed immediately — or on paper for **$70**, which can take up to 10 business days. Paper filings can be submitted in person at 307 Black Street in Whitehorse, by mail (Box 2703, Whitehorse, Y1A 2C6) or by email to societies@yukon.ca. You receive a certificate of incorporation once the registry accepts the application.

Full instructions live on the Government of Yukon's [Incorporate a society](https://yukon.ca/en/doing-business/businesses-societies-and-securities/yukon-societies/incorporate-society) page.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

A Yukon society's compliance year revolves around its fiscal year end. Government guidance calls for the **annual general meeting to be held within four months of fiscal year end**, and the **annual report (Form 4)** must be filed **within 30 days of the AGM — and no later than five months after fiscal year end**. Online filing costs **$25** (paper $45), and director changes made at the AGM are captured in the annual report itself, so no separate notice is needed for those. Financial statements are not attached to the annual report, but they must still be prepared and presented to members; larger "Class A" societies — income over $120,000 or assets over $250,000 — must involve an accountant in their financial reporting, subject to a limited waiver.

Between AGMs, file a Notice of Change of Directors (Form 5) or Change of Address (Form 6) when things change, and file constitution or bylaw alterations (Forms 7–9, $20–$40 each) before relying on them. The registry notes that an unfiled annual report is the most common reason a society falls **into default** — and a society in default risks losing good standing with funders and, ultimately, being dissolved. If you cannot hold your AGM on time, apply for an extension (Form 10) rather than letting the deadline slide.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many directors do you need for a society in Yukon?

A standard Yukon society needs at least three directors, and at least one of them must be a Yukon resident. A member-funded society — one funded primarily by its own members — needs only one director, with no residency requirement. Minors, undischarged bankrupts and people under guardianship cannot serve as directors.

### How much does it cost to register a society in Yukon?

Incorporation costs $50 filed online through the Yukon Corporate Online Registry or $70 on paper, plus a name reservation of $40 online or $60 by paper. Ongoing costs are modest: the annual report is $25 online, and bylaw or constitution changes run $20 to $60 depending on the filing method.

### How long does it take to incorporate a society in Yukon?

Online applications through YCOR are processed immediately once submitted with payment. Paper applications can take up to 10 business days, and name reservations up to 5 business days, with expedited service available for paper filings. Reserving your name first is required, so budget about a week end-to-end for a typical online incorporation.

### What is the new Yukon Societies Act?

Yukon's modernized Societies Act came into force on April 1, 2021. It is written in plain language, requires only the society's name and purposes in its constitution, introduces a member-funded society category, sets director eligibility rules and moved most filings onto the Yukon Corporate Online Registry. Existing societies had two years to transition.

### Does a Yukon society have to file an annual report?

Yes. Every society must hold an AGM and file an annual report within 30 days of that meeting, and no later than five months after its fiscal year end. The fee is $25 online or $45 on paper. An unfiled annual report is the most common reason societies fall into default with the registry.

### Can a Yukon society become a registered charity?

Incorporation and charitable registration are separate steps. Once incorporated under the Societies Act, a society with exclusively charitable purposes can apply to the Canada Revenue Agency for registered charity status, which allows it to issue official donation receipts. The CRA reviews the society's purposes, activities and governing documents before granting registration.
