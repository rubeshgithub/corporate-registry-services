# Chiropractic Professional Corporation in Alberta: The Complete Guide

Chiropractors are among the regulated professions Alberta allows to practise through a professional corporation, under sections 103 to 115 of the *Health Professions Act*. The tax logic is the same as for any professional practice — income taxed at corporate rates rather than top personal rates — but the compliance path has one feature that trips up first-time incorporators.

Alberta is an **endorsement-first** province. A professional corporation may only be incorporated with the **written approval and articles endorsement of the governing college** — for chiropractors, the College of Chiropractors of Alberta. You do not incorporate and then notify the college. You get the articles endorsed, and only then file.

This is the same sequencing Alberta applies to physicians, lawyers and CPAs, and it is the opposite of how Ontario works. If your advisor is used to the Ontario order, say so early.

## Key facts at a glance

| Item | Alberta rule |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | *Health Professions Act* (Alberta), **sections 103–115** |
| Corporate registry | Alberta Corporate Registry, filed through an authorized registry agent |
| Regulator & required authorization | College of Chiropractors of Alberta — professional corporation permit |
| Sequencing | Articles require the college's **written approval and endorsement before** the registry filing |
| Shareholders | Only licensed members of the same regulated profession may hold shares |
| Directors | Directors must be regulated members of the college |
| Permit | Must be obtained from the college and renewed **annually** |

## How to set up a chiropractic professional corporation in Alberta

1. **Confirm you are a regulated member in good standing** with the College of Chiropractors of Alberta.
2. **Get legal and tax advice** on the share structure before drafting.
3. **Choose a compliant name** carrying the "Professional Corporation" legal element and satisfying the college's naming rules.
4. **Prepare Articles of Incorporation** with the share and director restrictions the *Health Professions Act* requires.
5. **Submit the articles to the college for written approval and endorsement** — this precedes the registry filing.
6. **File the endorsed articles with an Alberta registry agent**, with an Alberta NUANS report and the standard notices, to receive your Certificate of Incorporation.
7. **Apply to the college for the professional corporation permit.**
8. **Renew annually** — the college permit and the corporation's registry annual return.

Step 5 before step 6 is the whole game. Reversing them means articles of amendment, a second registry fee, and weeks of delay before you can bill through the corporation.

## Step 1: Articles endorsement, then the registry

Your articles have to satisfy the *Health Professions Act* and the college's requirements — the share provisions and director restrictions below — and the college reviews and endorses them before they go anywhere near the Corporate Registry.

Once the endorsed articles are in hand, the incorporation goes through an Alberta registry agent. Alberta has no direct government counter for incorporations: the agent enters the incorporation into the Corporate Registry and issues your certificate, charging the government fee plus its own service fee. You will also need an Alberta NUANS report and the Notice of Address, Notice of Directors and Notice of Agent for Service.

We handle the registry-agent side and prepare the articles to the college's requirements so the endorsement clears first time.

## Step 2: Get your professional corporation permit

With the Certificate of Incorporation issued, apply to the College of Chiropractors of Alberta for the professional corporation permit. Sections 103–115 of the *Health Professions Act* are the operative authority: the corporation may provide chiropractic services only once it holds a valid permit.

The permit is an annual obligation, not a one-time clearance. It has to be obtained from the college and renewed every year for the corporation to keep providing services.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

Alberta's professional-corporation rules restrict both ownership and governance:

- **Shareholders** must be licensed members of the same regulated profession — for a chiropractic professional corporation, that means chiropractors regulated by the college.
- **Directors** must be regulated members of the college.

Both are continuing conditions. A change in the board or in share ownership is a compliance event, and structural changes generally have to go back through the college before the registry will accept them — the same endorsement requirement that governs the initial filing applies to later amendments.

Alberta's professional-corporation provisions have been amended over time, including in respect of who may hold shares. Confirm the current rules with your lawyer before finalising the structure, particularly if a family shareholding or holding company is contemplated.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

**College track.** The professional corporation permit renews annually with the College of Chiropractors of Alberta, alongside your own practice permit. Confirm the current corporation renewal fee with the college.

**Registry track.** The corporation must file its annual return at the Alberta Corporate Registry through a registry agent. A corporation struck for non-filing cannot hold a valid permit, so a lapse on the registry side quietly ends the college side too — and reviving a struck corporation is slower and more expensive than filing the return was.

## Why chiropractors incorporate

The tax case is the familiar one: practice income taxed at small-business corporate rates rather than top personal rates, deferring tax and giving you control over when income is drawn personally. Retained earnings can be invested inside the corporation, and depending on your circumstances there may be planning available around family shareholdings — subject to the college's share restrictions, which are narrower than for an ordinary business.

Incorporating does not limit your professional liability. Those tax numbers belong with your accountant; we describe the structure factually and handle the filings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I set up a chiropractic professional corporation in Alberta?

Prepare Articles of Incorporation with the required share and director restrictions, submit them to the College of Chiropractors of Alberta for written approval and endorsement, file the endorsed articles with an Alberta registry agent along with a NUANS report, then apply to the college for the professional corporation permit. Both the permit and the registry return renew annually.

### Can chiropractors incorporate in Alberta?

Yes. Sections 103 to 115 of the *Health Professions Act* allow chiropractors to provide professional services through a professional corporation, provided the corporation holds a valid permit from the College of Chiropractors of Alberta.

### Do I need college approval before incorporating?

Yes. A professional corporation may only be incorporated with the written approval and articles endorsement of the Alberta governing body. Incorporating first and seeking approval afterwards means amending the articles.

### Who can own shares in a chiropractic professional corporation?

Only licensed members of the same regulated profession may hold shares, and directors must be regulated members of the college. Both requirements continue for as long as the corporation holds its permit.

### Does the permit need to be renewed?

Yes — annually. The professional corporation permit must be obtained from the college and renewed each year, separately from your own practice permit and separately from the corporation's registry annual return.
