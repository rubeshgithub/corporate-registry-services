# Dental Professional Corporation in Alberta: The CDSA Guide

Alberta dentists incorporate for the same tax reasons as their colleagues elsewhere, but the province's sequencing is what separates a clean incorporation from an expensive one. Alberta is an **endorsement-first** jurisdiction: the college approves your articles *before* they are filed at the Corporate Registry, not after.

One naming note before anything else. The regulator is now the **College of Dental Surgeons of Alberta (CDSA)** — the body many guides still call the Alberta Dental Association and College. If you are reading advice that names ADA&C, check how old it is; the administration of dental professional corporations sits with the CDSA.

## Key facts at a glance

| Item | Alberta rule |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | *Health Professions Act* (Alberta), **sections 104–115** |
| Corporate registry | Alberta Corporate Registry, filed through an authorized registry agent |
| Regulator & required authorization | **College of Dental Surgeons of Alberta (CDSA)** — professional corporation permit |
| Sequencing | Articles must be **approved and endorsed by the college before** they are filed at the registry |
| Who may practise | Only a licensed member, a registered practitioner, or a professional corporation may practise dentistry |
| Voting shares | Legal **and beneficial** ownership of **all issued voting shares** vested in one or more regulated members of the college |
| Directors | **All** directors must be regulated members of the college |
| CDSA application fee | **$500.00** |
| Permit renewal | Annual — confirm the current annual fee with the CDSA |

Fees are set by the college and updated periodically. The CDSA publishes a Professional Corporations information package; we confirm current amounts at the time of filing rather than quoting a figure that may have moved.

## How to set up a dental professional corporation in Alberta

1. **Confirm eligibility.** Any registered dentist in Alberta can incorporate — the constraint is on structure, not on who may apply.
2. **Get legal and tax advice** on the share structure before drafting anything.
3. **Choose a compliant name** meeting the college's requirements and carrying the "Professional Corporation" legal element.
4. **Prepare Articles of Incorporation** with the share and director restrictions the *Health Professions Act* requires.
5. **Submit the articles to the CDSA for written approval and endorsement.** This comes *before* the registry filing — that is the step people skip.
6. **File the endorsed articles with an Alberta registry agent**, with an Alberta NUANS report and the standard notices, to receive your Certificate of Incorporation.
7. **Apply to the CDSA for the professional corporation permit** with the **$500** application fee.
8. **Renew annually** — the CDSA permit and the corporation's registry annual return.

Filing first and asking the college afterwards means articles of amendment, a second registry fee, and delay. The order is not optional.

## Step 1: Articles endorsement, then the registry

Alberta inverts the sequence used in Ontario and BC. Your articles have to satisfy the *Health Professions Act* and the college's requirements — specifically the share provisions and director restrictions below — and the CDSA reviews and endorses them before anything reaches the Corporate Registry.

Only once the endorsed articles are in hand do you go to an Alberta registry agent. Alberta has no direct government counter for incorporations: an authorized registry agent enters the incorporation and issues the certificate, charging the government fee plus its own service fee. You will also need an Alberta NUANS report and the Notice of Address, Notice of Directors and Notice of Agent for Service.

We handle the registry-agent side and prepare the articles to the CDSA's requirements so the endorsement goes through first time.

## Step 2: Get your CDSA professional corporation permit

With the Certificate of Incorporation issued, apply to the CDSA for the professional corporation permit. The **application fee is $500.00**.

Section 104–115 of the *Health Professions Act* is the operative authority: only a licensed member, a registered practitioner, or a professional corporation may engage in the practice of dentistry. Until the permit issues, the corporation is not in that list.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

Two hard requirements define an Alberta dental professional corporation:

- **All issued voting shares** must be vested — **legally and beneficially** — in one or more regulated members of the college. The "beneficially" wording matters: a nominee arrangement that puts legal title in a member's hands while the economic interest sits elsewhere does not satisfy it.
- **All directors** of the corporation must be regulated members of the college.

Both conditions are continuing, not one-time. A change in the board or in beneficial ownership is a compliance event, and structural changes generally have to go back through the college before the registry will accept them — the same endorsement logic that governs the initial filing.

Confirm the current share-class and family-shareholder rules with your lawyer before finalising the structure; Alberta's professional-corporation provisions have been amended over time.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

**CDSA track.** The professional corporation permit renews annually with the college, alongside your own annual practice permit. Confirm the current corporation renewal fee with the CDSA — the college sets it and updates it periodically.

**Registry track.** The corporation must file its annual return at the Alberta Corporate Registry through a registry agent. Let that slide long enough and the corporation is struck, which ends the college permit with it — a struck corporation cannot hold one.

## Why dentists incorporate

The tax case is the standard one: practice income taxed at small-business corporate rates rather than top personal rates, deferral, and control over when income is drawn personally. Retained earnings can be invested inside the corporation, and depending on your circumstances there may be planning available around family shareholdings — subject to the voting-share restriction above, which is stricter than many dentists expect.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I set up a dental professional corporation in Alberta?

Prepare Articles of Incorporation with the required share and director restrictions, submit them to the College of Dental Surgeons of Alberta for written approval and endorsement, then file the endorsed articles with an Alberta registry agent along with a NUANS report, and finally apply to the CDSA for the professional corporation permit with the $500 application fee.

### Do I need college approval before incorporating in Alberta?

Yes. Alberta is endorsement-first: a professional corporation may only be incorporated with the written approval and articles endorsement of the governing college. Filing at the registry first means amending the articles afterwards.

### Who can own shares in an Alberta dental professional corporation?

Legal and beneficial ownership of all issued voting shares must be vested in one or more regulated members of the college, and all directors must be regulated members. The requirement to hold shares both legally and beneficially rules out nominee arrangements.

### How much does a dental professional corporation cost in Alberta?

The CDSA application fee is $500.00, plus the Alberta registry-agent fees for the incorporation itself and any professional preparation. Annual renewal fees are set by the college — confirm the current amount with the CDSA.

### Is the regulator still called the Alberta Dental Association and College?

Dental professional corporations in Alberta are administered by the College of Dental Surgeons of Alberta. Older guides referring to the Alberta Dental Association and College predate the change, so verify any fee or process detail sourced from them.
