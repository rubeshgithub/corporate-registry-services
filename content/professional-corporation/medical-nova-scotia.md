# Medical Professional Corporation in Nova Scotia: CPSNS Permit Guide

Nova Scotia physicians incorporate for the same reasons as their colleagues elsewhere — practice income taxed at corporate rates rather than top personal rates, and the timing flexibility that comes with it. What differs is the machinery. Nova Scotia runs its professional corporations under a dedicated statute, the *Medical Professional Corporations Act*, and the corporation itself is registered not at a general corporate registry but at the **Registry of Joint Stock Companies**.

Setting up a medical professional corporation in Nova Scotia is a two-step product, and the order has one wrinkle most guides miss: you choose your name when you set the corporation up, but the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Nova Scotia (CPSNS) must give **written approval of that name** before the permit will issue. Get that sequence wrong and you are amending a corporation you have already registered.

## Key facts at a glance

| Item | Nova Scotia rule |
|---|---|
| Governing legislation | *Medical Professional Corporations Act* (Nova Scotia) and its regulations |
| Corporate registry | Registry of Joint Stock Companies |
| Regulator & required authorization | CPSNS — **medical corporation permit** |
| Name approval | Written approval from CPSNS required — request by email or fax |
| Permit application | Application for a Permit (**Form A**), with the corporate seal affixed; original only, no faxed copies |
| First-time permit fee | **$150** |
| Permit term | Calendar year — valid **January through December** |
| Renewal deadline | **December 31**; renewal opens in November |
| Renewal fee | **$100** (Nov 1–30) · **$125** with late fee (Dec 1–31) · **$225** with late + restoration fee (January) |
| Who renews | The **medical director** of the corporation |

All CPSNS figures are from the College's [medical corporation permit page](https://cpsns.ns.ca/registrants/physicians/your-practice/medical-corporation-permit/).

## How to set up a medical professional corporation in Nova Scotia

1. **Get legal and tax advice first.** CPSNS says this plainly — a lawyer or accountant should explain the conditions the *Medical Professional Corporations Act* places on your corporation before you commit to the structure.
2. **Contact the Registry of Joint Stock Companies** to set up the corporation. You choose your corporate name at this stage.
3. **Get written name approval from CPSNS** — email registration@cpsns.ns.ca or fax 902-422-5035. Do this alongside the registry step, not after the permit application.
4. **Complete the Application for a Permit (Form A)** once the Registry approves your application.
5. **Affix your corporate seal** to the application — Nova Scotia still requires it.
6. **Mail the original application** to CPSNS at Suite 400 – 175 Western Parkway, Bedford, NS B4B 0V1. The College does not accept faxed copies.
7. **Receive the permit.** CPSNS sends it to your lawyer or law firm; if you did not use one, it goes to the address you gave on Form A under Registered Office of the Corporation.
8. **Renew every year by December 31**, and separately keep the corporation itself current at the Registry of Joint Stock Companies.

One clarification worth making early: you only need this permit if you intend to **practise medicine** through the corporation. A corporation that holds assets but does not carry on practice does not need a CPSNS permit.

## Step 1: Register at the Registry of Joint Stock Companies

Nova Scotia does not use the same corporate-registry model as Ontario or BC. Your corporation is created through the Registry of Joint Stock Companies, and the name you select there is the name CPSNS will be asked to approve.

Because the name approval and the registration run in parallel rather than in sequence, the practical order is: settle on a name, put the approval request to CPSNS in writing, and register with the Registry — so that when the Registry approves your application, the College's written name approval is already in hand and Form A can go out immediately.

## Step 2: Get your CPSNS medical corporation permit

The permit application is **Form A — Application for a Permit**, a five-page form from the College. Three requirements catch people out:

- the **corporate seal must be affixed** to the application;
- the College accepts the **original only** — faxed copies are rejected outright; and
- the application goes by **mail**, to Suite 400 – 175 Western Parkway, Bedford, NS B4B 0V1.

The first-time application fee is **$150**. Once approved, CPSNS sends the permit to your lawyer or law firm, or to the registered office address you provided on Form A.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

The conditions attaching to a Nova Scotia medical professional corporation come from the *Medical Professional Corporations Act* and its [regulations](https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/medpc.htm), which require the corporation to undertake that, while its permit is in force, it will comply at all times with the obligations of a licensed physician. In practice that means the corporation is held to the same professional standards you are — incorporating does not create a shield between your practice and your professional obligations.

Because share-ownership and director rules can change by regulation, confirm the current requirements with your lawyer before finalising your share structure. CPSNS's own guidance is that legal and tax advice should come before the corporation is set up, not after.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

Two renewals, two clocks — and in Nova Scotia they are unusually easy to confuse, because **both fall in November**.

**CPSNS track.** The **medical director** of the corporation renews the medical corporation permit every year. Permits run on the calendar year, January through December. Renewal is done through the College's Registrant Services portal under "Renew Your Permit to Practise within a Corporation," and there is a Save and Pay Later option that holds your progress until the December 31 deadline.

The fee escalates sharply if you drift:

| Renewal period | Renewal fee | Late fee | Restoration fee | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| November 1–30 | $100 | — | — | **$100** |
| December 1–31 | $100 | $25 | — | **$125** |
| January | $100 | $25 | $100 | **$225** |

Miss December 31 entirely and the permit expires — at which point you cannot practise medicine under the corporation. CPSNS warns specifically that physicians whose practice income flows through the corporation **could suffer tax consequences** if the permit lapses, which is the real cost, not the $100 restoration fee.

**Registry track.** Renewing the permit with the College is a separate process from keeping the corporation current at the Registry of Joint Stock Companies. Both have to happen.

And a third thing that trips up medical directors: your **corporation permit renewal and your own licence renewal are two separate questionnaires**, both due annually in November. Completing one does not complete the other. Our [renewals guide](/professional-corporation/renewals) puts every regulator's dates in one table.

## Winding it up

If you stop needing the corporation — retirement, leaving Nova Scotia, a leave of absence — CPSNS asks you to contact the College with your name, licence number, the date you will no longer need the permit, and the reason. Do this rather than simply letting the permit lapse.

## Why physicians incorporate

The tax case is the usual one: income earned through the corporation is taxed at small-business corporate rates instead of top personal rates, which defers tax and gives you control over when income is drawn personally. Retained earnings can be invested inside the corporation, and depending on your circumstances there may be planning available around family shareholdings and the capital gains exemption on qualifying shares.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I set up a medical professional corporation in Nova Scotia?

Get legal and tax advice, contact the Registry of Joint Stock Companies to set up the corporation and choose a name, obtain written approval of that name from CPSNS, then complete the Application for a Permit (Form A), affix the corporate seal, and mail the original to the College with the $150 fee.

### How much does a medical corporation permit cost in Nova Scotia?

The first-time application fee is $150. After that the annual renewal is $100 if you renew between November 1 and 30, $125 in December once the $25 late fee applies, and $225 in January once a $100 restoration fee is added on top.

### When does a Nova Scotia medical corporation permit expire?

Permits run January through December. The renewal deadline is December 31. If the permit is not renewed by then it expires, and you cannot practise medicine under the corporation until it is restored.

### Do I need CPSNS approval of my corporation's name?

Yes. CPSNS requires written approval of the name you have chosen for the corporation. Request it by email at registration@cpsns.ns.ca or by fax at 902-422-5035, alongside setting the corporation up at the Registry of Joint Stock Companies.

### Does my licence renewal also renew my corporation permit?

No. They are two separate renewal questionnaires, both falling annually in November. As a medical director you must complete both — finishing your licence renewal does not renew the corporation permit.

### Do I need a permit if I do not practise through the corporation?

No. CPSNS is explicit that the permit is required to practise medicine within your corporation. A corporation that does not carry on practice does not need one.
