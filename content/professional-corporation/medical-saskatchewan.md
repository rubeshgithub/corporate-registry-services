# Medical Professional Corporation in Saskatchewan: The CPSS Guide

Saskatchewan physicians who incorporate do it for the familiar reason — practice income taxed at corporate rates rather than top personal rates — but the province runs its medical corporations under its own statute, *The Medical Profession Act, 1981*, and its own registry. The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Saskatchewan (CPSS) maintains the register of medical professional corporations and issues the permit that lets you actually practise through one.

Like every province, this is a two-step product: the corporation is created at the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry, and CPSS issues the permit separately. Both then renew annually, on separate tracks. What Saskatchewan adds is an unusually specific naming rule and a hard restriction on what you may do with your voting shares.

## How to set up a medical professional corporation in Saskatchewan

1. **Get legal and tax advice** on whether the structure suits your practice and how the shares should be held.
2. **Choose a compliant name.** Under the amended *Medical Profession Act*, the name must carry "Professional Corporation", "Prof. Corp." or "P.C." — the legal element is not optional and not interchangeable with "Inc." or "Ltd."
3. **Clear the name with CPSS** before you file, so the corporation you register is one the College will register on its corporations list.
4. **Incorporate at the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry**, with articles carrying the restrictions the Act requires.
5. **Apply to CPSS to register the medical professional corporation** and obtain its permit — the College's corporations unit handles this at cpssreg-corp@cps.sk.ca.
6. **Sort out billing separately.** Saskatchewan Health's Medical Services Branch handles incorporation of your billing arrangements — a different process from the College permit. Contact prss@health.gov.sk.ca or 306-798-0013.
7. **Renew annually on both tracks** — the CPSS permit and the corporation's own registry filing.

Step 6 is the one people forget. The CPSS permit makes practising through the corporation lawful; it does not redirect your billings. Both have to be arranged.

## Step 1: Incorporate at the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry

The corporation is created through the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry. Two things must be right before you file.

**The name.** *The Medical Profession Amendment Act, 2000* settled the naming requirement: the words "Professional Corporation" or the abbreviations "Prof. Corp." or "P.C." must form part of the name. A name that reads as an ordinary company — anything ending in "Inc." or "Ltd." alone — will not satisfy the Act, and fixing it after the fact means articles of amendment before CPSS will complete your registration.

**The articles.** The corporation's articles need the restrictions the Act imposes on a professional corporation, including the share provisions discussed below. Filing a template company and adjusting later is the expensive path.

## Step 2: Register with CPSS and obtain the permit

CPSS maintains the register of medical professional corporations and runs registration, permit renewal and revalidation through its corporations unit. The College's own guidance and forms are the authority on what the current application package contains; the unit can be reached at cpssreg-corp@cps.sk.ca or (306) 244-7355 during office hours.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

Saskatchewan's most distinctive rule is about control rather than ownership percentages. The Act provides that no owner of voting shares in a professional corporation may pledge those shares, or enter into a voting trust agreement, proxy, or **any other type of agreement that vests control of the voting shares in another person**. In plain terms: the physicians who hold the voting shares must actually hold the votes. Financing arrangements that take security over voting shares, or shareholder agreements that hand voting control to a non-physician, run straight into this provision.

The CPSS Regulatory Bylaws also make each member who practises "by, through or in the name of" a professional corporation personally responsible for ensuring the corporation's advertising complies with the College's rules. Incorporating does not move professional accountability off the physician.

Confirm the current share-class and family-shareholder rules with your lawyer before finalising the structure — these provisions are set by statute and bylaw and can change.

## A note on Saskatchewan's changing regulatory framework

Saskatchewan enacted *The Regulated Health Professions Act* (SS 2025, c 12), which contemplates health professional corporations regulated by each profession's council. Where a profession has transitioned, the governing rules may sit under that Act rather than the older profession-specific statute. Because the transition affects which rules apply to your corporation, we confirm the current framework with CPSS at the time of filing rather than assuming. If you are reading an older guide that cites only the 1981 Act, treat its detail as potentially dated.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

Two tracks, as everywhere.

**CPSS track.** The medical corporation permit is renewed annually through the College, which also operates a revalidation process for corporations. CPSS publishes a renewal fee, a late fee and a restoration fee — the escalation is the College's standard lever for late filers, so treat the renewal window as a hard date and confirm it in your CPSS account rather than relying on memory.

**Registry track.** The corporation must also stay current at the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry. A corporation struck for non-filing cannot hold a valid permit, which means the registry lapse quietly kills the College side too.

## Why physicians incorporate

The tax case is the standard one: income earned through the corporation is taxed at small-business corporate rates rather than top personal rates, deferring tax and letting you control when income is drawn personally. Retained earnings can be invested inside the corporation, and there may be planning available around family shareholdings — subject, in Saskatchewan, to the voting-share restrictions above.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I set up a medical professional corporation in Saskatchewan?

Choose a name containing "Professional Corporation", "Prof. Corp." or "P.C.", clear it with CPSS, incorporate at the Saskatchewan Corporate Registry with compliant articles, then apply to CPSS to register the corporation and obtain its permit. Arrange your billing separately through Saskatchewan Health's Medical Services Branch.

### What must a Saskatchewan medical professional corporation be called?

The name must include the words "Professional Corporation" or the abbreviation "Prof. Corp." or "P.C." This was settled by *The Medical Profession Amendment Act, 2000* and applies regardless of what else the name contains.

### Can I pledge my professional corporation's voting shares to a lender?

No. The Act prohibits an owner of voting shares from pledging them, or entering a voting trust, proxy or any other agreement that vests control of those shares in another person. Financing that takes security over voting shares needs to be structured around this restriction.

### How much does a CPSS medical corporation permit cost?

CPSS publishes permit, late and restoration fees in a fee schedule that is updated periodically. Rather than quote a figure that may have changed, we confirm the current amount with the College when we prepare your filing.

### Does the CPSS permit handle my billing?

No. The College permit authorises you to practise medicine through the corporation. Incorporating your billing arrangements is handled separately by Saskatchewan Health's Medical Services Branch (prss@health.gov.sk.ca, 306-798-0013).
