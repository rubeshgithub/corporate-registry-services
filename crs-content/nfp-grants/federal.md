# Federal Grants for Not-for-Profits in Canada (2026 Funding Guide)

Federal grants for nonprofits in Canada are one of the most dependable sources of project funding available to community organizations — and unlike many provincial programs, they are open to eligible not-for-profits in every province and territory. The Government of Canada funds thousands of organizations each year through departments such as Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC), Canadian Heritage, the Canada Water Agency and Women and Gender Equality Canada (WAGE), covering everything from summer staff wages to building renovations and seniors' programming.

The catch is that nearly all of these programs are application-based, run on fixed intake windows, and expect applicants to be properly constituted organizations with a board, financial records and — in most cases — incorporation. This guide walks through the major federal programs not-for-profits actually use, what they pay, when they open, and what your organization needs in place before the next call for proposals.

A few notes that matter in practice. Canada Summer Jobs remains the single most widely used federal program among small not-for-profits: in 2025 more than 27,450 employers were funded and over 75,000 jobs were created, and the program explicitly prioritizes not-for-profit and public-sector employers alongside private businesses with 50 or fewer full-time staff. The New Horizons for Seniors Program raised its community-based maximum to $50,000 for the 2026–27 call — double the previous ceiling — making it one of the most accessible first grants for a new seniors-serving organization. And the Canada Cultural Spaces Fund is in transition: it is currently accepting applications only for projects with eligible expenses before April 1, 2028, and is no longer funding new construction except in exceptional circumstances, so cultural organizations planning capital work should apply early.

Youth-serving organizations should also look at the broader [Youth Employment and Skills Strategy](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/youth-employment-strategy.html), an umbrella of 16 federal programs that fund organizations to deliver work experience, training and skills development for people aged 15 to 30. Not-for-profits are expressly eligible, and intakes range from annual (Canada Summer Jobs) to multi-year calls (the YESS Program itself).

## How to find more federal funding — the Government of Canada funding pages

Beyond the flagship programs above, the fastest way to scan what is currently open is ESDC's searchable [funding programs directory on canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/programs.html). You can filter by status (accepting applications or not), audience (not-for-profits are a dedicated category), theme, funding amount and application process, which makes it a practical first stop each quarter rather than a one-time visit. Canadian Heritage, the Canada Water Agency and WAGE each maintain their own funding pages as well, and many calls appear only for a six-to-eight-week window.

Because most federal programs can be stacked with provincial money, it is worth reviewing your provincial guide alongside this page — for example, [Ontario grants for not-for-profits](/nfp-grants/ontario) — since a federal wage subsidy plus a provincial project grant is one of the most common funding mixes for small organizations.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Almost every program in this guide names "not-for-profit organizations" as a primary eligible applicant — and federal reviewers take that phrase literally. The Canada Cultural Spaces Fund, for instance, requires applicants to be not-for-profit organizations incorporated under federal or provincial legislation and active for at least two years, and it expressly excludes unincorporated groups. Grant agreements are legal contracts: the funder needs a legal entity that can sign, carry liability insurance, hold funds in its own bank account and produce audited or reviewed financial statements.

An unincorporated community group can occasionally apply under a "trustee" arrangement, but in practice most federal calls screen out applicants without a certificate of incorporation, a registered office and a functioning board. If your group is planning to apply within the next year, incorporating now — federally under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act or in your home province — is the single most important step toward eligibility. Our guide to [federal not-for-profit incorporation](/not-for-profit/federal) explains the process.

## How to strengthen your grant application

Federal programs are competitive, and the difference between funded and declined applications usually comes down to preparation rather than mission. Start with governance: reviewers expect an active board, up-to-date bylaws and minutes, and clear signing authority — exactly the corporate records a minute book keeps in order. Build a project budget that matches the program's eligible-cost rules line by line, and where matching is required (EcoAction's 1:1 match is a good example), document cash and in-kind commitments in writing before you apply.

Describe outcomes, not just activities: state who benefits, how many, and how you will measure the change. Letters of support from partner organizations, municipalities or the community you serve consistently strengthen federal applications, as does evidence that your organization has delivered programming before — even modest programming. Finally, apply early in the window; portals slow down near deadlines, and several ESDC calls close at 3:00 p.m. Eastern, not midnight.

## Frequently asked questions

### What federal grants are available for nonprofits in Canada?

The largest recurring programs are Canada Summer Jobs (youth wage subsidies), the New Horizons for Seniors Program (up to $50,000 for community projects), the Enabling Accessibility Fund, the Social Development Partnerships Program, Canadian Heritage's cultural spaces and arts presentation funds, EcoAction freshwater funding and Women and Gender Equality Canada's programs. ESDC's funding directory on canada.ca lists current open calls.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get federal grants in Canada?

No. Most federal programs fund incorporated not-for-profit organizations whether or not they hold charitable registration with the CRA. Charitable status mainly matters for issuing donation receipts and accessing certain foundation funding. What federal programs do consistently require is incorporation, an active board and sound financial records.

### How much does Canada Summer Jobs pay a nonprofit employer?

Canada Summer Jobs provides wage subsidies so employers can hire youth aged 15 to 30 for summer positions. Not-for-profit and public-sector employers are eligible, along with private businesses with 50 or fewer full-time employees. Subsidy levels and job requirements are set out in each year's applicant guide on canada.ca, so check the current edition before budgeting.

### When does the New Horizons for Seniors Program accept applications?

The community-based stream runs one call for proposals per year. The 2026–27 call was open from June 2 to July 14, 2026, offering grants of up to $50,000 for one-year projects led by and for seniors. Eligible applicants include not-for-profit organizations, Indigenous organizations and municipalities. Watch the canada.ca program page for the next call.

### Can an unincorporated community group apply for federal funding?

Sometimes, but rarely successfully. Many federal programs — including the Canada Cultural Spaces Fund — explicitly exclude unincorporated groups, and grant agreements must be signed by a legal entity. Incorporating as a not-for-profit gives your group the legal status, bank account and governance structure federal funders require, and typically takes far less time than the grant cycle itself.
