# Grants for Not-for-Profits in New Brunswick (2026 Funding Guide)

If you run a community organization, sports club, cultural association or environmental group, there are more grants for nonprofits in New Brunswick than most first-time applicants realize. The provincial government channels community funding through the Regional Development Corporation (RDC), which reports that eleven departments — from Tourism, Heritage and Culture to Environment and Local Government — offer grant programs open to non-profit organizations, registered charities, foundations and other groups serving a public benefit ([GNB grant funding directory](https://www2.gnb.ca/content/gnb/en/corporate/promo/grant-funding.html)).

Provincial money is only part of the picture. New Brunswick not-for-profits also draw on federal programs such as Canada Summer Jobs and the New Horizons for Seniors Program, on arm's-length funders like the New Brunswick Arts Board (artsnb), and on community foundations and United Way chapters. The catch, in almost every case: funders want to see an incorporated organization with a functioning board before they sign a cheque. This guide walks through the major provincial programs we verified in July 2026, recent funding announcements, and what your organization needs in place before the next intake deadline.

The **Community Investment Fund** deserves a closer look because it is purpose-built for the kind of organization most of our readers run. Eligible applicants are not-for-profit incorporated organizations, incorporated charitable organizations, municipalities, regional service commissions and First Nations communities ([CIF guidelines](https://www2.gnb.ca/content/dam/gnb/Departments/rdc-sdr/pdf/CommunityInvestmentFundGuidelines.pdf)). Note the word *incorporated* — an unincorporated committee cannot apply. Community Growth Initiative contributions up to $5,000 need no matching funds, which makes the CIF a realistic first grant for a newly incorporated group.

The **Environmental Trust Fund** is open to non-profit organizations, local governments, First Nations and institutions furthering sustainable development. Multi-year projects must reapply each year, and funding covers the April-to-March fiscal year. If you missed the November 30, 2025 deadline for 2026–27 money, use the coming months to prepare for the next fall call.

On the arts side, artsnb's project programs (Creation, Documentation, Career Development, Artist in Residence and Equinox) fund individual professional artists, with deadlines spread across the year ([artsnb deadlines](https://artsnb.ca/web/grants/deadlines/)). Arts *organizations* now apply to artsnb's Operational Support Program — a significant change made in 2026, covered below. Project-based and sector-development funding for organizations remains with the Department of Tourism, Heritage and Culture.

## Federal grants New Brunswick not-for-profits can also access

Federal programs are often the largest single source of funding for smaller New Brunswick organizations. [Canada Summer Jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/canada-summer-jobs.html) subsidizes summer wages for youth employees, with employer applications typically opening in late fall for the following summer. The [New Horizons for Seniors Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/new-horizons-seniors-community-based.html) funds community-based projects led by or for seniors — the June 2026 call for proposals accepted requests up to $50,000 and closed July 14, 2026, so watch for the next annual call. The [Enabling Accessibility Fund](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/enabling-accessibility-fund-small-projects.html) helps non-profits pay for ramps, accessible washrooms and similar retrofits through its small projects component. See our full guide to [federal grants for not-for-profits](/nfp-grants/federal) for eligibility details and current intakes.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Read the fine print of any program above and a pattern emerges. The CIF requires applicants to be *incorporated* not-for-profit or charitable organizations. artsnb's Operational Support Program states plainly that non-incorporated collectives are ineligible — applicants must be incorporated non-profits. The ETF and RDC funds likewise deal with organizations, not individuals.

Incorporation is what turns your group into a legal entity that can sign a funding agreement, open a bank account to receive the money, and be held accountable for reporting. Many funders go further and ask for evidence of governance: a board of directors, bylaws, financial statements, and often a year or more of operating history. If your organization is still an informal committee, incorporating under New Brunswick's Companies Act (or federally) is the practical first step toward every program on this page — start with our guide to [incorporating a not-for-profit in New Brunswick](/not-for-profit/new-brunswick).

## How to strengthen your grant application

Grant reviewers in a small province read a lot of applications from similar organizations, so the fundamentals separate winners from the rest. Match your project to the program's stated priorities — an ETF application should name one or two of the fund's three priority areas and speak directly to them. Build a realistic budget that shows exactly how the grant plus your own contribution covers costs; programs like the CIF publish their cost-share rules, and a budget that ignores them signals inexperience.

Demonstrate governance. List your board members and their roles, attach your most recent financial statements, and reference your certificate of incorporation. Describe measurable outcomes — how many participants, what change, measured how — rather than vague community benefit. Finally, collect letters of support from municipalities, partner organizations or user groups; for community infrastructure and events funding, evidence that the community actually wants the project carries real weight. Submit early: several RDC programs allocate money on an ongoing basis with per-riding caps, so late-fiscal-year applications can find the budget already committed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in New Brunswick?

The main provincial programs are the Regional Development Corporation's Community Investment Fund (up to $20,000 for community projects, up to $10,000 for events), the Community Development Fund and Rural Economy Fund, the Environmental Trust Fund for environmental projects, and artsnb's Operational Support Program for incorporated arts organizations (up to $400,000, tiered by revenue).

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in New Brunswick?

No. Most New Brunswick programs — including the Community Investment Fund and the Environmental Trust Fund — accept incorporated not-for-profit organizations that are not registered charities. Charitable registration with the CRA matters mainly for issuing donation tax receipts and for some private foundation funding, not for provincial government grants.

### How much can a nonprofit get from the Community Investment Fund?

The Community Growth Initiative stream provides $500 to $20,000 per project; contributions up to $5,000 require no matching funds, while amounts above $5,000 are reimbursed at 50% of eligible costs. The Community Events & Festivals stream provides $500 to $10,000 depending on your event budget, with $10,000 available for events over $75,001 in eligible expenses.

### When is the Environmental Trust Fund deadline?

The ETF runs one intake per year in the fall. For 2026–27 funding, applications opened October 31, 2025 and closed November 30, 2025. Watch the Department of Environment and Local Government's ETF page in the fall for the next call, and remember that multi-year projects must reapply annually.

### Can an unincorporated community group apply for these grants?

Generally no. The Community Investment Fund lists incorporated not-for-profit and charitable organizations as eligible applicants, and artsnb's Operational Support Program explicitly excludes non-incorporated collectives. Incorporating provincially or federally before you apply gives your group the legal status funders require to sign agreements and receive payment.
