# Grants for Not-for-Profits in the Northwest Territories (2026 Funding Guide)

Grants for nonprofits in the Northwest Territories come almost entirely from the Government of the Northwest Territories (GNWT), spread across several departments that each run their own contribution programs. Executive and Indigenous Affairs stabilizes the NGO sector itself; Municipal and Community Affairs (MACA) funds youth and volunteer programming; Education, Culture and Employment (ECE) delivers arts funding on the advice of the NWT Arts Council; Health and Social Services runs the Anti-Poverty Fund; and the Department of Justice supports victim services and community justice work.

For founders of community organizations, the practical takeaway is that the GNWT writes cheques to incorporated bodies — most programs require an organization registered and in good standing under the NWT Societies Act (or an equivalent statute). This guide covers the major territorial programs with amounts and deadlines verified in July 2026, recent funding announcements, and how to position a new organization to qualify.

Two of these deserve extra context. The NGO Stabilization Fund exists precisely because northern non-profits run lean: it pays for the unglamorous backbone — strategic plans, board training, core operations — that program-restricted funding never covers. Applicants must deliver critical GNWT-funded services and be registered and in good standing in the NWT as a society or association (or created under NWT or federal statute), and demand routinely exceeds the budget, so partial awards are common.

On the arts side, the GNWT announced a redesign of its arts funding in April 2026: the Small Arts Grant now accepts applications year-round on a first-come basis, the Medium Arts Projects Grant moved to three intakes for 2026–27 (May 1, June 30 and October 31, 2026), the Large Arts Fund and Arts Operating Fund closed May 1, 2026, and two new streams — the Artist Travel and Touring Fund and the Arts Business Support Fund — accept applications year-round ([GNWT newsroom, April 17, 2026](https://www.gov.nt.ca/en/newsroom/revamped-nwt-arts-programs-better-fit-artists-applications-open-now)). The [NWT Arts Council](https://www.eia.gov.nt.ca/en/northwest-territories-arts-council) continues to advise the minister on arts funding awards. Because deadlines shifted with the redesign, always confirm the current intake dates on the ECE program pages before planning an application.

MACA also runs smaller supports around volunteerism — including Volunteer Recognition Funding of up to $1,000 per year, though that stream flows through community governments rather than directly to non-profits. Organizations working in restorative justice can contact the Department of Justice's [Community Justice division](https://www.justice.gov.nt.ca/en/community-justice-committees/), which supports community justice committees across the territory.

## Federal grants NWT not-for-profits can also access

Federal programs stack well on top of GNWT funding. [Canada Summer Jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/canada-summer-jobs.html) reimburses not-for-profit employers up to 100% of the territorial adult minimum hourly wage plus mandatory employment-related costs — double the rate available to private-sector employers. The [New Horizons for Seniors Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/new-horizons-seniors-community-based.html) offers community-based project grants of up to $50,000; the 2026 call for proposals ran June 2 to July 14, 2026, with calls typically issued annually. National arts, environment and heritage funds are also open to NWT applicants. See the full rundown in our [federal grants guide](/nfp-grants/federal).

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Read the fine print on any GNWT program and the same phrase recurs: "registered and in good standing." The NGO Stabilization Fund requires registration as a society or association in the NWT. The Arts Operating Fund goes further — organizations must be registered and in good standing under the NWT Societies Act and have operated for at least a year before the deadline, with staff and a delivery track record. MACA's youth programs are open to community governments and registered non-profit organizations, not informal groups.

That makes incorporation the first fundable milestone for any new NWT organization. A society gives the GNWT a legal counterparty for its contribution agreements: a registered name, a governing board, a registered office and annual filings that prove ongoing compliance. Fall out of good standing — miss an annual return, let your registration lapse — and you can become ineligible mid-stream. If your group is still unincorporated, start with our guide to [incorporating a not-for-profit in the Northwest Territories](/not-for-profit/northwest-territories); if you incorporate now, you can meet the one-year operating history several programs expect by the time next year's intakes open.

## How to strengthen your grant application

NWT funding programs are administered by small teams serving 33 communities, and several — the NGO Stabilization Fund and RYSE among them — are consistently oversubscribed. Applications that stand out do three things well. First, they demonstrate governance: current corporate filings, a board that has formally approved the application, and financial statements that show money is tracked properly. Second, they build honest budgets. MACA's youth funding covers a percentage of eligible costs, so show your own contribution and get real quotes for northern prices, including travel, which reviewers know dominates project costs in small communities.

Third, they prove community backing. A letter of support from your community government is mandatory for some programs (youth centres run by organizations other than the community government need one) and persuasive everywhere else — as is partnership with Indigenous governments, a formal requirement for NGO applicants to the Anti-Poverty Fund. Finally, report well on every grant you receive: outstanding reports to a GNWT department are one of the most common reasons otherwise-eligible organizations are turned away the following year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in the Northwest Territories?

Core GNWT programs include the NGO Stabilization Fund (up to $50,000), the Anti-Poverty Fund ($1.75 million annual pool), MACA's Youth Contributions Program (up to $5,000) and Youth Centres Initiative (up to $25,000), ECE arts grants (up to $10,000 per project intake; up to $200,000 in operating funding), and the Justice department's Victims Assistance Fund.

### What is the NGO Stabilization Fund?

It is a GNWT fund providing up to $50,000 per year to non-governmental organizations that deliver critical GNWT-funded services — covering one-time capacity costs like strategic planning, governance training and core operations. Applicants must be registered and in good standing in the NWT as a society or association. The most recent intake closed February 13, 2026.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in the NWT?

No. GNWT programs fund incorporated not-for-profits — societies and associations in good standing — without requiring CRA charitable registration. Charitable status matters mainly for issuing tax receipts to donors and for some foundation funding. What the GNWT checks is territorial registration, good standing and, often, a year of operating history.

### How much youth funding can an NWT organization get from MACA?

The Youth Contributions Program funds up to 75% of eligible expenses to a maximum of $5,000 for projects inside the NWT (up to $4,000 outside), for projects benefiting five or more youth. Organizations operating youth centres can separately receive up to $25,000 per fiscal year through the Youth Centres Initiative.

### When are NWT arts funding deadlines?

Following the April 2026 program redesign, the Small Arts Grant accepts applications year-round; Medium Arts Projects Grant intakes for 2026–27 are May 1, June 30 and October 31, 2026; and the Large Arts Fund and Arts Operating Fund closed May 1, 2026. Check ECE's program pages for each year's confirmed dates.
