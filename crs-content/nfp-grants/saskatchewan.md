# Grants for Nonprofits in Saskatchewan (2026 Funding Guide)

Saskatchewan has an unusually community-minded funding system: much of the money available to local organizations comes from gaming and lottery revenue that the province deliberately routes back into sport, culture, recreation and social programs. If you are looking for grants for nonprofits in Saskatchewan, the Community Initiatives Fund (CIF) — financed by SaskGaming casino revenues — is the flagship, distributing millions of dollars every year to hundreds of community projects. Beside it sit the Sask Lotteries Trust Fund programs delivered through SaskCulture, Sask Sport and the Saskatchewan Parks and Recreation Association, plus SK Arts (the Saskatchewan Arts Board's successor) for arts organizations.

The province also runs practical, targeted funding — most famously the Community Rink Affordability Grant, which was doubled for 2026. Federal programs layer on top. This guide gathers the verified amounts, deadlines and official links for the major programs, along with recent 2025 and 2026 funding announcements.

The CIF deserves special attention because of its scale and reliability. Created under The Lotteries and Gaming Saskatchewan Corporation Act, it receives a dedicated share of SaskGaming casino revenue — typically $8 million to $9 million a year — and is governed by a volunteer board. Its Community Grant Program accepts applications from incorporated Saskatchewan non-profits (as well as municipalities, schools and health entities delivering community-led programming), and organizations may hold up to two CIF grants per year for different programs. Programs must start no sooner than two months after the deadline and finish within twelve months.

For arts organizations, note that the Saskatchewan Arts Board now operates as SK Arts. Its Professional Arts Organizations Program runs in three streams (Lite, Core Support and Sector Development), each with its own eligibility rules and guidelines available through the online application portal.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Saskatchewan's funders are explicit about legal status. The CIF Community Grant Program requires applicants to be incorporated Saskatchewan non-profit organizations (or public bodies such as municipalities and schools delivering community-led programs), in good standing with no outstanding reports. The Sask Lotteries Community Grant Program funds non-profit community organizations operated by volunteers, the Community Rink Affordability Grant names "registered non-profit groups" among eligible applicants, and SK Arts' organization programs fund established Saskatchewan-based arts organizations.

In other words, incorporation is the entry ticket. It gives your group the legal name that appears on the grant agreement, a board that can be held accountable, and the governance records — bylaws, minutes, financial statements, annual returns — that "good standing" clauses actually test. Groups that incorporate under Saskatchewan's non-profit legislation (or federally) and keep their filings current can apply to every program on this page; unincorporated committees generally cannot, unless a municipality or incorporated partner applies on their behalf.

## How to strengthen your grant application

Saskatchewan's community funders read hundreds of applications from small volunteer organizations, so clarity wins. Anchor your application to the funder's stated themes — CIF, for instance, funds work under healthy child and youth development, individual and community wellbeing, and nonprofit leadership — and say plainly which theme you fit and why.

Budget honestly and completely. Show all revenue sources, note any other lottery or provincial funding, and remember CIF's timing rules: programs cannot start until at least two months after the deadline and must finish within a year. If your grant request exceeds $2,000 under the Sask Lotteries Community Grant Program, demonstrate how funds reach under-represented groups such as Indigenous people, seniors, persons with disabilities or low-income families. Letters of support from schools, municipalities or partner organizations carry weight in small communities, and a clean track record — final reports filed, no outstanding repayments — is a hard requirement, not a nicety. Finally, build a deadline calendar: February 1 (CIF Summer), April 1 and October 1 (CIF Community), and program-specific arts deadlines through the year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in Saskatchewan?

The Community Initiatives Fund is the flagship, offering Community Grants of up to $30,000 (local) or $50,000 (provincial) and a Summer Grant stream for camps and day programs. Sask Lotteries funding flows through SaskCulture, Sask Sport and SPRA, SK Arts funds arts organizations, and the province runs targeted programs like the $5,000 Community Rink Affordability Grant.

### When are the CIF grant deadlines?

The Community Grant Program has two deadlines each year: April 1 and October 1 at 4:00 p.m. The Summer Grant Program closes February 1 for programs running that summer. Funded programs must begin no sooner than two months after the deadline and be completed within twelve months, so plan your program calendar around the intake you choose.

### Where does Saskatchewan lottery and casino grant money come from?

Two parallel systems fund community organizations. The Community Initiatives Fund receives a dedicated portion of SaskGaming casino revenue — typically $8 million to $9 million a year — under The Lotteries and Gaming Saskatchewan Corporation Act. Separately, the Sask Lotteries Trust Fund for Sport, Culture and Recreation distributes lottery proceeds through Sask Sport, SaskCulture and SPRA.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in Saskatchewan?

No. The key requirement across CIF, Sask Lotteries and SK Arts programs is incorporated non-profit status and good standing, not CRA charitable registration. Charitable status matters if you want to issue donation receipts or approach private foundations, but most provincial and lottery-funded programs in Saskatchewan fund incorporated non-profits directly.

### Can a sports club or rink get provincial funding in Saskatchewan?

Yes. Volunteer-run sport organizations are core clients of the Sask Lotteries Community Grant Program, and facilities with indoor ice surfaces — including those run by registered non-profit groups, municipalities, First Nations and schools — can apply for the Community Rink Affordability Grant, doubled to $5,000 per rink for 2026 with an application window early each year.
