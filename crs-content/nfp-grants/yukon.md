# Grants for Not-for-Profits in Yukon (2026 Funding Guide)

For a territory of roughly 45,000 people, Yukon offers one of the most accessible grant environments in Canada. Grants for nonprofits in Yukon flow from three main directions: the Government of Yukon, whose flagship Community Development Fund supports everything from festival planning to facility upgrades; Lotteries Yukon, which returns lottery proceeds to arts, sport and recreation groups; and federal programs that Yukon organizations can access alongside every other Canadian not-for-profit.

The common thread is that almost every funder expects to see an incorporated organization on the application — typically a society registered under Yukon's Societies Act or a federal not-for-profit corporation based in the territory. This guide walks through the major territorial programs, current amounts and deadlines (verified July 2026), recent award announcements, and what your organization needs in place before you apply.

The Community Development Fund deserves special attention because of its breadth. Eligible applicants include registered non-profit societies, not-for-profit corporations under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act based in Yukon, charitable organizations, and municipal and First Nations governments. Applications must arrive by 4:30 pm on the deadline date, and the three-tier structure means a small community event and a major capital project apply through the same fund on different timelines.

Lotteries Yukon's Projects Fund has its own gatekeeping worth planning for: applicants must be registered Yukon non-profits that have operated for at least one year, held their first annual general meeting, and remain in compliance with the Societies Act with no outstanding reports or debts to Lotteries Yukon.

## Federal grants Yukon not-for-profits can also access

Territorial money is only half the picture. [Canada Summer Jobs](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/canada-summer-jobs.html) reimburses not-for-profit employers up to 100% of the territorial adult minimum hourly wage, plus mandatory employment-related costs — a significant advantage over the 50% rate offered to private and public sector employers. The [New Horizons for Seniors Program](https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/funding/new-horizons-seniors-community-based.html) funds community-based projects led by or for seniors up to $50,000; its 2026 call for proposals ran from June 2 to July 14, 2026, and new calls are typically announced annually. Yukon organizations also compete well in national arts, heritage and environment programs. See our full guide to [federal grants for not-for-profits](/nfp-grants/federal) for the complete list.

## Who qualifies — and why incorporation matters

Look closely at the eligibility rules above and a pattern emerges. The Community Development Fund names registered non-profit societies and Yukon-based federal not-for-profit corporations as eligible applicants. Lotteries Yukon goes further, requiring at least one year of operation, a completed first AGM and ongoing Societies Act compliance. Unincorporated community groups are generally shut out of the largest funding streams, and even where an individual can apply (as with parts of the Youth Investment Fund or Culture Quest), organizational applicants tend to pursue larger, multi-year work.

Incorporation is therefore not paperwork for its own sake — it is the entry ticket. A society registered in Yukon (or a federal not-for-profit corporation) gives funders what they need to sign a contribution agreement: a legal entity that can hold funds, a board accountable for spending, and annual filings that prove the organization is alive and in good standing. If you are still operating as an informal committee, the most valuable grant-readiness step you can take this year is to [incorporate a not-for-profit in Yukon](/not-for-profit/yukon) — ideally a full year before you want to apply to Lotteries Yukon, so the one-year operating requirement is behind you.

## How to strengthen your grant application

Yukon's funders are small teams who often know the applicant community well, which cuts both ways: sloppy applications are remembered, and well-run organizations build a track record. Start with governance. Make sure your society's filings are current, your board minutes record the decision to apply, and your bylaws match what you tell the funder about how decisions are made.

Build the budget from real quotes rather than round numbers, and show your own contribution — the CDF covers up to 90% of costs and the Arts Fund up to 70%, so reviewers will look for the remainder in cash, volunteer time or community partnerships. Describe outcomes in measurable terms: how many participants, what changes for them, and how the community benefits after the funding ends. Letters of support from First Nations governments, municipalities or partner organizations carry real weight in a small territory. Finally, respect the clock — Yukon deadlines are firm (4:30 pm for CDF and Arts Fund intakes), and late submissions are not accepted.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants are available for nonprofits in Yukon?

The main territorial programs are the Community Development Fund (three tiers, from under $20,000 to over $75,000), the Yukon Arts Fund ($500,000 pool annually), Culture Quest (up to $10,000), the Youth Investment Fund (up to $5,000), and Lotteries Yukon's Projects Fund and Travel Assistance Program. Federal programs such as Canada Summer Jobs add wage funding on top.

### How much can we get from the Yukon Community Development Fund?

The CDF funds up to 90% of total project or event costs across three tiers: Tier 1 for projects of $20,000 or less, Tier 2 for $20,001 to $75,000, and Tier 3 for projects over $75,000. Tier 1 has four intakes a year; Tier 3 has a single January 15 deadline.

### Do you need to be a registered charity to get grants in Yukon?

No. Most Yukon programs fund incorporated not-for-profits — registered societies or federal not-for-profit corporations — without requiring CRA charitable registration. Charitable status mainly matters for issuing donation receipts and accessing certain foundation funding, not for territorial programs like the CDF or Lotteries Yukon funds.

### Who can apply for Lotteries Yukon funding?

The Projects Fund is open to registered Yukon non-profits that have operated for at least one year, held their first annual general meeting, comply with the Societies Act, and have no outstanding reports or debts to Lotteries Yukon. Individuals and governments are not eligible. Projects must support arts, sport or recreation.

### Can a brand-new non-profit get funding in Yukon?

A newly registered society can apply to the Community Development Fund and Youth Investment Fund right away, but Lotteries Yukon requires one year of operation and a completed AGM first. Incorporating early — before you need the money — starts that clock and builds the filing history funders check.

### When are the Community Development Fund deadlines?

Tier 1 (projects of $20,000 or less) closes January 15, May 15, July 15 and October 15. Tier 2 ($20,001–$75,000) closes April 15 and September 15. Tier 3 (over $75,000) closes January 15. Applications are due by 4:30 pm, rolling to the next business day if the date falls on a weekend or holiday.
