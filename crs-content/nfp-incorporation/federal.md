# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit Under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act (Federal Guide)

The Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act (CNCA, often called the NFP Act) is the federal law that governs not-for-profit corporations across Canada. When you incorporate under the CNCA, you create a corporation without share capital whose profits must be applied to its purposes rather than paid out to members — and you do it through Corporations Canada, the federal registry operated by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED).

Federal incorporation suits organizations that plan to operate in more than one province, want nationwide name protection, or expect to apply for charitable registration with the Canada Revenue Agency later. This guide is written for first-time incorporators — community groups, sports associations, cultural and religious organizations, and prospective charities — and walks through every form, fee, and filing.

## What counts as a not-for-profit under federal law

A federal not-for-profit is a corporation without share capital created under the CNCA. Unlike a business corporation, it has members rather than shareholders, and it operates under a non-distribution constraint: any surplus must advance its purposes, not be distributed to members, directors, or officers. A CNCA corporation can still earn revenue — memberships, ticket sales, sponsorships, services — as long as the money serves the mission.

The CNCA draws one distinction that matters from day one. A **soliciting corporation** is one that has received more than $10,000 in income from public sources — donations from non-members, government grants, or funds from other entities that themselves received public money — in a single financial year. Soliciting corporations face stricter rules, including a minimum of three directors (at least two of whom are not officers or employees of the corporation or its affiliates) and an obligation to file financial statements with Corporations Canada.

It is also important not to confuse a not-for-profit corporation with a **registered charity**. Incorporating under the CNCA does not let you issue donation tax receipts. Charitable status is a separate registration with the Canada Revenue Agency, granted only to organizations with exclusively charitable purposes. Many founders incorporate federally first, then apply to the CRA — and the wording of your articles matters enormously for that second step.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

An unincorporated association is legally just a group of people, which means the people themselves can be on the hook for its debts and contracts. Incorporation creates a separate legal person: members get **limited liability** protection, and the organization gains **perpetual existence**, carrying on as volunteers and board members come and go.

Practical doors open too. An incorporated not-for-profit can sign leases, hold property, and open a bank account in its own name. Most government grant programs and foundations require applicants to be incorporated, and many ask for your certificate of incorporation and corporate records with the application. And if charitable registration is in your future, federal incorporation under the CNCA is a common, well-understood foundation for the CRA application.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate

1. **Choose your corporate name.** You can incorporate with a word name or a numbered name. If you file online, Corporations Canada's incorporation service includes an integrated name search, so a separate [Nuans](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/nuans-corporate-name-trademark-reports/en/nuansr-corporate-name-and-trademark-reports) report is not required for online incorporation. If you want certainty before you file, you can request a [corporate name pre-approval](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/naming-corporation/naming-corporation-how-get-name), which is valid for 90 days. Keep in mind that a Nuans search itself is only valid for 90 days and does not guarantee approval.

2. **Prepare your articles of incorporation (Form 4001).** The articles state your corporate name, the province or territory of the registered office, the classes of members, the number of directors (fixed or a range), a statement of purpose, and the rules for distributing property on dissolution. Planning to seek charitable status later? Draft the purposes and dissolution clause with CRA requirements in mind.

3. **Confirm your first board of directors.** One director is enough for a non-soliciting corporation, but if you expect more than $10,000 a year in public donations or government grants, plan for at least three directors, with at least two who are not officers or employees. Directors must be individuals at least 18 years old, not bankrupt, and not incapable — there is no Canadian residency requirement.

4. **Set your initial registered office and first directors (Form 4002).** This form records the registered office address where official documents can be sent and the names and addresses of your first board. Filed online, it is part of the same incorporation application.

5. **File through the Corporations Canada Online Filing Centre.** Online incorporation costs **$200** and is normally processed in **one business day**; a four-hour express service is available for an extra $100. Filing by email or mail costs more and takes considerably longer, so nearly everyone files online. You can [start your incorporation online](https://www.ic.gc.ca/app/scr/cc/CorporationsCanada/bs/crp-wz.html?lang=eng) directly.

6. **Receive your certificate of incorporation.** Corporations Canada issues a certificate of incorporation with your corporation number. From there, organize your first board meeting, adopt by-laws, admit members, and set up your minute book — the by-laws must be filed with Corporations Canada within 12 months of member confirmation.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

Every CNCA corporation must file an **annual return (Form 4022)** with Corporations Canada within **60 days of its anniversary date**, at a cost of $12 online. This is a corporate filing, separate from any CRA tax or information return. Corporations Canada actively dissolves corporations that are in default of their annual return filings — an administrative dissolution program for non-compliant not-for-profits has been running since July 2023 — so a missed filing can literally end your organization.

Beyond the annual return, you must hold an annual meeting of members, prepare financial statements, and keep your corporate records current: changes of registered office, changes of directors, and amendments to your articles all require filings. Soliciting corporations must also send their financial statements (and public accountant's report, where required) to Corporations Canada, with the level of review — from a review engagement to a full audit — depending on gross annual revenues. A well-maintained minute book with registers of members and directors is not just good practice; funders and banks routinely ask to see it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many directors do I need for a federal not-for-profit in Canada?

The CNCA requires only one director for a non-soliciting corporation. However, if your organization receives more than $10,000 in a year from public sources — donations from non-members or government grants — it becomes a soliciting corporation and must have at least three directors, at least two of whom are not officers or employees.

### How much does it cost to incorporate a not-for-profit federally?

Corporations Canada charges $200 to incorporate online under the Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act, with standard processing in one business day. A four-hour express service costs an additional $100. After incorporation, budget $12 per year for the online annual return. A Nuans report or name pre-approval may add a small extra cost.

### Do I need a NUANS report to incorporate a federal not-for-profit?

Not for online incorporation — Corporations Canada's online service includes an integrated name search. A Nuans report, valid for 90 days, is still used for some filings such as amalgamations and continuances, and you can optionally request a corporate name pre-approval (also valid for 90 days) before filing your articles.

### Do federal not-for-profit directors have to live in Canada?

No. The Canada Not-for-profit Corporations Act sets no Canadian residency requirement for directors. Directors must simply be individuals who are at least 18 years old, are not bankrupt, and are not legally incapable. This makes federal incorporation attractive for organizations with international boards or directors based outside Canada.

### Can a not-for-profit make a profit in Canada?

Yes. A not-for-profit corporation can earn surpluses from memberships, events, services, or sponsorships. What it cannot do is distribute those profits to its members, directors, or officers — every dollar must be applied to the organization's purposes. That non-distribution constraint is what separates a not-for-profit from a business corporation.

### What is the annual return for a federal not-for-profit?

It is Form 4022, a corporate filing made with Corporations Canada within 60 days of your corporation's anniversary date each year. Filing online costs $12. It is not a tax return — it simply confirms your corporate information. Corporations that fail to file risk administrative dissolution by Corporations Canada.
