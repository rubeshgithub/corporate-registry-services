# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit in Quebec (Companies Act, Part III Guide)

Learning how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Quebec means getting to know a distinctive legal vehicle: the **non-profit legal person** (personne morale sans but lucratif), constituted under Part III of the [Companies Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-38) (CQLR, c. C-38). Rather than filing articles as in most provinces, Quebec founders apply to the government for **letters patent** — a charter issued by the Registraire des entreprises that brings the organization to life for purposes of a national, patriotic, religious, charitable, scientific, artistic, social, professional, athletic or sporting nature, without pecuniary gain to members.

The process is administered by the [Registraire des entreprises du Québec](https://www.registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca/en/demarrer/constituer-pmsbl.aspx), which also maintains the enterprise register where your organization's public information will live. This guide walks first-time incorporators — community groups, cultural associations, sports clubs and future charities — through the founders, forms, fees and French-language rules involved.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Quebec

Quebec's Part III entity is formally a "legal person without share capital." It has members instead of shareholders, and section 218 of the Companies Act limits its objects to non-profit purposes — national, patriotic, religious, philanthropic, charitable, scientific, artistic, social, professional, athletic, sporting or similar aims. The defining constraint is the absence of pecuniary gain: surpluses belong to the organization and must be devoted to its objects, never distributed to members as profit.

Two distinctions matter for planning. First, a Quebec non-profit legal person is not the same as a **registered charity**. Charitable registration is granted separately by the Canada Revenue Agency (with Revenu Québec recognition for provincial tax purposes), and only registered charities can issue official donation receipts. Second, the non-profit legal person differs fundamentally from a business corporation under Quebec's Business Corporations Act: there are no shares, no dividends and no capital appreciation — membership confers voting and participation rights, not ownership.

One more Quebec-specific rule deserves emphasis at the naming stage: under the [Charter of the French Language, an enterprise's name must be in French](https://www.quebec.ca/en/businesses-and-self-employed-workers/start-entreprise/choose-entreprise-name/definition-rules). If your constituting documents do not include a French version of the name, you must declare a French name in the enterprise register; versions in other languages can be registered as "other names used in Québec," but the official name must be French-compliant.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

Until letters patent issue, an association is legally its members — and those members can be personally responsible for the group's leases, debts and commitments. Constitution as a legal person changes that: the organization itself contracts, borrows, sues and is sued, holds property and opens bank accounts, insulating volunteers from most personal liability. It also gives the organization perpetual existence independent of its founders, so programs, assets and agreements survive board turnover.

Funders take incorporation seriously. Quebec ministries, municipalities, foundations and federal programs alike generally require an incorporated entity — with an NEQ (Quebec enterprise number) on the register — before releasing grant money. Banks require the letters patent and register information to open an account in the organization's name. And if your long-term plan includes CRA charitable registration, constituting first under Part III with clearly charitable objects makes that application substantially easier.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Quebec

1. **Choose a French-compliant name and check availability.** Search the enterprise register to confirm your proposed name is not already in use, and make sure it satisfies the Charter of the French Language and the Registraire's naming rules. You may reserve the name for 90 days ($27 in 2026) before filing.
2. **Gather your founders — at least three.** Section 218 of the Companies Act requires "any number of persons, not less than three" to apply for constitution. Founders typically become the first directors, and the board itself must have no fewer than three members.
3. **Complete the application for constitution (form RE-303).** The Demande de constitution en personne morale sans but lucratif sets out the proposed name, the organization's objects, the registered office judicial district and details of the applicants. It is accompanied by a sworn declaration and your name search or reservation confirmation. The form is filed in French, consistent with Quebec's language requirements.
4. **File with the Registraire des entreprises and pay the fee.** Submit the application with the 2026 fee of $199 (regular) or $298.50 (priority). The Registraire examines the application and, if satisfied, issues **letters patent** constituting your organization.
5. **Receive your letters patent and NEQ.** Constitution automatically registers your organization in the enterprise register and assigns your Quebec enterprise number (NEQ), which you will use in all government dealings.
6. **File the initial declaration within 60 days.** The initial declaration completes your public file — directors, officers, establishments and activity. Filed within the 60-day window, it is free ($20.50 for priority processing). Then hold your organizational meeting to adopt by-laws, appoint officers and admit members.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

Quebec's recurring obligation is the **annual updating declaration**, which confirms or corrects the information on your public file — name, registered office, directors and officers, establishments. For legal persons, the filing window runs from **May 15 to November 15** each year; filed within that period, the declaration itself carries no fee, and the organization pays the **annual registration fee** ($41 for non-profit legal persons in 2026). Filing late attracts additional fees and exposes the organization to penal sanctions.

The consequence of persistent neglect is existential: an enterprise that fails to file two consecutive annual updating declarations may have its registration cancelled ex officio by the Registraire — and for a non-profit legal person, that cancellation results in **dissolution**. Beyond the annual cycle, changes during the year (new directors, a moved head office, new establishments) are reported through an updating declaration, and changes to the letters patent themselves — such as new objects or a name change — require supplementary letters patent ($67 regular in 2026).

Good governance rounds out compliance: hold your annual members' meeting, keep minutes, by-laws and member registers up to date, and retain financial statements. These records are exactly what grant funders, banks and the CRA will request.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need to start a non-profit in Quebec?

At least three. Part III of the Companies Act allows "any number of persons, not less than three" to apply for letters patent constituting a non-profit legal person. The organization's board must also have at least three directors, since the Act's minimum-board rule applies to Part III legal persons through section 224.

### How much does it cost to incorporate a non-profit in Quebec?

Under the 2026 fee schedule, constitution by letters patent costs $199 with regular processing or $298.50 with priority processing, and an optional 90-day name reservation costs $27. The initial declaration is free if filed within 60 days. Each year thereafter, the organization pays a $41 annual registration fee with its updating declaration.

### Does a Quebec non-profit's name have to be in French?

Yes. Under the Charter of the French Language, an enterprise's name must be in French. If your constituting documents contain no French version, you must declare a French name in the enterprise register. You may also register versions in other languages as "other names used in Québec," but the official French name is mandatory.

### What are letters patent for a Quebec non-profit?

Letters patent are the charter document the Registraire des entreprises issues to constitute a non-profit legal person under Part III of the Companies Act. They state the organization's name, objects and other founding particulars. Later changes to those particulars — new objects, a new name — are made through supplementary letters patent rather than simple amendments.

### What annual filings does a Quebec non-profit have to make?

One core filing: the annual updating declaration, due between May 15 and November 15 for legal persons, together with the annual registration fee ($41 in 2026). The declaration confirms the register's information about your organization. Missing it two years running can lead the Registraire to cancel your registration, which dissolves a non-profit legal person.

### Can a Quebec non-profit legal person become a registered charity?

Yes. Many Quebec charities are Part III legal persons. Constitution creates the entity; registered charity status is then sought from the Canada Revenue Agency, which reviews your objects and activities against charitable-law requirements. Only after registration can the organization issue official donation receipts, so draft your letters patent objects with that application in mind.
