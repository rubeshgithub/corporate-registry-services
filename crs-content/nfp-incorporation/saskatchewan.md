# How to Incorporate a Not-for-Profit in Saskatchewan (Non-profit Corporations Act, 2022 Guide)

If you are wondering how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Saskatchewan, the process now runs under [The Non-profit Corporations Act, 2022](https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/#/products/120423) — modern legislation that replaced the province's 1995 statute and came into force in March 2023. Under this Act, a not-for-profit corporation is a legal entity created to carry on activities without financial gain to its members, whether that means running a community hall, a minor sports league, a cultural association or a public charity.

Incorporation is handled by the [Corporate Registry](https://www.saskregistries.ca/corporateregistry) operated by Information Services Corporation (ISC), and filings are made through ISC's online Corporate Registry portal. This guide is written for first-time incorporators — volunteer boards, community founders and organizers who want a clear, plain-English path from a proposed name to a certificate of incorporation.

## What counts as a not-for-profit in Saskatchewan

Saskatchewan's Act draws a distinction that matters from your very first filing: every non-profit corporation is either a **membership corporation** or a **charitable corporation**, and your articles of incorporation must say which one you are.

A membership corporation carries on activities primarily for the benefit of its own members — think golf and curling clubs, chambers of commerce or professional associations — and is typically financed through membership fees, member loans and internal fundraising. A charitable corporation carries on activities primarily for the benefit of the public. Importantly, a membership corporation is *deemed* to be charitable under the Act if it carries on activities for non-members, solicits donations from the public, receives government grants exceeding 10% of its annual income, or is a registered charity under the federal Income Tax Act. In other words, taking public money can change your legal category, so choose carefully at the outset.

Both types share the core non-profit feature: no part of the organization's income may be distributed to members as profit. Assets are applied to the corporation's purposes, and on dissolution a charitable corporation's remaining property cannot go to members. Note that a provincial non-profit corporation is not automatically a **registered charity** — charitable registration is a separate federal application to the Canada Revenue Agency that unlocks the ability to issue donation tax receipts. And unlike a for-profit corporation, a non-profit has members rather than shareholders and exists to advance a purpose, not to generate returns on investment.

## Why incorporate your not-for-profit

An unincorporated club can operate informally, but incorporation creates a separate legal person. The corporation, not its individual volunteers, signs leases, opens bank accounts, holds property and enters contracts, which shields members and directors from most personal liability for the organization's obligations. Incorporation also gives your group perpetual existence: the corporation continues even as founders move on, which matters to funders assessing whether your programs will outlive this year's board.

Practically, most grant programs, municipalities and financial institutions expect a certificate of incorporation before they will fund you, insure you or open an account in your organization's name. And if charitable registration with the CRA is in your future, incorporating first — with purposes drafted to charitable standards — makes that application far smoother.

## Step-by-step: how to incorporate a not-for-profit in Saskatchewan

1. **Reserve your corporate name.** Submit a name reservation through the ISC Corporate Registry application ($35, or $60 if the name contains a restricted word such as "Canada" or "Canadian"). You will receive a reservation number needed for the incorporation filing.
2. **Decide: membership or charitable corporation.** Review the definitions above with your founding group. If you plan to fundraise publicly or seek government grants, expect to be a charitable corporation — which means a minimum of three directors, at least two of whom are not officers or employees of the corporation.
3. **Prepare your articles of incorporation.** Your articles must state the corporation's type, the nature of its activities, its director structure (a fixed number, or a minimum and maximum), and what happens to property on dissolution. Draft bylaws covering membership, meetings and governance at the same time — you will need them at your first organizational meeting.
4. **Confirm your board and registered office.** Line up your directors (18 or older, with capacity, not bankrupt) and a registered office with a physical Saskatchewan address — a P.O. box alone is not acceptable, although a separate mailing address may be added. If no director or officer lives in Saskatchewan, prepare a power of attorney appointing someone in the province.
5. **File online through the Corporate Registry.** Log in to the [ISC Corporate Registry application](https://corporateregistry.isc.ca/), choose File → Non-profit corporations → Incorporate, enter your name reservation number, complete the application with director, officer and registered office details, and pay the $50 fee.
6. **Receive your certificate of incorporation.** Once the registry approves the filing, your corporation exists. Hold an organizational meeting to adopt bylaws, appoint officers, admit members and set your financial year end.

## After incorporation: ongoing compliance

Your first recurring obligation is the **annual return**, filed through the same online Corporate Registry application. Saskatchewan pairs the return with financial disclosure: you must submit summary financial statements showing total revenue, total expenditures, total assets, total liabilities and member equity, signed by a director, together with any required auditor's report. The registry emails or mails a reminder at least a month before your due date, and the on-time fee is $20 (rising to $40 if you file late).

The annual return is tied to your **annual general meeting** cycle. You must hold your first annual meeting of members within 18 months of incorporation and subsequent meetings no more than 15 months apart, present financial statements dated within six months before the meeting, and file the annual return within 30 days after the statements are presented. Charitable corporations face graduated assurance rules — an audit is mandatory above $500,000 in revenue, while smaller organizations can pass member resolutions to substitute a review or waive assurance entirely.

Beyond the annual cycle, changes to directors, officers or the registered office must be filed within 15 days. The consequence of ignoring these obligations is severe: a corporation that misses its annual return can be struck from the register after the registry's final notice, dissolving the entity and putting its property and name at risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many board members do I need for a nonprofit in Saskatchewan?

It depends on your corporation's type. A membership corporation — one operating primarily for its members' benefit — needs only one director. A charitable corporation, which operates primarily for the public benefit, must have at least three directors, and at least two of those three cannot also be officers or employees of the corporation.

### How much does it cost to incorporate a non-profit in Saskatchewan?

Under the ISC fee table effective April 15, 2026, incorporating a not-for-profit corporation costs $50, plus $35 to reserve your name first ($60 if the name includes a restricted word such as "Canada"). Ongoing costs are modest: the annual return is $20 when filed by the due date, or $40 if filed late.

### What is the difference between a membership and a charitable corporation in Saskatchewan?

A membership corporation carries on activities primarily for its members and is financed by fees and member contributions. A charitable corporation operates primarily for the public benefit. A membership corporation is deemed charitable if it serves non-members, solicits public donations, receives government grants above 10% of annual income, or holds CRA registered-charity status.

### Do Saskatchewan non-profit directors have to live in the province?

No fixed residency quota applies under the 2022 Act — the old requirements for a Saskatchewan-resident director and 25% Canadian directors were removed. However, if no director or officer is resident in Saskatchewan, the corporation must appoint a power of attorney in the province so there is a local point of contact for service.

### Can a not-for-profit make a profit in Canada?

Yes — a not-for-profit corporation can earn revenue that exceeds its expenses, and healthy organizations often do. What it cannot do is distribute that surplus to members, directors or officers as personal gain. Surpluses must be reinvested in the organization's purposes, such as programs, staff, equipment or reserves for future years.

### Is a Saskatchewan non-profit corporation the same as a registered charity?

No. Incorporation under The Non-profit Corporations Act, 2022 creates the legal entity, while registered charity status is granted separately by the Canada Revenue Agency and allows you to issue official donation receipts. Many organizations incorporate provincially first, then apply to the CRA once their charitable purposes and activities are clearly documented.
