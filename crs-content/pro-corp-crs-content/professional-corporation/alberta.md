# Professional Corporations in Alberta: The Complete Guide

A professional corporation (PC) in Alberta lets a regulated professional — a physician, lawyer, accountant, dentist, chiropractor or optometrist — carry on their practice through a corporation instead of personally. Alberta reserves the words "Professional Corporation" as a legal element in corporate names, and the province's [incorporation rules](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-alberta-corporation) allow only a defined list of professions to use it. For most incorporating professionals, the motivation is tax planning: corporate income taxed at small business rates, flexibility on when income is drawn out, and — depending on the profession — the ability to bring family members in as non-voting shareholders. The details belong with your accountant; the filings belong with us.

Setting up an Alberta professional corporation is always a two-step product. Step one is incorporating under Alberta's Business Corporations Act through a registry agent or authorized service provider. Step two is obtaining the authorization your regulator issues — a permit from the College of Physicians & Surgeons of Alberta (CPSA) or the Law Society of Alberta, registration with CPA Alberta, and so on. Neither step works without the other, and both must be renewed every year. This guide covers who qualifies, how the process runs, and Alberta's naming rules.

## Who can form a professional corporation in Alberta

Alberta's corporate registry lists the professions entitled to use the "Professional Corporation" name element. Each one answers to its own regulator, and each regulator issues its own authorization document:

The eligible name elements published on [alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-alberta-corporation) cover Chartered Professional Accountants, chiropractic, dentistry, law, medicine and optometry. Health professions are governed by the Health Professions Act alongside their college's bylaws; lawyers are governed by the Legal Profession Act and the Rules of the Law Society of Alberta. Alberta's official list of health regulators is maintained at [alberta.ca/regulated-health-professions](https://www.alberta.ca/regulated-health-professions). If your profession is not on the list, you may still incorporate an ordinary Alberta corporation — you just cannot call it a professional corporation or practise a restricted profession through it.

## The common process

However the regulators differ in paperwork, every Alberta PC follows the same skeleton.

**The registry step.** Alberta corporations are filed through registry agents and authorized service providers rather than directly with a government portal. You will need an Alberta NUANS report for your proposed name — it must be less than 91 days old when submitted and reserves the name for 90 days — plus Articles of Incorporation (form REG3047), a Notice of Address (REG3016), a Notice of Directors (REG3017) and a Notice of Agent for Service (REG3037). The service provider charges the government fee plus its own service fee; current amounts are published in Alberta's Registry Agent Product Catalogue.

**The regulator step.** Before or after the registry filing (the sequence varies — CPSA, for example, must endorse your articles *before* you file them, while the Law Society approves articles first and issues its permit after incorporation), you apply to your regulator for its permit or registration. Regulators typically want a certified copy of your Certificate of Incorporation or proof of corporate status — a document CRS can obtain quickly through a [corporate profile report or certificate order](/corporate-profile-report) — plus shareholder and director declarations and an application fee.

Only when both steps are complete may you practise through the corporation. From then on, two renewal tracks run in parallel: the corporation's annual return at the corporate registry, and the regulator's annual permit or registration renewal. Our [renewals and compliance guide](/professional-corporation/renewals) covers both.

## Naming rules in Alberta

An Alberta corporate name has three elements: a distinctive element, a descriptive element, and a legal element. For a PC, the legal element is the words "Professional Corporation," which replace the usual Ltd., Inc. or Corp. On top of the registry's rules, each regulator layers its own requirements. The Law Society, for instance, requires the name to contain the voting shareholder's surname with one or more given names or initials, followed by "Professional Corporation"; CPSA requires physician PC names to comply with the naming rules in its bylaws. Because the NUANS report only screens for conflicts — it does not confirm regulator compliance — it pays to have the regulator's naming rules in hand before you order the report, and, where the regulator pre-approves names or articles, to secure that approval before filing.

## PC vs regular corporation

A professional corporation is a standard Alberta business corporation with restrictions bolted on. Its articles must limit the business to the practice of the profession (and what the regulator allows alongside it). Voting shares must generally be held by licensed members of the profession, and in several professions the directors must be licensed members too. The corporate shield is also narrower: incorporation never protects a professional from personal liability for professional negligence, which is why regulators require professionals to remain personally answerable and fully insured.

What a PC does *not* change is tax treatment. A professional corporation is taxed like any other Canadian-controlled private corporation — the small business deduction, income-timing flexibility and other planning tools apply on the same terms. The trade-off, in short: same tax toolkit, more rules, two annual renewals. For most established practices the arithmetic still favours incorporating, but run your numbers with your accountant first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can set up a professional corporation in Alberta?

Alberta's corporate registry permits professional corporations for Chartered Professional Accountants, chiropractors, dentists, lawyers, physicians and optometrists. Each profession's regulator — CPSA, the Law Society of Alberta, CPA Alberta and the respective health colleges — must also issue its own permit or registration before the corporation may practise. Other professions incorporate as ordinary Alberta corporations.

### How much does a professional corporation cost in Alberta?

Budget for three layers: the Alberta government filing fee plus your registry agent's service fee (see the Registry Agent Product Catalogue), the NUANS name report, and your regulator's fee — for example, CPSA charges $500 plus GST per physician shareholder to register a PC, and the Law Society of Alberta charges a $500 plus GST permit application fee.

### Do I incorporate first or get regulator approval first?

It depends on the regulator. CPSA must endorse a physician's articles of incorporation before they are filed at the corporate registry, and the Law Society of Alberta approves proposed articles before incorporation, then issues the permit once the Certificate of Incorporation exists. Always confirm your regulator's sequence before filing — undoing a non-compliant incorporation costs time and amendment fees.

### Can my spouse own shares in my Alberta professional corporation?

It depends on your profession's statute. Physicians may have spouses, common-law partners and children hold non-voting shares while voting shares stay with CPSA-registered physicians. Law PCs must vest voting shares in active Law Society members, with non-voting shares limited to prescribed classes. Confirm your regulator's rules before issuing any shares.

### Does a professional corporation protect me from being sued?

Not for professional negligence. Alberta regulators keep professionals personally liable for their professional work, and insurance obligations continue unchanged. The corporation can, however, offer the ordinary advantages of incorporation for the business side of a practice — contracts, leases, employees — along with the tax-planning benefits that drive most incorporations.

### What renewals does an Alberta PC have to file every year?

Two. The corporation must file an annual return at the Alberta Corporate Registry through a registry agent to stay in good standing, and the regulator's permit or registration must be renewed on its own cycle — December 31 for Law Society PC permits, and annually alongside the practice-permit window for CPSA physicians. Missing either can suspend your right to practise through the PC.
