# Law Professional Corporation in Alberta: Law Society Permit to Practise Guide

For an Alberta lawyer, incorporating the practice is usually a tax decision — corporate rates on retained earnings, flexibility on when income is drawn, a cleaner structure for an eventual sale of the practice. But a law professional corporation (PC) is a two-step product with the Law Society of Alberta in the middle of it. Section 137(1) of the Legal Profession Act prohibits practising under any name containing "Professional Corporation" or "P.C." unless the corporation exists under Alberta's Business Corporations Act **and** holds a Law Society permit that is not under suspension.

The sequence matters: the Law Society approves your proposed articles of incorporation before you file anything at the Corporate Registry, and issues the permit only after the corporation exists. This guide covers the [Law Society's PC process](https://www.lawsociety.ab.ca/lawyers-and-students/membership-services/professional-corporations/) end to end — articles approval, registry incorporation, the Form 8-1 permit application, the shareholder restrictions, and the December 31 renewal that keeps the permit alive.

## Step 1: Incorporate at the registry — after the Law Society approves your articles

Before any registry filing, submit the Law Society's [Approval of Articles of Incorporation form](https://documents.lawsociety.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/23175130/approval_articles_incorporation.pdf) with your proposed articles. The Law Society checks two things: that the name and share structure comply with the Legal Profession Act and the Rules, and that the articles carry the required restrictions — the Society publishes template Schedules C and D covering the business restriction and the shareholder liability provisions under section 133 of the Act. The form discloses every person who will own shares, legally or beneficially, voting or non-voting, and lists all directors; an active-member director certifies that the statement is true and complete.

The name must follow the statutory pattern: one or more given names or initials plus the surname of the voting shareholder, followed by "Professional Corporation" — "J. A. Smith Professional Corporation," for example — subject to limited exceptions under Rule 159(2). With approval in hand, incorporate through an Alberta registry agent: Alberta NUANS report (under 91 days old), the approved Articles of Incorporation, Notice of Address, Notice of Directors and Notice of Agent for Service, plus the government fee and the agent's service fee.

## Step 2: Get your permit to practise law as a professional corporation

Once the Certificate of Incorporation issues, apply to the Law Society for the permit itself using [Form 8-1, Application for a Permit](https://www.lawsociety.ab.ca/lawyers-and-students/membership-services/professional-corporations/professional-corporations-forms/). The application includes a copy of the Certificate of Incorporation — copies are accepted and the package can be submitted by email — together with the application fee of $500 + GST ($525 on the 2026 fee schedule). If you need an extra certified copy of the certificate or proof of the corporation's status for your records or your bank, CRS can pull a [corporate profile report](/corporate-profile-report) from the Alberta registry the same day.

Under section 131 of the Legal Profession Act, only active members of the Law Society of Alberta are entitled to hold a professional corporation permit. Once the permit issues, the corporation — not just the lawyer — is authorized to carry on the practice of law, and the "Professional Corporation" name may lawfully appear on your letterhead, trust accounts and retainers.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

The Legal Profession Act and Rule 155(1) draw hard lines around ownership. All voting shares must be vested in one or more active members of the Law Society; all directors must also be active members. Non-voting shares must be vested in persons within the prescribed classes — the Law Society itself will not advise on how to structure the corporation beyond its template schedules, so the choice and drafting of non-voting share classes for family or estate-planning purposes belongs with your own counsel and accountant before any shares are issued.

Two drafting points recur. Share transfer restrictions are permitted, but they must specify that only voting shareholders or directors may approve transfers — keeping control decisions inside the profession. And the articles must carry the Schedule C business restriction and Schedule D liability language, which preserves shareholders' liability for professional obligations under section 133; incorporation does not shelter a lawyer from professional negligence or from the Society's conduct jurisdiction.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

A law PC runs on two renewal calendars. The corporation files its annual return at the Alberta Corporate Registry through a registry agent — miss it long enough and the corporation is struck, which kills the permit with it. Separately, the Law Society permit renews annually by **December 31**, completed online in the Lawyer Portal under the "My PC" section. The 2026 renewal fee is $240 + GST ($252), payable by electronic bill payment (credit cards are not accepted), with processing taking three to five business days after payment; your renewed permit is posted to the portal rather than mailed.

The lapse rule has real teeth: a permit not renewed on time cannot simply be reinstated — Rule 158 requires a fresh application with the full application fee. If you wind up the PC instead, the Law Society requires written notice plus a Certificate of Dissolution or Amendment from the Corporate Registry. ## Why lawyers incorporate

The financial case is the standard professional-corporation case. Active practice income retained in the corporation is taxed at the small business rate rather than top personal rates, creating a deferral for lawyers who earn more than they draw. Income can be timed — salary and dividends set year by year — and qualifying shares of a Canadian-controlled private corporation may access the lifetime capital gains exemption on a future sale, subject to conditions. Every one of these levers depends on your facts, and the tax-on-split-income rules constrain family dividends; confirm the numbers with your accountant before committing to the structure.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a law professional corporation cost in Alberta?

The Law Society's 2026 fees are $500 + GST ($525) for the permit application and $240 + GST ($252) for annual renewal. Add the Alberta Corporate Registry's government fee and your registry agent's service fee, a NUANS report, and legal or accounting advice on the share structure. Budget for both annual renewals thereafter.

### Do I need Law Society approval before incorporating?

Yes. Submit the Approval of Articles of Incorporation form with your proposed articles — including the Society's Schedule C and D language — before filing at the Corporate Registry. Only after the Law Society approves do you incorporate, and only after incorporation do you apply for the permit with Form 8-1 and the Certificate of Incorporation.

### Can my spouse own shares in my law professional corporation in Alberta?

Not voting shares. All voting shares must be vested in active members of the Law Society of Alberta, and all directors must be active members. Non-voting shares are limited to prescribed classes of persons under the Act and Rules, so have counsel confirm any family shareholding before shares are issued.

### What is the deadline to renew a law PC permit in Alberta?

December 31 each year. Renewal is completed in the Lawyer Portal under "My PC" and paid by electronic bill payment, with three to five business days' processing. Miss the deadline and the permit cannot simply be revived — the Rules require a new application with the full application fee.

### What name can I give my law corporation in Alberta?

The name must contain one or more given names or initials plus the surname of the voting shareholder, followed by "Professional Corporation" — for example, "M. J. Chen Professional Corporation." Limited exceptions exist under Rule 159(2). Section 137(1) of the Legal Profession Act makes it an offence to practise under a PC name without a valid permit.

### Does incorporating protect a lawyer from liability?

Not professionally. The mandatory Schedule D provisions preserve shareholder liability for professional obligations under section 133 of the Legal Profession Act, and the lawyer remains personally subject to the Law Society's conduct and insurance requirements. The corporation's value lies in tax planning and business structuring, not in shielding professional negligence.
