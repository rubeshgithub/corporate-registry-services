# CPSA Professional Corporation Permit: Incorporating Your Alberta Medical Practice

Most Alberta physicians who incorporate do it for the tax planning: fee-for-service income earned through a corporation is taxed at corporate rates, income can be timed to suit the household, and family members can hold non-voting shares. But a CPSA professional corporation is a two-step product, and the steps come in an order that surprises many first-time incorporators. You cannot simply incorporate at the registry and tell the College afterwards — the College of Physicians & Surgeons of Alberta (CPSA) must endorse your articles of incorporation *before* they are filed.

CPSA registers professional corporations under section 108 of the Health Professions Act and governs them through the CPSA Bylaws and its Professional Corporations Guidelines (updated May 2025). Alberta's Business Corporations Act governs the corporate side, filed through a registry agent. This guide walks through both steps — the endorsed incorporation and the [CPSA permit process](https://cpsa.ca/physicians/registration/update-or-renew-practice/incorporate-practice/) — plus the shareholder rules and the two renewal tracks that keep the structure alive.

## Step 1: Incorporate at the registry — after CPSA endorses your articles

Start with the paperwork CPSA has to see first. Review the naming requirements in the CPSA Bylaws, the Health Professions Act and the Business Corporations Act — your corporate name must carry the "Professional Corporation" legal element and satisfy the College's naming rules. CPSA recommends consulting a lawyer or accountant at this stage, and the sequencing is where professional help earns its keep.

Prepare Articles of Incorporation (or Articles of Continuance if you are moving an existing corporation into PC form), signed by the incorporating physician — digital signatures are accepted. Submit them to CPSA using its Articles Submission Form; the College endorses compliant articles within 15 business days. Only then do you take the endorsed articles to an Alberta registry agent, along with an Alberta NUANS report (less than 91 days old), the Notice of Address, Notice of Directors and Notice of Agent for Service. The agent charges the government fee plus its service fee and issues your Certificate of Incorporation.

## Step 2: Get your CPSA professional corporation permit

With the corporation in existence, complete CPSA's [Professional Corporation Application Form](https://cpsa.ca/physicians/registration/update-or-renew-practice/incorporate-practice/professional-corporation-application-form/). It registers the PC under section 108 of the Health Professions Act and asks for the PC's name and Alberta registered office, every voting shareholder (all CPSA-registered), every non-voting shareholder, and the CPSA-registered directors. You must also confirm a series of statutory declarations — among them that no voting shares are subject to trusts or agreements that would violate section 111 of the Act, and that the corporation is in good standing with the Registrar of Corporations.

The form must be accompanied by a copy of the PC's Certificate of Incorporation **certified correct by the Registrar of Corporations** — not the plain certificate the registry agent hands you. CRS obtains certified certificates and [corporate profile reports](/corporate-profile-report) from the Alberta registry routinely, which removes the most common cause of bounced applications. With payment received ($500 + GST per physician shareholder), CPSA opens the PC within 15 business days and emails Form F — the document confirming your professional corporation — to your designated contact.

Later changes run through the [Professional Corporation Information Form (PCIF)](https://cpsa.ca/physicians/registration/update-or-renew-practice/incorporate-practice/professional-corporation-information-form/): use it to update addresses, add shareholders or refresh director information. Name or structure changes to the articles themselves require an Articles of Amendment Form — amendments and amalgamations carry no CPSA fee.

## Shareholders, directors and restrictions

CPSA's rules on ownership are short and strict. [Voting shares](https://cpsa.ca/faqs/who-can-be-a-shareholder-in-my-pc/) may be held only by physicians with active CPSA registration. Non-voting shares may be held by the spouses, common-law partners and children of those physicians — this is the family income-planning channel that makes medical PCs attractive, though how useful it is after the federal tax-on-split-income rules is a question for your accountant. Directors listed with CPSA must themselves be registered members, and every physician practising through the PC must be a CPSA regulated member.

Two structural cautions. First, voting shares cannot be parked in trusts or voting agreements that hand control to non-physicians — the application makes you declare compliance with section 111 of the Health Professions Act. Second, the CPSA Professional Corporations Guidelines defer detailed shareholder mechanics to the Act itself, so anything creative — holding structures, trusts for minor children — needs a lawyer's sign-off before shares are issued, not after.

## Keeping it alive: annual renewals

An Alberta medical PC has two renewal tracks, and they do not talk to each other. At the corporate registry, the corporation must file its annual return through a registry agent to stay in good standing — fall out of good standing and you jeopardize a condition of your CPSA permit. At the College, the PC permit renews annually at $200 + GST per physician shareholder; renewal opens at the same time as your practice permit renewal (CPSA's 2026 renewal window ran to December 31, 2025) but is a separate process — renewing your practice permit does not renew your PC. In multi-shareholder PCs, only the designated physician sees the renewal, though every shareholder receives the final permit and receipt.

Miss the deadline and CPSA charges a $200 late penalty per PC; letting the registration lapse entirely means a $200 + GST revival fee and time practising outside your corporate structure. ## Why physicians incorporate

The corporate tax rate on active income eligible for the small business deduction sits well below top personal marginal rates, so income left inside the PC compounds pre-tax — a deferral that rewards physicians earning more than they spend. A corporation also allows income timing (salary and dividends drawn in the years that suit you) and, through non-voting shares, potential family participation. None of this is automatic: the tax-on-split-income rules, passive investment limits and your own cash needs all move the answer. Treat this section as a map of the terrain and confirm the route with your accountant.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need CPSA approval before incorporating my medical practice?

Yes. CPSA must endorse your Articles of Incorporation before you file them at the Alberta Corporate Registry. Submit the articles through CPSA's Articles Submission Form; endorsement takes up to 15 business days. Filing unendorsed articles is the most common sequencing mistake physicians make, and fixing it means amending the articles and refiling.

### How much does a CPSA professional corporation cost?

CPSA charges $500 + GST per physician shareholder to register a professional corporation and $200 + GST per physician shareholder for each annual renewal (2025–2026 fee schedule). On top of that, budget for the Alberta registry's government and agent fees, a NUANS report, and professional advice on structuring the share classes.

### Who can own shares in an Alberta medical professional corporation?

Voting shares may be held only by physicians with active CPSA registration. Non-voting shares may be held by those physicians' spouses, common-law partners and children. Voting shares cannot be subject to trusts or agreements that shift control to non-physicians, per section 111 of the Health Professions Act.

### Can my spouse own shares in my medical corporation in Alberta?

Yes — as a non-voting shareholder. CPSA permits spouses and common-law partners of actively registered physicians to hold non-voting shares, alongside children. Whether dividends to family shareholders survive the federal tax-on-split-income rules is a separate question; confirm the plan with your accountant before shares are issued.

### What is the CPSA PCIF form?

The Professional Corporation Information Form (PCIF) updates an existing PC's file with CPSA: new shareholders, changed addresses, updated directors. A designated physician director signs it electronically and confirms the statutory declarations. It is filed as needed rather than on a fixed schedule; changes to the corporate name or structure need an Articles of Amendment instead.

### When does my medical PC permit renew?

Annually. PC renewal opens at the same time as CPSA's practice permit renewal — the 2026 window closed December 31, 2025 — but it is a separate transaction, so renew both. The renewal fee is $200 + GST per physician shareholder; late renewal costs an extra $200 per PC, and a lapsed PC pays a revival fee.
