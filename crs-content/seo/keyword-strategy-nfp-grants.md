# SEO Keyword Strategy — Cluster 2: Not-for-Profit Grants (Canada)

**Date:** 2026-07-17 · **Site:** bizfile.ca · **Geography:** Canada (EN) · **Goal:** top-of-funnel traffic → incorporation cluster → free-consultation bookings

Methodology note: as with cluster 1, difficulty/volume are expert SERP-based assessments, not tool exports. Grants queries skew higher-volume and more seasonal than incorporation queries — demand spikes around program intake windows (OTF deadlines, Community Gaming Grant sector windows, Canada Summer Jobs each winter).

## Cluster architecture & funnel logic

Pillar: `/nfp-grants/` targeting **"grants for nonprofits in Canada"** (High volume, High difficulty — competing with grant databases and government portals). The 14 jurisdiction pages win on the province-level modifier where national databases are thin and government pages list programs without guidance. Strategic role: grants content attracts founders *earlier* in their journey than incorporation content; every grants page routes readers who aren't yet incorporated into cluster 1 and the consultation CTA ("funders fund organizations, not ideas").

## High-value secondary & long-tail keywords

- Program-name queries (the traffic engine of this cluster) — "Ontario Trillium Foundation grants", "Community Gaming Grants BC", "CFEP Alberta", "Community Initiatives Fund Saskatchewan", "New Horizons for Seniors Program", "Canada Summer Jobs application". People search programs by name; each page covers its flagship programs with amounts + deadlines, positioning us for "program name + how to apply/deadline/amount" long tails.
- Year-modified queries — "nonprofit grants 2026", "{program} 2026 deadline/intake". The `2026 Funding Guide` H1 pattern and dated recent-award sections capture these; refresh titles annually.
- Eligibility queries — "who qualifies for nonprofit grants", "do you need to be a charity to get grants", "grants for new nonprofits". Answered in FAQ snippets — these searchers are pre-incorporation and are the cluster's best consultation leads.
- Category queries (future expansion posts) — "arts grants {province}", "seniors grants Canada", "youth program funding", "community facility grants", "startup funding for nonprofits Canada".
- "Recently awarded" queries — "who got OTF grants 2026", "{program} recipients". The recent-awards sections with dated announcements serve these and earn freshness signals.

## Quick wins

1. Territories + Atlantic pages — no commercial competitor aggregates YT/NWT/NU or PEI/NL grant programs with amounts and deadlines; expect fast rankings on all long tails.
2. Program-name + guidance long tails in mid-size provinces (SK CIF, MB Building Sustainable Communities, NS Support4Culture) where only the government page ranks.
3. Seasonal refresh calendar: update each page ~4 weeks before its flagship intake (e.g., OTF Seed in July, Grow in Oct; Gaming Grants by sector window; CSJ in Dec) — timed freshness beats static competitors.

## SERP-feature strategy

Program tables (Program | Funder | Amount | Deadline | Link) are built for AI Overview and featured-snippet extraction. FAQ H3s target PAA ("What grants are available for nonprofits in {province}?", "Can a new nonprofit get grants?"). Dated recent-award items give the pages news-adjacent freshness that grant databases lack. Add FAQPage + BreadcrumbList schema; consider Dataset/ItemList schema on program tables.

## Internal linking & conversion

Every grants page links: grants pillar ↔ jurisdiction incorporation page ↔ federal grants page ↔ consultation. The CTA reframes grants urgency into incorporation action ("before the next intake deadline"). Track grants-page → incorporation-page → consultation as the primary conversion path in analytics.

## Measurement

GSC queries by page; alert on intake-window spikes. Quarterly re-verification of amounts/deadlines is mandatory — a stale deadline is worse than no deadline for both users and rankings.
